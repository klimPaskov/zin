# Roots of the Magical Lands

**Adopted (owner, 31 October 2026; decision K68):** every proposal in this note is established canon; the "Proposed" labels below record how each entry began.

**Status of this note:** proposed (decision K67, 25 October 2026). Everything below is a **Proposed** creative addition under the [[Canon Policy]] unless it is marked **Established**, and nothing here answers an item of [[Open Questions]]. The play hooks are in `docs/specs/02_lore/realm_roots/magical_lands.md` and the data in `tools/content/data/world/history/magical_lands.json`.

**Established background:** the Magical Lands were never conquered, not by [[Volgan]]'s corrupted kin and not by [[Elzon]]; the elves helped the Grand Alliance at [[Battle of Dardan|Dardan]] and then kept [[The Elven Refusal]]; dragons come chiefly from Dragon Mountain; the mycids live in the Mushroom Forest; the rift-coast elves were neighbours of [[Tarkia, the Bridge Kingdom|the Bridge Kingdom]], never its subjects, and Luv-zhise ferries over its drowned road (K64).

The roots of these realms are not stories of empire. Each is the one deed, gift, loss or oath from which a small community's law grew, and each realm's sign is the object or creature at the centre of that deed.

## The Magical Forest: the First Listening

**Proposed.** Long before Aen the houses that became the council came into the Living Forest from many directions, and their songs disagree about whence. They feuded over glades and springs until they met under the oldest Singing Grove meaning to fight, and, by their own telling, stayed to listen because the grove was singing. There they made the first precedent: a house speaks only while it holds a bough that the grove has let fall, and nobody breaks a bough from a living tree to get a turn.

The vote, the book of precedents and the rule of fallen wood only all come from that night, and the Speaking Bough is kept in the council-hall at Lyrion. The rule was broken once, when the houses felled the northern wood against the [[Black Roots]] in 3A 802; each house carried a bough of its felled trees to Lyrion, and the heap behind the hall is the hardest page of the book.

**Sign (proposed): the Speaking Bough**, a fallen bough laid level, mist grey on deep wood green. Sirael carried a bough of the grove at Dardan instead of a standard. It is never shown upright or held, because no house owns it.

## Herrodon: the White Winter

**Proposed.** Long before Aen the hill elves lived on both faces of the range and had houses but no hall. In the White Winter the snow came off the Herrodon rim in one night and buried the summer camps. A herd-keeper, remembered only as the First Queen, followed a white owl down to a hollow under the crags, kept a fire there, and brought most of the houses in by spring. She was acclaimed in the hollow that became Verozo, and the houses swore that no one of the hills would ever again be left out in the snow.

The queenship by acclamation, the summons home of 2A 3, the seat kept empty for the lost household that became Dunelaeth, and the protection of the men of Teste all come from that oath. No hill elf harms an owl.

**Sign (proposed): the Hall-Owl**, a white owl with raised wings on night slate, first carried as a banner of search.

## Andos Enda: the court afloat

**Proposed.** Long before Aen the lake elves were boat-folk who took the waters' names and moved them with the water, and they sheltered every stranger for one night and sent him on at dawn. In the Year of the Broken Weirs, 1A 476, a drought set the groves breaking one another's weirs, and the children of the grove of Ilawen drowned in a loosed race. Enlire of that grove called the groves onto the largest lake and held the first Water-Court in her own boat on open water, so that no grove owned the court's ground; the Weir Compact of 1A 480 was sworn there.

**Sign (proposed): the Court-Boat**, an empty lake-boat with a tall curled prow, water silver on lake teal. The boat at Enliras has been rebuilt plank by plank and is the same boat.

## Nolorim: the Cracked Horn

**Proposed.** Before the oath the families of the southern crags were herders of the heights who traded with the men of Mirolon's shore. **Established:** Volgan invaded Mirolon in the First Age. **Proposed:** on that night in 1A 1150 their eldest herder, Nolanwe, blew the great horn of the heights until its bell split, and the warning came too late. At dawn the families swore, each to one post, that nothing of Volgan's would come up through the heights and that the warning would never come late again.

**Sign (proposed): the Cracked Horn**, a war-horn with a split bell, dawn silver on pass grey-green. It hangs at Ennaton and every Pass-Warden swears on it.

## Lccimio: the Seed Pledge

**Proposed.** Long before Aen the shore-houses lived beside eastern families they could reach on foot, and an elven house that kept such a friendship took a guest-name from the family and received orchard seed in return. When the neck broke, the houses sealed the seed in jars as a pledge that the friendship would be taken up again. The foreign sounds in Lccimio's names are those guest-names, the quay-book of 2A 300 comes from the same care, and the jars were opened for the orchards of 4A 610.

**Sign (proposed): the Pledge-Tree**, a fruit tree in white blossom, quay silver on rift teal, never shown in fruit because the pledge is not yet kept.

## Eldafaen: the Broken Spears

**Established (K64):** Eldafaen keeps the treaty of passage at the end of the old roads. **Proposed:** the house was first a house of witnesses who saw who came along the road. After the Ember Lands the companies laid the spears of their fallen at the road's end, and the witnesses broke each spear across the treaty-stone and wrote each name before they let the living pass; that was the Archive's first book, in 2A 9. Hence the Archive's rule that no elf should march before the names of the last war are read.

**Sign (proposed): the Broken Spears**, a spear broken in two with the halves laid apart, quay silver on night sea. The unfinished book of 3A 1180 lies in the same hall, and the loremasters say only that both are records of something that stopped.

## Yavieth: the First Scale

**Proposed.** Long before Aen elves fleeing a hard winter sheltered under the three spurs of Dragon Mountain, and a dragon came among them, spared them and left a shed scale by their fire. Their eldest climbed to give it back, and the dragon would not take it; the bond began with that refused gift. The keepers take only shed scale, never a dragon's blood, keep no lists of names, and give the word without which no realm of the Kindred asks a dragon for help.

**Sign (proposed): the First Scale**, a shed scale above the three spurs, keeper silver on slope green. The dragon itself is never shown, because a dragon is no one's sign.

## Teste: the Swallows at Dusk

**Proposed.** The families who fled Elzon's Order over the Kingson Pass in 3A 150 carried seed-grain sewn into their hoods. The lake elves sent them on at dawn, and at dusk Halvar saw swallows dropping into a valley under the Herrodon rim and led them down into the empty Teste Vale. The founding-day gate, shut at dawn and opened at dusk, keeps that day.

**Sign (proposed): the Dusk Swallow**, a diving swallow, straw on vale green.

## Luv-zhise: the Western Gate

**Established (K64):** Luv-zhise keeps the crossing over the drowned road, and the halves of the bridge-city lie under the fungal belt and the Delbiz grass. **Proposed:** the quay-folk's songs make them the people of that city, who stayed on the water between its halves and have ferried travellers ever since, steering by the tops of drowned pillars. The Quay-Master is sworn in under the half arch of the Western Gate and crosses once a year without a fare, after Blosian's crossing in the storm of 4A 140.

**Sign (proposed): the Western Gate**, a half arch broken at its crown, quay ochre on narrows teal; the other half stands on the far shore.

## The Mushroom Forest: the Bale-Mark

**Proposed, written only as others see it.** The rift-coast elves' rolls say the fungal belt already stood when the neck broke and grew over the drowned city's western half, and that when the Black Roots reached the rift coast in 3A 820 the fungi grew over them and the dead roots rotted there. Since 4A 388 every bale the mycids bring to Luv-zhise bears a stamped mark of a pale cap rising from a black severed root. **Open:** how the mycids rule, what they call themselves, whether they stopped the Black Roots, and what the mark means to them.

**Sign (proposed): the Bale-Mark**, cap cream and loam dark on spore purple.

## The Worshipdom: the Hermit's Loaf

**Proposed, public story only.** Long before Aen an old road of the East passed through the Zilin Hills toward the South Pass, and its wells and hostels lie under the herb-meadows. A hermit of the Deliverer came in 4A 1520 because the old wells still ran, and in the famine winter of 4A 1523 he gave bread from the hermitage's one oven to every traveller before he asked the stranger's name. The valleys' first rule, that a pilgrim house feeds and doctors a guest before it asks who he is, comes from that winter.

**Sign (proposed): the Hermit's Loaf**, a hand offering a round loaf, bread brown on hostel white. Nothing in this note touches the Worshipdom's hidden layer.
