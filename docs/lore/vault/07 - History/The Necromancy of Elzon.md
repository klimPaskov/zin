# The Necromancy of Elzon

**Established:** [[Elzon]] is a **Great Spirit** and the **Great Necromancer**. During the Second Age he summons or reanimates the dead on a huge scale, including fallen fighters of Aenor. This increases his own forces as his enemies lose men. He also borrows ancient magic associated with [[Zol]], though how that magic was obtained is unknown.

## Proposed battlefield rules

To prevent necromancy from becoming an automatic victory in every battle, the following are candidates for later adoption:

- A corpse must be reachable, sufficiently intact, or otherwise bound through a necromantic object, name, or place.
- Elzon can raise huge numbers, but lesser necromancers depend on sources of power, prepared marks, and dedicated ritual sites. This gives defenders infrastructure to attack.
- Ordinary undead and powerful named revenants differ in how much will, memory, and sorcery they retain.
- A society might guard graves, retrieve its dead, destroy sustaining shrines, or learn counter-rites. No single defense should be assumed universal.
- Reanimation of a body is **not automatically command of its true soul**. Whether souls are imprisoned at all, or merely memories and bodies are used, remains a major theological question.

## A dangerous distinction

Zol, as God of Death and the only [[Old Gods|Old God]] in Erdae, possesses a vastly greater kind of divine authority. His [[The Fading|Fading]] and the unusual magic of Erdae limit what he can manifest directly. Elzon's immense applied necromancy does not give him Zol's rank or a right to rule death in the cosmic sense.

## Third Age limitations and permanent death

Established limits: undead raised by Elzon fade with time, and he loses power when he stays in Aenor rather than the magically sustaining Dead Lands. He cannot fully conquer the Magical Lands, where [[Varner]], a Lesser Spirit, can lift the curse of the dead. Men refuse to submit entirely. Elzon desires to convert Aenor into another region that naturally sustains his magic.

The proposed [[Black Roots]] and the [[The Last Tithe|Last Tithe]] explain his failure and **literal death** when he draws too deeply on old magic associated with [[Zol]]. These mechanics are proposed, while his eventual permanent death and broad limits are established.
