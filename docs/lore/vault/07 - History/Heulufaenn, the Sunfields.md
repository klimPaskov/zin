# Heulufaenn, the Sunfields

**Established (owner, 25 October 2026; decision K64):** Heulufaenn was the realm of the proto-Boreal men of the north-west, the Sunfields, which lay in the southern and western Cold Lands when the ice was far to the north. It fell to lengthening winters over about a thousand years, and nobody worked the fall. Its people became the Gunwyn clans, the Tritonic islanders and the Coldic ice clans, and all three Boreal peoples descend from it.

**Status of this note:** owner-adopted on 25 October 2026 (decision K64). The **Belief** items are not presented as fact, and the names in quotation marks are working labels. The details are in the design addendum `docs/plans/deep_history/k62_fallen_realms.md`, and the play-facing summary is `docs/specs/02_lore/10_fallen_realms.md`.

## Names

Three peoples keep three names for one lost land without knowing it.

- **Heulufaenn**, the reconstructed proto-Boreal name, *heulu* "sun" and *faenn* "field", the sun-fields, which scholars who compare the Boreal tongues use.
- **Heulfaenn**, the Coldic form, with its final vowel lost by Coldic's sound law. The ice clans and Coldor's fire-keepers use it.
- **Faenn-Cheulu**, the Gunwyn form, with the quality after the noun, as Gunwyn builds names. The West Lands clans use it.
- **Heulofaennos**, the Tritonic form, with the island ending *-os*. The chart-islanders use it.
- **Tawrhalwe**, from proto-Boreal *tawr*, "king", and *halwe*, "hall": the king's hall, its seat. The ice clans' oldest songs keep it.
- **The Sunfields**, in Common Speech.

## The people

The Heulufaenn people were the proto-Boreal men of the north-west, ancestors of the Coldic, Gunwyn and Tritonic peoples. This descent is adopted (K64, S3). The Boreal family of tongues is the one the language data already records.

## Age and extent

The realm's height lay about 3,500 to 2,500 BA, and its slow decline ran over about a thousand years after that.

At its height it covered the southern and western Cold Lands when the ice lay far to the north. That is the Fandan valley, the interior that is now the Frozen Lands of Coldor, and the west coast below the Icy Mountains. It also held the neck and the Neridor Gap, the northern West Lands, and the nearer isles of the arcs.

## What made it great

It was the first sea-craft of Aoaz. The islanders' words for reef, strait, cape and tide are older than any chart, and the Sunfields' sailors are the first to have used them. The realm had bronze, great timber halls and horse-herds in the Fandan meadows. Its first kings were the kings of the north-west, which is why *tawr*, "king", is the word that all three Boreal tongues still share.

## How it fell

No one worked this fall. The winters lengthened over many lifetimes, and the ice came a little farther south in every age. The fields failed, the halls emptied, and the realm broke into clans.

Its people went three ways. Some went south over the neck and became the Gunwyn clans of the West Lands. Some went west by sea to the arcs and became the Tritonic islanders, who would one day swear [[Margorodai, the Sea-Roads|Margorodai]]. The clans who stayed and learned to live through the Long Dark became the Coldic ice clans.

**Belief.** Coldor's fire-keepers say the land was punished. The ice giants of Nivaros say that the snow breathes in and out over the ages. Neither is established. The Winter Hearth's relighting of every house-fire from the clan fire is said to be the custom of the last hall, and the fire-keepers of the Frost-Hall call the hearth older than any crown.

## What survives

- **The shared Boreal words**, *tawr* among them.
- **The Winter Hearth rite**, which relights the house-fires from the clan fire.
- **The Neck Road** over the Neridor Gap, which the clans already knew when they showed it to Aenor's surveyors about 1A 400, read here as the Sunfields' road.
- **Timber halls and bronze ship-prows** that the ice gives back in mild summers in the Frozen Lands.
- **The snowline fair at Fentene**, read as the Sunfields' old border-market.
- **A belief of the people of Mp'E.** They believe that the Watcher in the Hall is a sleeping king of the old north. This is a belief only (adopted, S4). Horos stays what the vault says he is, and who built the castle stays open.
