# Roots of the Dark Lands

**Adopted (owner, 31 October 2026; decision K68):** every proposal in this note is established canon; the "Proposed" labels below record how each entry began.

**Status of this note:** proposed (decision K67, 25 October 2026). Everything below is a **Proposed** creative addition under the [[Canon Policy]] unless it is marked **Established**, and nothing here answers an item of [[Open Questions]]. The play hooks are in `docs/specs/02_lore/realm_roots/dark_lands.md` and the data in `tools/content/data/world/history/dark_lands.json`.

**Established background:** the Dark Lands were never one nation; men, orcs, goblins, trolls, ogres, giants and demons live there under separate rulers, and none is [[Volgan]]'s by nature. Volgan opened contacts with Dark Lands powers in the First Age and orcs and goblins marched in his armies, while parts of the Dark Lands joined the [[Grand Alliance]]; Dark Lands soldiers fought on both sides at [[Battle of Dardan|Dardan]]. Many orcs and goblins kept fighting in Volgan's name in the Second Age. [[Elzon]] relied on orc and goblin forces, and the Buried Wars of 3A 431 to 690 eroded his manpower. Smash rules the trolls of his own forest only. Devils are corrupted Lesser Spirits (owner, 24 October 2026); who they served stays unsaid here. The giants of Hoorunoron are the Forgotten Giants of the low hills of [[Muzerar, the Low Country|the Low Country]] (K64), and the giants of the Giant Forest are a different people (K66).

The roots of these realms are the stories of peoples who were used by masters, left behind by them, or refused them. Each realm's law grew from the way it met a master, and each sign is the thing at the centre of that meeting.

## Goldoroth: the Long Walk

**Proposed.** The Volcano Clans began in the iron hills. Their songs turn on Dardan, where the orcs of four great clan-lines held the forward lines of Volgan's host and were left on the field when the battle was lost. The few who lived walked home through hostile country, and the Fire-Speakers chant the Long Walk at every Ember-Night. From it came the ring, in which a chief must win his place with his own hands, and the saying that no spirit rules the Dark. The Fire-Mountain became holy later, as the one master that never left them.

**Sign (proposed): the Crown of Broken Blades**, the beaten chiefs' blades hammered into Skarag's crown at Sungodor in 4A 1452, with a blade added by every Warking.

## Dondor: the garrison that was never relieved

**Proposed.** Aen's heirs kept Ainic soldiers on the Dondor plain and paid them in iron bars. When the ships stopped about 1A 352 the last pay-chest was divided among the hearths, one bar each, and the captains swore to hold the plain as if relief were coming; that is the Hearth-Iron. Dromand of the Barand was given the last captain's helm instead of a crown. Rovek of Dondor (a proposed figure of the First Age roster) raised that helm on a spear at Dardan under Edran's Charter of Free Banners, and Brunmar of the Urdung took Dondor's spears to the Burning Gate of Nezer in 2A 1388.

**Sign (proposed): the Garrison Helm**, an open-faced ridge-crested helm on blood-red.

## Dark Gate: the chain

**Proposed.** The pass is the throat of the Goldoroth arm, the road of Volgan's envoys and of the orcs who went south and came back from Dardan. In the Buried Wars free orcs broke the Necromancer's carts on a smith's chain stretched across the narrows at night, and Zugash hung a three-link chain there in 3A 880. Nothing passes unpaid and uncounted; since 4A 1470 the chain that made the gate free has been the yoke it pays Cocanoor.

**Sign (proposed): the Gate-Chain**, three black links in an arc on dull gold.

## Burnikit: the Unpaid and the Fire at the Door

**Proposed.** In 2A 2110 the warrens opened their deep ways to the Necromancer for a price that never came, and the goblins taken as guides and levies never returned; a notch on the Unpaid Stick marks each one. In 3A 1004 Snikkit, the first Big Snik, lit sulphur fires in every deep way's mouth and told the recruiters the warrens were burning with sickness. From the grief comes payment first, and from the trick the Big Sniks and the powder of the yellow crusts.

**Sign (proposed): the Fire at the Door**, a tunnel-arch with a fire in its mouth on rust-red.

## Veshazel: the stolen name

**Proposed.** The hall's telling is that its lord was of a house of the [[Nether]]'s court that lost the right to open portals in the Third Age, and that he carried the house's pact-name out of the court rather than serve a rival house. He came north to the Ash Fields in 4A 1180, raked the bounds of his lordship with three claw-scores and raised a hall of pressed ash. The hall swears to no master, marks its thralls with the scores and fears the court it left. The elves' belief that demons are corrupted angels is theirs; the thralls know only that their masters came out of the south. No pentagram belongs to this hall.

**Sign (proposed): the Three Scores**, three red claw-scores on ash-grey.

## Blackton: the empty seat

**Proposed.** The beaches below the Iron Road launched raids across the Northern Dark Strait in the Raiding Years, and Leren fleets burned them. The Leren who came to trade in 4A 1071 married the raiders' grandchildren, and the league of 4A 1102 set up a high seat in the moot-hall that nobody may sit in, so that neither people could rule the other.

**Sign (proposed): the Empty Seat**, white on black.

## Belonton: the rowers

**Proposed.** The sea orcs were rowers chained to the benches of the galleys of Volgan's eastern and southern clients. Stranded after Dardan, they broke their shackles and took the isles and the coast with the masters' ships. They kept the Salt Tithe, the Sea-Chief chosen by voyages won and the oath never to row for a master again. The Dark Sea Accord holds because both its peoples were left behind by masters.

**Sign (proposed): the Broken Shackle**, salt-white on sea-green.

## Bordon: the horn and the Road-Peace

**Proposed.** In 3A 700 Kargesk took the gorge of the Black Road by blowing a great horn from the cliff at the garrison's change of watch. The horn now opens the Road-Peace: from its note until a caravan leaves the gorge, no blade may be drawn on it.

**Sign (proposed): Kargesk's Horn**, a banded black war-horn on dust-ochre.

## Dreson: the three piles

**Proposed.** In 3A 520 a column of orcs taken in chains for the Necromancer's garrisons broke free and fled into the fens, where three eel-fishers among them knew the paths and drove the first three piles at Dolido. The reed-council are their heirs, the paths are taught only by walking them, and no chains enter the fens.

**Sign (proposed): the Three Piles**, black over fen-green on reed-yellow.

## Dulundur: the four fires

**Proposed.** Four valleys kept four fire-rites and despised each other's until the Necromancer's last levy came for all four. In 3A 1150 the chiefs lit their fires on one hearthstone at Lesderle and swore they would burn together or not at all; the Free Moot of 4A 1488 offered the free holds the same bargain.

**Sign (proposed): the Four Fires**, four flames on one hearthstone.

## The Troll Forest: the King-Stones

**Proposed.** A troll king too great and too old walks out of Holos at the end of a night and faces the dawn, and the sun makes him a stone that keeps watch at the forest's edge. The bounds are where the kings stand, and silver, cold like stone, belongs to them, which is the root of the troll silver. Smash is already turning.

**Sign (proposed): the King-Stone**, a hunched troll-shaped stone on forest-green.

## Hoorunoron: the dike rebuilt

**Established (K64, K66):** the Forgotten Giants are the giants of the low hills of the drowned Low Country, who withdrew north-east behind a cairn-line and keep the oldest stones of the count; the giants of the Giant Forest are another people. **Proposed:** the cairns of the Borderline are built from the stones of the Low Country's broken sea-dikes, carried north-east after the Night of the Sea, so the line is a dike rebuilt on dry land, and that is why it may not be crossed. The giants still say only that the land was asked for too much.

**Sign (proposed): the Dike-Cairn**, a cairn standing on water on moss-green.

## The Darks: the Meat Moot

**Proposed.** The forest orcs say they came home from Dardan to full hills; the ogres say they walked from the sunrise long before Aen (belief). In the hard winter of 4A 1222 Grimduth walked into the ogres' caves with meat on a spear turned point-down, and the moot of both peoples was made.

**Sign (proposed): the Grounded Spear**, point-down on blood-red.

## Skragdurg: the Old Black

**Proposed.** The felling camps feared the forest's revenge on whoever cut the Old Black, the tree whose shade kept the sun off them. Grashdug felled it in 4A 1120 and left his axe in the stump, and the forest-hold was built around it.

**Sign (proposed): Grashdug's Axe**, bitten into a black stump on charcoal-red.

## Dun: the crooked ancestor

**Proposed.** The Zhaggul boast of a forefather who sold each side of Volgan's war the other's crossing and was paid by neither. The toll-king's sail of two halves is dyed in the colours of paying neighbours and turned when the payment changes.

**Sign (proposed): the Turned Sail**, half red and half white on sea-grey.

## Britadon: the Hollow

**Established:** the Damned Spirits rose from beneath the mountains in Volgan's war. **Proposed:** something breathed out of the deepest pit of the south-east hills in that war, which the hill-orcs will not describe; the black boars fled first and the orcs who followed them lived. No shaft goes deeper than the Brakgul measure, and nobody hunts the black boar.

**Sign (proposed): the Running Boar**, black on iron-red.

## The Wild Lands: the rag-pole

**Proposed.** The free holds' last host of the Buried Wars was broken on the moor about 3A 690, and no hold would settle the field of the dead. The moor became the refuge of outcasts, and the winter rule of 4A 1500 gives it to whoever stays through a winter; every band that stays ties a strip of its old clan-cloth to the winter-holder's pole.

**Sign (proposed): the Rag-Pole**, three streaming strips on moor-brown.
