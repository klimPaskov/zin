# Second Age Progress and Recovery

The [[The Second Age of Erdae|Second Age]] lasted **2,133 years**, making it longer than the First Age's 1,675 years. It is remembered as the Dark Age and Age of Long Grief because repeated wars, famine, diseases, raids, and corruption prevented long, uninterrupted recovery after Dardan. Yet it was **not entirely a period of suffering**.

**Established achievements:** Aenor and other regions made substantial progress in **warfare and military technology, disease treatment and cures, farming and famine prevention**. These discoveries and methods improved the survival of later generations and contributed to Aenor's ability to recover in the Fourth Age.

## Proposed examples to develop later

- **Farming:** rotation of crops, drought-resistant plants, irrigation canals, maintained seed reserves, improved storage structures, and communal harvest rules to reduce the risk of hunger after a bad season.
- **Medicine:** specialist healers, cleaner water supply, isolation of certain contagious illnesses, remedies developed from regional plants, shared case records, and magical healing where appropriate. Not every plague is magical and not every cure is miraculous.
- **Military practice:** improved layered fortifications, siege defenses, organized supply columns, messengers, roads, reliable reserve forces, and weapons designed to halt fast raids or resist the undead.
- **Political cooperation:** agreements to open roads and trade grain even when rulers distrust one another, temporary neutral hospitals, and community institutions that endure after royal authority fails.

These techniques are inventions of people living during the Second Age. Although religious histories of [[Afrit the Deliverer|Afrit the Deliverer]] may later attribute recovery to a god, the original achievements remain those of generations of engineers, farmers, healers, and soldiers.

## The death of King Edran

[[King Edran]], the great king of the [[Grand Alliance]], guides rebuilding in the early Second Age but eventually **dies of disease**. His exact age, year of death, and the particular illness remain undecided. His death is one episode in a long series of human successions and renewals, not proof that Aenor collapses immediately.
