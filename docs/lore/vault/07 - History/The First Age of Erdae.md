# The First Age of Erdae

**Length: 1,675 years.** The First Age begins with [[Aen]]'s victory in the centuries-long War of Men and ends with the conclusion of the first great war against [[Volgan]], after the [[Battle of Dardan]] and the failure of the attempted invasion of the [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]]. The following [[Ages of Erdae|Second Age]] is the Age of Rebuilding.

> **Canon distinction:** The broad sequence and the events, figures, places, relationships, and durations explicitly supplied by the owner on 17 October 2026 are **established**. Names invented below, specific military tactics, council decisions, rituals, battlefield phases, dates other than the length of the age, and the motives of newly described participants are **proposed narrative development**, not fixed canon.

## 1. Before the First Age: the birth of Aen

Human beings and their many generations had existed for **thousands of years** before Aen. They possessed kingdoms, fought one another, and followed their own traditions. Some of the earliest men were stronger, longer-lived, and more naturally receptive to magic than the men of later ages. There was something godlike in their power, although they were still men and not gods.

[[Aen]] was exceptional even in that ancient world. He was **First of His Name**, born from magic and power without a direct father. This does **not** mean that he was the first biological human. His title as the First Man reflects his supernatural beginning and his status as the founder of the first unified kingdom of men. He is widely recognized as the greatest man who ever lived, and his blood is considered the most royal of all human lineages.

Aen lived for **more than four centuries** and had **hundreds of sons**. The surviving lineage central to later history is Aen, his son **Ein**, and Ein's son [[Eindil]]. The relationship between the name **Ainu**, the First Men, and all men living before Aen still needs formal definition. Do not assume that every early human was Aen's descendant.

### The War of Men

The War of Men lasted **hundreds of years** and killed **many thousands**. Aen united the kingdoms of men through a long, bloody struggle. The war ends in his victory and the establishment of the first kingdom of men, **Aenor** in the [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Middle Lands]]. The date of this victory is **First Age, Year 1**. The centuries of fighting preceding it are not counted as part of the First Age.

**Proposed depth:** Aen's cause did not begin with universal acceptance. Several human kings refused the idea of one crown. Others allied with Aen, submitted for protection, or resisted until their own cities were ruined. For the people of Aenor, his victory ended the wars of rival kings. For the conquered houses, the same victory marked the loss of independence. Those memories can survive in rival genealogies and conflicting chronicles.

**Proposed depth:** The first law of the united realm is the *Peace of the One Crown*. Human lords retain local inheritance and custom but cannot make war against one another without judgement from the crown. Breaking this peace becomes the original meaning of rebellion in later Aenoran law. The exact law and name are suggestions.

## 2. The first half of the First Age: peace and expansion

Aenor's victory brought **centuries of peace** to the central realm. The first half of the age is broadly remembered as prosperous. Farming, trade, roads, shipbuilding, and exploration spread beyond the Middle Lands. Such prosperity was real inside much of Aenor, but frontier settlement was often violent.

Aenoran explorers and settlers reached the [[Erdae and Its Regions|West Lands]]. **The West Lands already had inhabitants and kingdoms.** Their lands were not empty. Aenoran colonies, trade stations, and claims produced repeated wars with existing peoples, and the resulting hostility survives into the Fourth Age. The large sea separating West Lands and Middle Lands means that direct rule has always depended on ships, ports, local allies, and intermittent contact.

The kingdom made an alliance with what older accounts call *North Earth*, now the **North Lands**. Traders established contact with the peoples of the [[Erdae and Its Regions|East Lands]], sometimes called Easterlings, as well as routes to more distant peoples. Voyages also reached the Cold Lands, southern islands, and other eastern and southern shores. Much of the current map ultimately derives from the exploration begun in this period.

The **Dead Lands were exceptionally difficult to reach**. Their southern heat and dangerous surrounding seas obstructed ordinary expeditions. Men could chart distant coasts without knowing or controlling the lands beyond them. [[Zols Mistake|Zol's Mistake]] in the north was already a desolate and nearly unusable wasteland. Zol had arrived there long before Aen, and his local influence was comparatively dormant during the First Age.

**Proposed depth:** The *Roads of Aen* and later maritime charts become a source of political prestige. Aenoran kings insist that early discovery grants them sovereignty, while West Lands peoples reject the claim because they inhabited the shores long before Aenoran ships arrived. Fourth Age conflicts can descend from these competing histories without implying one uninterrupted war across many millennia.

## 3. The long weakening of Aenor

After the long prosperous period, **successive bad kings**, succession struggles, wars, and revolts weakened Aenor. The vast distance between crownlands, frontier colonies, and allied territories made the kingdom increasingly difficult to govern. Rival descendants of Aen competed for legitimacy. Parts of the West Lands resisted Aenor's authority, and old enemies exploited internal conflict.

**Proposed depth:** Aen's hundreds of sons created a durable political problem. Many later noble lines genuinely descend from him. A disputed succession therefore rarely concerns whether a claimant carries Aen's blood. It concerns which branch has the lawful right to rule, which oaths remain valid, and whose account of Aen's intentions the realm accepts. Later monarchs could use his name to justify actions he never approved.

**Proposed depth:** The core kingdom remains strong enough to defeat individual revolts, but its victories exhaust its finances and veterans. Provincial governors collect private taxes, old roads deteriorate, and remote garrisons begin acting independently. The appearance of order in the capital conceals a weakening ability to respond to a major invasion.

## 4. The rise of Volgan and the fall of the capital

[[Volgan]] is a [[Spirits and Their Ranks|Great Spirit]] of extraordinary antiquity. Before establishing his own realm, he served a [[New Gods|New God]], somehow overpowered that deity, and founded an independent dominion. This earlier history remains separate from the First Age war, and the name of the New God is not yet known.

During the **second millennium of the First Age**, Volgan invaded **Mirolon** and began a major conquest. With Aenor weakened and scattered, his victories were rapid and exceptionally destructive. He gathered forces in the Dead Lands, formed alliances with rulers in the eastern and southern lands, and opened contacts with powers in the **Dark Lands**. These alliances were selective. Not all Easterlings, southerners, Dark Lands peoples, or monsters joined him.

Volgan's armies included **corrupted elves, orcs, goblins, eastern and southern men, demons, devils associated with Shachihata and realms unknown to men, dragons, and many Great Spirits**. He released **Damned Spirits** from deep beneath mountains and the underground world. These beings brought additional forms of danger that most men had never encountered.

**Proposed strategic account:** Volgan used his Dead Lands stronghold and allied fleets to cross waters that had hindered earlier human explorers. Eastern client rulers provided ships, landing sites, food, and access to routes through the Dark Lands. He struck several distant Aenoran dependencies to keep the defenders scattered before driving at the central crownlands. His commanders seized food stores and ports wherever possible, since even magical armies require routes by which soldiers and war material can move.

Aenor's defenses failed. **The reigning king died, and Volgan's armies captured the capital.** In the working Fourth Age political geography the capital is **Parim**, though its precise historical status in the First Age has not been separately fixed. Aenor was on the verge of collapse. The loss of a royal city was also the loss of archives, succession records, sacred objects, and the administrative authority that held much of the human kingdom together.

## 5. The new king and the Grand Alliance

A new king emerged during the catastrophe. He became recognized as the **greatest king of men after Aen**. His defining achievement was the creation of the **Grand Alliance** against Volgan. He rallied men, elves, dwarves, giants, spirits, and other beings from across Erdae.

The alliance drew from the **West Lands, North Lands, islands, Magical Lands, and even portions of the Dark Lands**. It also received forces from distant elf peoples. **Lorien**, the great king of the **Gala elves** from the [[Erdae and Its Regions|Lands of the Sun]], personally led a formidable army to join the war. The Aenoran king knew little of the Gala elves before contact was made. This was one of the first significant encounters between their courts. Elves from the Magical Lands and elsewhere also gathered.

### King Edran, with proposed details of his early reign

The king is now established as **King Edran**. The suggested epithets are *Edran the Gatherer* or *the King of the Broken Crown*. In this proposed biography, he is a descendant of Aen from a branch that was not initially expected to inherit the throne. His legitimacy is contested, but a surviving council of nobles, soldiers, and refugees crowns him outside the occupied capital. He must win allies before he can hope to retake anything.

Edran's distinction is his ability to organize peoples who do not trust Aenor or one another. He accepts that Western rulers will fight under their **own banners**, that the North Lands remain allies rather than subjects, and that independent elves and Dark Lands peoples will not be placed under permanent human control. A proposed agreement, the **Charter of Free Banners**, gives all members a council seat and a common wartime command while protecting their separate sovereignties.

These concessions cost Edran support among hard-line Aenoran nobles. They make the Grand Alliance possible. His greatness is measured by the coalition he builds, the responsibilities he accepts, and the outcome of the war, even though his final decision will later damage that achievement.

### Why these alliances mattered

**Taryn** gives the West Lands a voice shaped by centuries of wars with Aenor. Her participation should not erase those wars. She fights Volgan because an unrestricted Volgan would be worse for her people, and she remembers Edran's promises after victory.

**Sirael** represents elves whose communities do not recognize a hereditary crown. Her support demonstrates that the Grand Alliance consists of independent societies, not one expanding empire.

**Rovek** prevents the Dark Lands from becoming a single evil faction. His soldiers know the terrain and the behavior of Volgan's allies, and they have a direct interest in defending their independence.

**Senra** and the island peoples provide maritime supply and transport. Their effort is essential to bringing distant forces together across Erdae's stormy and separated seas.

**Lorien** adds one of the most powerful armies in the war. He arrives as an ally, not an Aenoran subordinate. His relationship with Edran begins in respect and ends in serious disagreement.

## 6. The Battle of Dardan

**Established outcome:** The war's decisive confrontation is the **Battle of Dardan**. It kills **millions**, leaves a devastated wasteland, results in Volgan's defeat, and ends with other spirits stripping his power and banishing him to the **Void**. It is the pivotal battle of the war. Its precise position on the map, tactics, duration, participating named commanders, and banishment mechanism have not yet been established.

### Proposed place and strategic importance

**Dardan** could be placed in the southern approaches of the **Middle Lands**, at a broad plain adjoining an old inland road and a passage from the coast toward the Great Lake and the crownlands. The area should be close enough to Volgan's southern invasion routes to concentrate his main army and important enough that losing it would end any hope of retaking the central realm. This placement is **proposed** and requires future confirmation on the detailed map.

Dardan was formerly fertile, with settlements and roads that could supply a huge gathering of armies. Ancient stone formations below its hills, unnoticed by most mortals, could also make it suitable for a binding attempted by Great Spirits. Neither the landscape features nor the magical properties are canon yet.

### Proposed course of the battle: the Thirteen Days of Dardan

**Days 1–3: The lines close.** Volgan brings his central host against the Grand Alliance. Orc and goblin legions attack its forward positions, eastern and southern cavalry seek the open flanks, and corrupted elves break conventional defensive spells. Edran holds the mixed center. Taryn's Western forces defend one approach while Rovek's Dark Lands veterans prevent Volgan's scouts from finding an unguarded passage.

**Days 4–6: The crossings.** Dark dragons set supply depots and temporary defenses aflame. Brunek's dwarven engineers and giants from Hiloron defend the crossings and build replacement passages under fire. Senra's ships deliver food, weapons, and wounded men where inland roads cannot cope with the size of the armies. Thousands die merely keeping the coalition in position long enough to fight.

**Days 7–9: The earth opens.** Volgan releases the Damned Spirits into the battle. Creatures and disembodied powers emerge from beneath the hills, bringing down sections of the defensive line. Maerul and other Great Spirits hold the breaches while the dwarves collapse infected tunnels. Some of those spirits remain buried or sealed beneath the later wasteland of Dardan. This stage explains why the place is feared long after the armies have departed.

**Days 10–11: The host of the Gala elves.** Lorien commits his army after the Alliance has taken its worst losses. His soldiers counter the corrupted elven formations and help contain the dragons. Their arrival restores a collapsing flank. Sirael's elves from the Magical Lands fight alongside them, even though their customs and histories differ. The combined intervention makes it possible for Edran to counterattack, but the coalition is still badly outnumbered in several sectors.

**Day 12: The binding.** Ithren and other allied Great Spirits begin a dangerous rite beneath the battlefield. Volgan cannot simply be banished by a single spell. He has spent an immense portion of his active strength holding together his armies, summoning the deep beings, and forcing his will into the contested territory. The allied spirits exploit that concentration of power. To weaken him further, the Alliance must break the formations and bonds protecting him while the rite is prepared. Volgan's allied Great Spirits attempt to stop it.

**Day 13: The fall of Volgan.** Edran launches the final attack to hold open the way for the allied spirits. Lorien, Sirael, Brunek, and the surviving commanders take heavy losses. Volgan's guard is separated from him, and Ithren's rite takes hold. Several Great Spirits combine their power to **strip Volgan of the strength he can exercise in Erdae and cast him into the Void**. His essential existence is not destroyed. The effort also damages those who perform it. Ithren is proposed to become bound to the sealed threshold at Dardan, while Maerul loses much of the ability to manifest. This preserves the established immortality of Great Spirits while giving their victory a serious cost.

Volgan's army disintegrates as a unified force. Some units flee, surrender, or fight to the end. Other forces remain organized under surviving commanders. **The battle kills millions** when the full armies, support hosts, supernatural destruction, and subsequent collapse are considered, though no precise casualty figure is canon. Cities and fields around Dardan are ruined and the area becomes a wasteland. The names of individual days and the described military sequence are proposals.

### Why victory requires everyone

Dardan should not be a victory granted by a god, a single artifact, or one overpowering warrior. Edran's coalition makes it possible to survive the engagement. The elves provide military power and specialized knowledge, dwarves and giants hold crucial positions, island peoples keep the armies supplied, independent peoples of the West and Dark Lands protect the flanks, and allied Great Spirits carry out the banishment once Volgan is vulnerable. Each element matters.

The [[Holy Seven]] and the supreme Creator do not intervene. [[Zol]] does not intervene either. Zol's northern realm was already desolate, and his local power was still too restricted to make him a practical participant in this war.

## 7. The failed conquest of the Dead Lands

Volgan's banishment was an immense victory, but **the Dead Lands were not destroyed or conquered**. The king of Aenor wanted to enter and secure them so that Volgan's dominion could never threaten Erdae again. The elven leaders opposed the idea. The Alliance pressed forward anyway.

**Elzon** had gathered and prepared the surviving forces of Volgan. The invasion met fierce resistance and was **driven back with heavy losses**. The conditions of the Dead Lands and the exhaustion of the Alliance made further conquest too costly. The attack was abandoned. Many elves died in the campaign, and the king's decision badly damaged relations between men and elves, particularly given how much their forces had already sacrificed at Dardan.

### Proposed account: Elzon's defense

Elzon has survived Dardan by keeping a reserve outside the battle's central collapse or by escaping with surviving formations. His kind of being has since been established: he is a **Great Spirit**, the **Great Necromancer**, and a servant of Volgan. The story only requires him to be a capable commander who can lead the remnants without Volgan.

Having expected the possibility of Volgan's defeat, Elzon had prepared strongholds and fallback positions along the approaches to the Dead Lands. He withdrew surviving troops to defensible coasts, passes, and fortresses. He destroyed usable supply stores near the frontier and exploited weather, heat, and difficult terrain. The Alliance advanced into territory it could not feed or occupy without extraordinary losses. The defending armies had fewer soldiers than before Dardan, but they no longer had to fight Volgan's enormous offensive campaign. They only had to hold their own ground.

Edran viewed the invasion as necessary to prevent a future return. Lorien judged it a mistake after the decisive threat had been removed. Sirael and other elven leaders warned that the remaining troops could not endure another major struggle. When Elzon's counterattack struck the overextended invasion force, those warnings were vindicated. Lorien's people paid for a decision they had opposed.

This becomes the **central stain on Edran's otherwise great reign**. His commitment to a permanent peace led him into an avoidable campaign. Survivors of the Gala elves and Magical Lands return home with conflicting memories: gratitude for victory over Volgan and bitterness about the lives lost afterward. The consequences reach far into the later ages.

## 8. The end of the age and its legacy

The war ends when the remaining members of the Grand Alliance abandon the invasion of the Dead Lands and return to their homes. **Year 1675** closes the First Age. The exact year of Dardan within the final period of the age remains undetermined. The following Second Age begins with rebuilding a kingdom, healing lands, restoring trade, and responding to the devastation of the war.

The victory has lasting consequences:

- **Aenor survives**, but its capital, institutions, population, and foreign relationships have been severely damaged. The great kingdom must be rebuilt, and Edran's concessions to allies cannot simply be forgotten.
- **Volgan survives in exile in the Void.** A later return is possible, but its date, means, and any conditions upon him remain unwritten. Do not have him continuously ruling on the battlefield during the Second Age without first establishing how he returned.
- **Elzon and the remaining Dead Lands armies survive.** The Dead Lands remain an independent threat and a possible source of future rulers, cults, and restoration movements.
- **Dardan remains devastated**, remembered as the place where the largest forces of the age destroyed one another and Great Spirits brought down Volgan. Its exact location and how long it stays a wasteland remain open.
- **Relations between the races deteriorate after the victory.** The disastrous Dead Lands expedition weakens the trust of the Gala elves and other elven peoples in Aenor's kings.
- **The West Lands grievance survives.** The older dispute over Aenoran settlement, claims, and independence remains unresolved. Cooperation against Volgan did not erase centuries of conflict.
- **Eindil's extraordinary claim remains dormant.** When he emerges in the Fourth Age, his identity as son of Ein and grandson of Aen is a stronger blood claim than later descendants can make. He is also a living connection to a time most of the Fourth Age considers myth.

The second greatest king after Aen can therefore be remembered as a heroic victor who also made one of the most costly political decisions of the era. Different peoples preserve different accounts of him. Aenoran records praise the savior of the crown. Gala and Magical Lands chronicles remember his unnecessary pursuit into the Dead Lands. Dark Lands and Western chronicles may emphasize the price Edran promised for their loyalty.

## 10. Matters intentionally left open

1. Precisely when and where Aen first appeared, and how his fatherless magical birth occurred.
2. Whether *Ainu* means Aen's direct line, all First Men, or a different ancient designation.
3. The precise date and explanation of Eindil's freezing. His absence must remain consistent with his Fourth Age awakening. **Eindil and his brother Afrit have no established numerical age**, and the prior 100,000-year claim is superseded.
4. The names, reigns, and histories of Aenor's intermediate kings.
5. The details of **Edran**'s lineage, youth and early reign before he assembled the Grand Alliance.
6. The site of Dardan on the detailed map and the historical battlefield's terrain.
7. Which Great Spirits assisted Volgan, which assisted the Grand Alliance, and how the stripping and banishment actually worked.
8. The identity of the New God that Volgan once served, and the nature of the conflict that ended their relationship.
9. Whether Lorien and the Gala elves are a distinct people from, or a branch of, the other Sunhot elves of the Lands of the Sun.
10. The scope of Aenor's early rule in the West Lands, and what the Fourth Age kingdom still claims.
11. The precise rite by which Elzon later returned Volgan from the Void in the Second Age. His return itself is established.
12. The detailed sequence of the fixed **2,133-year Second Age** and the **1,200-plus-year Third Age**. The Third Age is provisionally 1,260 years.

## Retrospective revelation: Afrit's hidden role

**Established later in the owner's Second Age account:** [[Afrit]] played a significant but unrecognized part in Volgan's First Age defeat by assembling demons, spirits, men, and elves against him. His motive was to weaken both Volgan and the survivors so that he could rise in the Second Age. This adds a covert layer to the Grand Alliance's victory without replacing its established military and spiritual achievements. How he concealed his alliances and how much the principal commanders suspected are undecided.
