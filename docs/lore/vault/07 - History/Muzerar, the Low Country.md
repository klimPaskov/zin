# Muzerar, the Low Country

**Established (owner, 25 October 2026; decision K64):** Muzerar was a great realm of men that sank beneath the Crown Sea before the War of Men. Its mothers overdrew the land's magic, and no god judged it. Great realms of men stood before Aen, and none had one crown over all men.

**Status of this note:** owner-adopted on 25 October 2026 (decision K64). The items marked **Mystery kept** and **Gated by play** are not answered in text, and the working labels in quotation marks are names the owner has not fixed as canon. The lore's details are in the design addendum `docs/plans/deep_history/k62_fallen_realms.md`, and the play-facing summary is `docs/specs/02_lore/10_fallen_realms.md`.

## Names

- **Muzerar**, in Orran, the name of the Veiled and the Redbark in their own tongue and the name the restored Moot takes (K64, M2).
- **Ozelkak**, "the Many Halls", the city of the Moot, as the cord-mothers name it.
- **Humaran**, "the low sea", the giants' name for the water over the land.
- **The Nimlan banks**, the sailors' chart name for the shoals where nets bring up field-walls and quern-stones.
- **The Low Country**, the name in Common Speech.

## The people and their law

The Low Country's people were Orran-speaking men, whose descent ran through mothers. Every family belonged to a beast-clan: Wolf, Bear, Eagle, Horse, Raven and Water.

The realm was governed by the **Moot of Mothers** (working label) at Ozelkak, where the eldest cord-mother of every clan held a seat. For a war the Moot chose a war-chief, an *ukhar*, and took the office back when the war ended, so the Low Country had no king and no crown in all its centuries.

Its law was kept on knotted cords, one knot for each field, debt, marriage and death. The long peace between its clans is called the **Peace of the Cords** (working label).

The hill giants lived on the low hills as partners and not as subjects. They raised the long sea-dikes from field-stone and kept their cairn-fields on the high ground.

## Age and extent

The Low Country was at its height in the Age of Kingdoms, about 1,500 to 900 years before Aen's victory. The Long Settling ran from about 900 to 621 BA, and the Night of the Sea came in 620 BA.

At its height it covered the plain that now lies under the eastern two-thirds of the Crown Sea, from the Aenor Isle shore west to within a day's sail of Varnondor's island, north to the Sea of Monsters and the Goldoroth arm, and south to the Nesmael. Aenor Isle and the Tinta Hills were its eastern heights. On the mainland it held the river valleys of the western and central Middle Lands, which the Vale of Aenor now covers, together with the Konorar up to the red-oak forest by the Narrow Strait. Hirolon's ring stayed the giants' own, and the Moot never ruled it.

## What made it great

Three harvests a year came from the low fields behind the giants' dikes. A moot governed more land than Aenor ever held, and it did so without a crown and without a long war between its clans. Ozelkak's moot-halls stood on piled mounds above the fields. The **cord-reckoning** kept account of every field and of everything a field had given, which makes it the first account-keeping of Aoaz.

## How it fell

The mothers drew on the magic of the place to give their fields the third harvest and their cord-mothers their long rule. Over the centuries the ground lost its strength and settled below the level of the sea, so that it had to be held by ever higher dikes. Every field the sea took was tied as a black knot on the Moot's own cord.

The giants' cairn-keepers warned the Moot. The mothers divided between those who would stop drawing on the land and those who would draw harder to raise the dikes, and the second party won. In the Night of the Sea, in 620 BA, a storm-tide broke the last dikes, and the sea took Ozelkak and the low fields in one night.

This is the Low Country's own cause, and the vault's working model of magic gives it the greatest scale that the world remembers. No god judged the realm, and its ruin took centuries before it took a night.

## After the fall

The surviving eastern provinces became the Orran kingdoms of the river valleys. Their wars over a shrinking land ran on into the War of Men, which Aen ended by making one crown over the kingdoms, which is the thing the Low Country never had. The Orran houses that would not kneel took their cords into the grey downs and the red-oak forest.

The giants withdrew into their ring and raised the **Eldest Cairn**, to which its keeper adds one stone in every year since the sea came. The count is the one number of the deep past that a player learns in play, when the Cord and the Stone are brought together, and its total on 1 Lumae 4A 1748 is 7,436 stones.

## What survives

- **A tongue.** Orran lives among the Veiled and the Redbark, and under the Aenoran names of the Vale's rivers and towns.
- **A law and a custom.** Mother-line descent, the cord-mothers of the Cursed Lands and the Bloody Forest, and the Old Knots faith. The Isle Houses of Aenor Isle are ruled by their eldest women, who choose their queen in the tide-chamber at the turn of the tide and give their dead to the tide. The Low Country's last law is kept by a people who no longer speak its tongue and do not know why they keep it.
- **Places.** The tide-chambers of Orohaf, rock-cut moot-halls of the last eastern hill that flood at high water. The Nimlan banks. The **Mothers' Mound** (working label), the top of Ozelkak's greatest mound, which breaks the water as a reef west of Aenor Isle at the lowest spring tides; the map gains one islet there. The Eldest Cairn in Hirolon. The eldest barrow of the Cursed Lands, where the Last Cord lies, and the Old Tallies, which keep a yearly count on knotted cords.
- **Relics.** The Last Cord, the Mothers' Stone and the dike-stones.

## Knowledge layers

- **Authorial truth.** The overdraw of the place caused the sinking, as described above.
- **Belief.** The Veiled tell it as "the land that was asked for too much", and count the souring of their downs by the [[Black Roots]] as the second land to die under them.
- **Disputed.** Scholars of Parim, once they hear of it, argue that the land sank by its own nature and that the cords exaggerate.
- **Mystery kept.** The giants know, and will say only that the land was asked for too much.
- **Gated by play.** The year of the Night of the Sea and where the dikes stood, learned only when the Cord and the Stone are brought together.
