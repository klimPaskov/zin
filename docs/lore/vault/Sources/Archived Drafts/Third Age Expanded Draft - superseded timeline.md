# Third Age expanded historical draft (obsolete 128-year timeline)

**Do not treat this text as the current timeline.** It preserves an earlier longer narrative in full for detailed worldbuilding reference. The formerly proposed 128-year span was rejected. The Third Age now lasts **at least 1,200 years**, with **1,260 years** the current proposed model. Names, battles and mechanisms described in this archived text are not automatically established lore. For the current authoritative version, see [[The Third Age of Erdae]], [[Ages of Erdae]] and [[Third Age Canon Register]].

# The Third Age of Erdae

*The reign of the Great Necromancer, the destruction of Elzon, and the rise of Afrit as the Deliverer*

**Historical opening, fixed:** the Third Age begins immediately after [[Elzon]] captures [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Aenor]] in **year 2133 of the [[The Second Age of Erdae|Second Age]]**. [[Volgan]] has returned to Erdae but remains too weak to leave the [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]]. Elzon rules conquered Aenor in practice while continuing to obey Volgan.

**Historical ending, fixed in direction:** Elzon does not remain ruler indefinitely. He ultimately **dies permanently**, Aenor is restored under a good king, and the [[The Fourth Age of Erdae|Fourth Age]] begins with long peace. [[Afrit]] takes a decisive part in Elzon's downfall, then becomes publicly celebrated and worshipped as a **New God and savior**. The truth of his cosmic divine rank has not been settled.

**Proposed detailed history:** the exact reign of **128 years**, the Black Roots, the final battle, the mechanism by which Zol consumes Elzon, the names of the resistance, king, institutions, and campaigns below are carefully developed story proposals. They are not automatically established by the owner. [[Third Age Canon Register]] separates fixed lore and this proposed history.

## Before the Third Age: the Second Age's complicated legacy

The [[The Second Age of Erdae|Second Age]] lasted **2,133 years**, the longest known numbered age. The end of that age should never be taken to mean two thousand years of uninterrupted misery. Famine and plague recurred for generations, but survivors became better at preventing them. Engineers developed stronger walls, maintained defensible roads, and improved military organization. Farmers devised reliable systems of irrigation, storage, crop rotation, and seed reserves. Physicians and healers learned to treat or prevent diseases which had once wiped out whole settlements. Some of these developments involved magic. Many were achievements of ordinary men and other peoples.

The grand king [[King Edran]] had survived the First Age's wars and guided the early recovery. He eventually died of **disease**, not in battle. His successor kings lacked some of his authority, and Elzon's slow rebuilding of his forces exploited each succession, famine, regional quarrel, and outbreak.

The elves **did not send armies to save Aenor when Elzon invaded**. The refusal arose from Edran's earlier decision to invade the Dead Lands after Volgan's banishment. Elven losses in that failed campaign, commanded against their advice, had broken the political trust created by the Grand Alliance. Individual elves could still take pity on refugees, but the elven kingdoms did not renew their old military oath. This distinction matters in the Third Age: the elves of the [[Erdae and Its Regions|Magical Lands]] resist attacks on their *own* territory, without joining a second grand expedition to liberate men.

The techniques and institutions that survived the Second Age provide the material foundation for Aenor's resistance. Afrit will later receive public praise for relief and recovery, but the achievements of generations of farmers, physicians, builders, and human rulers should not be erased from the real history.

## I. The occupation, Third Age years 1 to 24 (proposed)

The fall of the Aenoran capital in the final year of the Second Age ended the old kingdom's freedom but did not end the war. Elzon had enough power to take its throne, and armies of orcs, goblins, corrupted men, and raised dead occupied the most important roads and cities. Outside their garrisons, entire districts refused to accept his authority. Local rulers fled to defended villages, abandoned strongholds, hills, and river crossings. Refugees carried news from one settlement to another. Kingdoms could be conquered much faster than they could be governed.

Elzon's practical rule was a military occupation supported by fear and necromancy. He required living officials to collect food, maintain infrastructure, and manage production. The dead could march and fight, but they could not recreate every skill or institution of the kingdom they had defeated. Each levy or confiscation provoked evasion, flight, or resistance.

He did not take the ancient royal line into his own family, and he could not legitimately become a descendant of [[Aen]]. Whether he publicly styled himself regent or conqueror is open. A possible epithet for historians is **the Regent of the Graves**, while the earlier working name *Pale Regency* can remain an informal term for his regime.

From the Dead Lands, Volgan gave orders through trusted captains and magical communication. Volgan was still weak. He could not travel to Aenor, take the field in person, or regularly exert his former strength outside his own domain. Elzon therefore made most military and administrative decisions himself, but did not renounce his master.

### The undead begin to fail

At the beginning of the occupation Elzon appeared invincible. The bodies raised during the fall of Aenor still marched in great numbers. Over the following decades, however, some weakened, collapsed, or lost cohesion. Elzon could rebuild their bindings, but every restoration demanded more of his power.

This was not simply an age of military decline. The living population had already learned to fight against Elzon's troops. Its fortification techniques and plague controls helped concealed strongholds survive sieges and refuge camps endure overcrowding. The very knowledge acquired during the Second Age was becoming a weapon against its conqueror.

## II. Why Elzon could not sustain the conquest (proposed rules)

The Dead Lands have a magical nature that nourishes Elzon's necromancy. Aenor, in the heart of the Middle Lands, does not. The [[The Fading|Fading]] and the different magical conditions of Erdae also constrain [[Zol]], whose power Elzon had borrowed during the Second Age.

Three rules would govern Elzon's undead armies:

1. **The dead are bound, not restored to ordinary life.** Their bodies are animated by necromantic commands. They cannot maintain themselves indefinitely without magic.
2. **Magic is affected by the land where it is used.** Within the Dead Lands, Elzon can replenish his own power. Across most of Aenor he spends far more power than he receives. A tether to the Dead Lands may help, but distance and the enemy's attacks interrupt it.
3. **The power taken from Zol is borrowed at a cost.** Elzon can use death-magic far beyond his normal capacity, but the channel is not a free reservoir. It binds some of the necromancer's very identity to the Old God's power. Elzon can notice exhaustion and decay without understanding that the bond may eventually allow Zol to consume him.

This is not an act of direct intervention by Zol. A mortal opening a floodgate may be swept away by the water without the sea choosing to strike. The analogy has limits, but the causal principle is important. Zol does not arrive in Aenor, take a body, command Elzon, or reveal himself. The law of the magic Elzon borrowed can destroy its user while Zol remains dormant and almost unknown.

This rule also makes it possible for a Great Spirit to **die permanently** in an otherwise immortal cosmology. Elzon is not merely defeated, returned to the Void, or trapped. If his spiritual identity is consumed in full, there is no Elzon left who could be summoned, freed, or resurrected. His individual existence ends. The Old God absorbs what remains of his essence, but does not become Elzon.

## III. The unconquered Magical Lands, years 25 to 65 (proposed)

The [[Erdae and Its Regions|Magical Lands]] were never brought completely under Elzon's control. He occupied or raided some approaches, but he did not break the defenders' hold on their forests, places of power, and concealed refuges. His necromancy met forms of magic it could not easily corrupt.

The central defender is **[[Varner]]**, a wizard who is a [[Spirits and Their Ranks|Lesser Spirit]]. Varner cannot overpower Elzon in a straightforward contest of strength. His particular gift is the capacity to perceive and undo the workings that keep the dead bound to the necromancer. He can lift the curse of the dead and release them from Elzon's command. This requires care, time, and often a protected place. It cannot instantly free an entire battlefield at a distance.

Varner works with the independent inhabitants of the Magical Lands. The elves defend their homeland, prevent the theft of its life-magic, and protect refuge sites. They do not march as allies of Aenor under an elven king. This preserves the political consequences of Edran's mistake.

Beside Varner is **[[Golbran]]**, his small purple humanoid servant of unknown origin. Golbran is not identified as an elf, demon, spirit, or mortal race. His appearance does not settle what he is. In the proposed story, he is an ingenious courier and keeper of Varner's enchanted objects. Because he is physically small, he can travel routes inaccessible to an army and reach occupied towns with tools for local curse-breaking. His work involves courage, experience, and choice, not an arbitrary power surpassing his master.

### Proposed institution: the Lantern Chain

Varner and Golbran help maintain a secret chain of sanctuaries and contacts linking the outer Magical Lands to communities in Aenor. Varner teaches a limited rite of release, which can quiet an individual corpse or interrupt the bond to a nearby group of dead. Skilled humans learn to operate wards and keep them from breaking. These methods are slowly shared between isolated strongholds.

The network never becomes a single great elven-human alliance. Its messengers and healers provide local relief, while the main armies of the Magical Lands remain committed to defending their own borders. The living peoples of Aenor must still fight their own liberation.

## IV. Elzon seeks to make another Dead Lands, years 50 to 93 (proposed)

Elzon finally recognizes the full scale of his problem. The decay of his undead hosts will eventually leave him without sufficient force to suppress the human revolts. His personal power also weakens whenever he remains in Aenor for long stretches. He might return to the Dead Lands to recover, but doing so repeatedly endangers his control of the occupied kingdom.

He therefore attempts to change the kingdom's underlying magic so that the country itself sustains him. The proposed project is known in surviving texts as **the Black Roots**.

At its heart is a network of buried necromantic anchors, filled grave-fields, corrupted wells, and sites of concentrated death-magic. These anchors would gradually turn the richest parts of Aenor into a second source of power for Elzon and his servants. The design must eventually reach the Magical Lands. Only there can Elzon find the living magical currents he intends to invert and bind into a permanent necromantic domain.

The project is incomplete and contested. Where its effects spread, the dead become harder to quiet, crops weaken, and some settlements become uninhabitable. Human rebels destroy shrines and anchor stones. Varner reverses parts of the corruption. Golbran delivers his wards to hidden allies. The defenders of the Magical Lands prevent the final network from closing across their territory.

The danger is immense. If Elzon succeeds, his occupation could endure without drawing continuously on the Dead Lands. In time it might spread the condition of his new kingdom across other regions of Erdae. The conflict has become larger than the liberation of one human realm.

## V. Afrit builds the appearance of a savior, years 60 to 113 (proposed)

[[Afrit]] spent the First Age secretly arranging forces against Volgan and the Second Age accumulating worship, bargains, and a wider cult network. His late Second Age ascension has already strengthened him, although its metaphysical nature is unknown.

For much of the Third Age his open face is different from the necromancer's. Afrit's followers feed the hungry, keep roads open, shelter displaced families, and fund medical care. His organization spreads through households and communities that otherwise receive little help. The system should draw on genuine Second Age advances in medicine, food storage, sanitation, and agriculture. Some relief may come from Afrit's real supernatural gifts, but much of it depends on ordinary knowledge and labor.

A proposed name for the public organization is **the Houses of the First Light**. Villages welcome its healers and organizers. Some survivors pray to Afrit because they believe he hears them. Others praise him because his followers helped them survive. Many are sincere people without any knowledge of their patron's hidden ambitions.

Afrit's purpose is political and religious. He wants Aenor to survive, but he also wants its recovery to depend on institutions that owe their fortunes to him. He competes with Elzon for the future of the kingdom while refusing to place himself openly on its throne. For Afrit, the end of Elzon's reign could be the beginning of a much wider dominion built through gratitude, belief, and voluntary dependence.

### Public miracles and hidden knowledge

Afrit can perform or sponsor acts that witnesses sincerely regard as miracles. A village saved from disease, a protected refuge, and a ward holding back the undead are real achievements, not simple illusions. Their political interpretation, however, is shaped by people working for him. His followers ensure that his name appears on records, prayers, shrines, relief stores, and the histories later written about the war.

Afrit also learns something about Elzon that the necromancer has not fully grasped. Repeated use of old death-magic leaves a bond whose other end touches Zol. Afrit cannot command Zol, and he cannot predict the Old God's will. He can arrange events that make Elzon desperate enough to draw upon that bond one final time.

Afrit's involvement should never make Varner, Golbran, or the living resistance irrelevant. His opportunity exists because they spent decades breaking the necromancer's defenses.

## VI. The War of Unbinding, years 114 to 127 (proposed)

By the beginning of the final war, Elzon still rules the capital and many roads, but his position is weaker than it appears. More of his dead must be sustained with each year. His living officers are unreliable, his garrisons are stretched, and the Black Roots have not reached the magical sources he needs.

The surviving human lords have become experienced at resisting occupation. Protected seed stores help settlements feed armies without exposing every harvest to confiscation. Fortified market towns provide bases for long campaigns. Communal medical practices allow forces to recover from outbreaks that would have broken earlier generations.

Varner's Lantern Chain connects strongholds that had previously fought in isolation. Small groups can interrupt the dead near an attacked gate or clear a route through an old grave-field. They remain vulnerable to orcs, goblins, demons, and living collaborators. Removing necromancy does not remove ordinary war.

Afrit's Houses of the First Light increasingly supply the rebels with food, refuge, messengers, and funds. Some of his followers know that he intends to claim the place of protector when the war ends. Most believe they are working for a sacred cause. Afrit stays away from the position of open claimant, leaving the struggle for the human crown to men.

### Proposed key characters

**Harion of Selen** is a possible human leader and eventual king, a descendant of one of Aen's many royal lines. He may descend through records preserved during the conquest by Arelon, the previously proposed archivist. He is capable, principled, and determined to restore civil rule. His future legitimacy should depend on more than a convenient birth certificate. The free communities know what he has done during the occupation.

**Seron of Selen**, an earlier proposed human commander, might be Harion's predecessor or ancestor in the resistance. The relationship is not fixed. Their stories should not be collapsed into one without a later choice.

**Varner** refuses to turn his particular power over the dead into a claim to rule them. He aims to end the curse and protect the Magical Lands. **Golbran**, his servant, risks his life carrying the final workings of the release rite into places Varner cannot safely reach.

## VII. The Last Tithe, Third Age year 128 (proposed climax)

The final confrontation is remembered under several names: **the Last Tithe**, **the Night of Severance**, and **the Fall of the Black Crown**. These titles are proposals for different cultural memories of the same event.

The remaining free forces surround the occupied Aenoran capital. They cannot win a simple assault while Elzon still commands the full strength of his undead. The rebellion must instead break the magical structure that allows him to sustain his host.

The operation unfolds on three fronts.

**First, the living armies cut the anchors.** Human forces destroy the grave-fields and fortified shrines that supply the occupied capital. Their success rests on years of patient resistance and the practical knowledge inherited from the Second Age. Elzon loses much of his ability to reinforce the undead near the city.

**Second, Varner breaks the curse.** At the final junction of the Black Roots, Golbran carries a protected focus through the city and places it where Varner's magic can reach the bound dead. Varner begins a great release rite from the edge of the Magical Lands, aided by dispersed local wards. The curse does not vanish from all Erdae, but enough of Elzon's host is freed to make his final stand untenable.

**Third, Elzon draws beyond his limit.** He has no time to retreat to the Dead Lands and recover. His new domain has not become self-sustaining. He attempts to reinforce every collapsing bond by pulling immense power through the older death-magic that once helped him win Aenor. In his desperation he opens the channel to Zol farther than ever before.

That is the moment of catastrophe. Elzon discovers that the ancient power was never truly his. The bond allows his own spiritual essence to be drawn away. He attempts to escape, sever the channel, and retreat into his native magic, but the strain of holding the dead prevents him from withdrawing. The process destroys the coherence of his spirit. His identity is consumed. **Elzon dies in the full and final sense of the word.**

Zol does not appear. The Old God does not speak, command the battle, or conquer territory. The consumption is a consequence of the death-magic Elzon invoked. It gives Zol a great addition of strength, but the [[The Fading|Fading]] and Erdae's alien magical conditions still prevent him from using that strength freely. His importance as an open actor in Erdae remains for the end of the Fourth Age.

### Why Afrit is genuinely praised

Elzon's collapse endangers more than the necromancer. The Black Roots begin to break violently. Unbound death-magic threatens to drag living souls, not only the undead, through the open channel. The city and surrounding settlements could suffer a catastrophe larger than the battle itself.

Afrit finally reveals a power that most people had only heard described in distant miracles. He raises a protective working around the exposed city and sustains the release long enough for Varner to sever the remaining bonds. Thousands survive who might otherwise have died. Witnesses see his light above the ruined walls and hear that he has ended the dominion of the dead.

**Afrit really does save lives.** He is also the principal beneficiary of the story later told about the event. His priests and followers call him the Deliverer and attribute the entire victory to his grace. The records of the human resistance, Varner, Golbran, and years of preparation gradually become secondary in the official religious histories.

Afrit has not become monarch. His more durable victory is that the restored kingdom now regards him as its sacred rescuer.

## VIII. The New God of Deliverance (public faith, established direction)

Following Elzon's destruction, Afrit is publicly acclaimed as a **New God**, often proposed under the religious title **Afrit the Deliverer**, with a domain associated with **Deliverance, Renewal, and Protection**. This is a genuine shift in Erdae's public religious life. Shrines become temples, local caretakers become clergy, and rites of thanks enter state ceremonies. People who had previously feared his name may now consider him the power that saved the civilized world.

**Critical distinction:** Afrit's public divinity is established as part of the story. Whether his cosmic rank is *actually* New God remains deliberately open. Existing cosmology says that the New Gods were created by the [[Holy Seven]]. Worship and miracles alone do not prove that Afrit was created in that order. He might have always possessed undisclosed divinity, acquired some form of divine mantle, or successfully established worship of a being outside the divine order. These are alternative theories, not canon explanations.

The title *New God of Deliverance* describes how the common religions classify him, not a confirmed change to the universe's divine hierarchy. This preserves later revelations and disagreements among priests and scholars.

## IX. The restored human crown and the end of the Third Age

The survivors need a human king to restore laws, rebuild ruined towns, and keep the numerous independent free forces from turning against one another. A candidate is **Harion the Restorer**, who had earned confidence among the resistance. The crown is deliberately awarded by an assembly of human nobles, towns, and commanders, with genealogical evidence serving as one element of his claim.

Harion is intended to be a **genuinely good king**. He restores courts, abolishes Elzon's military decrees, protects returned refugees, and opens granaries and roads. He recognizes the people of the Magical Lands as independent and declines to demand an elven army in repayment for Varner's help. No part of his goodness requires that Afrit control his choices.

Harion also allows the temples of Afrit to continue their relief work. At the beginning this is a reasonable policy. Those institutions saved lives, hold useful stocks of food and medicine, and are trusted by many people. The first king cannot foresee what will happen when religious gifts, appointments, account books, and services become interwoven with the state over many generations.

The Third Age ends when Elzon is gone, Aenor has a recognized free king, and a new settlement has begun. The [[The Fourth Age of Erdae|Fourth Age]] starts with the restoration of peace rather than with another immediate invasion.

## X. The hidden foundations of the Fourth Age

The Fourth Age is intended to enjoy **more than a thousand years of peace** in the central kingdom. Multiple generations of good and bad rulers will succeed one another. The first restored king does not personally reign for a millennium. Roads, markets, farming, military organization, public medicine, and education benefit from knowledge acquired during the long Second Age. The kingdom can be prosperous for sincere and practical reasons.

Afrit remains publicly revered as the god who saved Aenor. His temples become important providers of education, welfare, healing, and diplomacy. Across many generations, his agents gain influence over court appointments, treaties, family fortunes, and the records through which kings interpret their own history. They need not openly overthrow anyone. The kingdom slowly becomes dependent on structures that they control.

This gives Afrit a position that resembles rule without an overt crown. Ordinary people know and worship their Deliverer. They do **not** know how much of the kingdom's political life he privately directs or what he wants to do when the opportunity comes.

Not every priest of Afrit is malicious. Most are sincere. Afrit's corruption works because useful institutions can gradually acquire hidden obligations and loyalties. In the late Fourth Age, this long network can intersect with the wealthy [[Golden King]], [[Castle of Yeldenne and Glo|Glo]], [[Roka]]'s [[Necklace of the Abyss]], and the return of [[Einendil]]. The Golden King's secret murder by Afrit and Einendil's eventual coronation remain part of the preexisting future story, with their exact ordering still open.

The death of Elzon also has a delayed second result. Through the final consumption, Zol gains more strength and a firmer magical trace in Erdae. He still cannot simply manifest, and most of the world neither knows nor cares. Over centuries his servants continue sustaining his presence. His consequential return to the story remains reserved for the end of the Fourth Age.
