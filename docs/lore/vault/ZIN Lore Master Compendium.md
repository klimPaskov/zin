- **Mixed:** A note containing both established plot points and proposals, labeled within the text.

## 07 - History/The First Age of Erdae.md

# The First Age of Erdae

**Length: 1,675 years.** The First Age begins with [[Aen]]'s victory in the centuries-long War of Men and ends with the conclusion of the first great war against [[Volgan]], after the [[Battle of Dardan]] and the failure of the attempted invasion of the [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]]. The following [[Ages of Erdae|Second Age]] is the Age of Rebuilding.

> **Canon distinction:** The broad sequence and the events, figures, places, relationships, and durations explicitly supplied by the owner on 17 October 2026 are **established**. Names invented below, specific military tactics, council decisions, rituals, battlefield phases, dates other than the length of the age, and the motives of newly described participants are **proposed narrative development**, not fixed canon.

## 1. Before the First Age: the birth of Aen

Human beings and their many generations had existed for **thousands of years** before Aen. They possessed kingdoms, fought one another, and followed their own traditions. Some of the earliest men were stronger, longer-lived, and more naturally receptive to magic than the men of later ages. There was something godlike in their power, although they were still men and not gods.

[[Aen]] was exceptional even in that ancient world. He was **First of His Name**, born from magic and power without a direct father. This does **not** mean that he was the first biological human. His title as the First Man reflects his supernatural beginning and his status as the founder of the first unified kingdom of men. He is widely recognized as the greatest man who ever lived, and his blood is considered the most royal of all human lineages.

Aen lived for **more than four centuries** and had **hundreds of sons**. The surviving lineage central to later history is Aen, his son **Ein**, and Ein's son [[Eindil]]. The relationship between the name **Ainu**, the First Men, and all men living before Aen still needs formal definition. Do not assume that every early human was Aen's descendant.

### The War of Men

The War of Men lasted **hundreds of years** and killed **many thousands**. Aen united the kingdoms of men through a long, bloody struggle. The war ends in his victory and the establishment of the first kingdom of men, **Aenor** in the [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Middle Lands]]. The date of this victory is **First Age, Year 1**. The centuries of fighting preceding it are not counted as part of the First Age.

**Proposed depth:** Aen's cause did not begin with universal acceptance. Several human kings refused the idea of one crown. Others allied with Aen, submitted for protection, or resisted until their own cities were ruined. For the people of Aenor, his victory ended the wars of rival kings. For the conquered houses, the same victory marked the loss of independence. Those memories can survive in rival genealogies and conflicting chronicles.

**Proposed depth:** The first law of the united realm is the *Peace of the One Crown*. Human lords retain local inheritance and custom but cannot make war against one another without judgement from the crown. Breaking this peace becomes the original meaning of rebellion in later Aenoran law. The exact law and name are suggestions.

## 2. The first half of the First Age: peace and expansion

Aenor's victory brought **centuries of peace** to the central realm. The first half of the age is broadly remembered as prosperous. Farming, trade, roads, shipbuilding, and exploration spread beyond the Middle Lands. Such prosperity was real inside much of Aenor, but frontier settlement was often violent.

Aenoran explorers and settlers reached the [[Erdae and Its Regions|West Lands]]. **The West Lands already had inhabitants and kingdoms.** Their lands were not empty. Aenoran colonies, trade stations, and claims produced repeated wars with existing peoples, and the resulting hostility survives into the Fourth Age. The large sea separating West Lands and Middle Lands means that direct rule has always depended on ships, ports, local allies, and intermittent contact.

The kingdom made an alliance with what older accounts call *North Earth*, now the **North Lands**. Traders established contact with the peoples of the [[Erdae and Its Regions|East Lands]], sometimes called Easterlings, as well as routes to more distant peoples. Voyages also reached the Cold Lands, southern islands, and other eastern and southern shores. Much of the current map ultimately derives from the exploration begun in this period.

The **Dead Lands were exceptionally difficult to reach**. Their southern heat and dangerous surrounding seas obstructed ordinary expeditions. Men could chart distant coasts without knowing or controlling the lands beyond them. [[Zols Mistake|Zol's Mistake]] in the north was already a desolate and nearly unusable wasteland. Zol had arrived there long before Aen, and his local influence was comparatively dormant during the First Age.

**Proposed depth:** The *Roads of Aen* and later maritime charts become a source of political prestige. Aenoran kings insist that early discovery grants them sovereignty, while West Lands peoples reject the claim because they inhabited the shores long before Aenoran ships arrived. Fourth Age conflicts can descend from these competing histories without implying one uninterrupted war across many millennia.

## 3. The long weakening of Aenor

After the long prosperous period, **successive bad kings**, succession struggles, wars, and revolts weakened Aenor. The vast distance between crownlands, frontier colonies, and allied territories made the kingdom increasingly difficult to govern. Rival descendants of Aen competed for legitimacy. Parts of the West Lands resisted Aenor's authority, and old enemies exploited internal conflict.

**Proposed depth:** Aen's hundreds of sons created a durable political problem. Many later noble lines genuinely descend from him. A disputed succession therefore rarely concerns whether a claimant carries Aen's blood. It concerns which branch has the lawful right to rule, which oaths remain valid, and whose account of Aen's intentions the realm accepts. Later monarchs could use his name to justify actions he never approved.

**Proposed depth:** The core kingdom remains strong enough to defeat individual revolts, but its victories exhaust its finances and veterans. Provincial governors collect private taxes, old roads deteriorate, and remote garrisons begin acting independently. The appearance of order in the capital conceals a weakening ability to respond to a major invasion.

## 4. The rise of Volgan and the fall of the capital

[[Volgan]] is a [[Spirits and Their Ranks|Great Spirit]] of extraordinary antiquity. Before establishing his own realm, he served a [[New Gods|New God]], somehow overpowered that deity, and founded an independent dominion. This earlier history remains separate from the First Age war, and the name of the New God is not yet known.

During the **second millennium of the First Age**, Volgan invaded **Mirolon** and began a major conquest. With Aenor weakened and scattered, his victories were rapid and exceptionally destructive. He gathered forces in the Dead Lands, formed alliances with rulers in the eastern and southern lands, and opened contacts with powers in the **Dark Lands**. These alliances were selective. Not all Easterlings, southerners, Dark Lands peoples, or monsters joined him.

Volgan's armies included **corrupted elves, orcs, goblins, eastern and southern men, demons, devils associated with Shachihata and realms unknown to men, dragons, and many Great Spirits**. He released **Damned Spirits** from deep beneath mountains and the underground world. These beings brought additional forms of danger that most men had never encountered.

**Proposed strategic account:** Volgan used his Dead Lands stronghold and allied fleets to cross waters that had hindered earlier human explorers. Eastern client rulers provided ships, landing sites, food, and access to routes through the Dark Lands. He struck several distant Aenoran dependencies to keep the defenders scattered before driving at the central crownlands. His commanders seized food stores and ports wherever possible, since even magical armies require routes by which soldiers and war material can move.

Aenor's defenses failed. **The reigning king died, and Volgan's armies captured the capital.** In the working Fourth Age political geography the capital is **Parim**, though its precise historical status in the First Age has not been separately fixed. Aenor was on the verge of collapse. The loss of a royal city was also the loss of archives, succession records, sacred objects, and the administrative authority that held much of the human kingdom together.

## 5. The new king and the Grand Alliance

A new king emerged during the catastrophe. He became recognized as the **greatest king of men after Aen**. His defining achievement was the creation of the **Grand Alliance** against Volgan. He rallied men, elves, dwarves, giants, spirits, and other beings from across Erdae.

The alliance drew from the **West Lands, North Lands, islands, Magical Lands, and even portions of the Dark Lands**. It also received forces from distant elf peoples. **Lorien**, the great king of the **Gala elves** from the [[Erdae and Its Regions|Lands of the Sun]], personally led a formidable army to join the war. The Aenoran king knew little of the Gala elves before contact was made. This was one of the first significant encounters between their courts. Elves from the Magical Lands and elsewhere also gathered.

### King Edran, with proposed details of his early reign

The king is now established as **King Edran**. The suggested epithets are *Edran the Gatherer* or *the King of the Broken Crown*. In this proposed biography, he is a descendant of Aen from a branch that was not initially expected to inherit the throne. His legitimacy is contested, but a surviving council of nobles, soldiers, and refugees crowns him outside the occupied capital. He must win allies before he can hope to retake anything.

Edran's distinction is his ability to organize peoples who do not trust Aenor or one another. He accepts that Western rulers will fight under their **own banners**, that the North Lands remain allies rather than subjects, and that independent elves and Dark Lands peoples will not be placed under permanent human control. A proposed agreement, the **Charter of Free Banners**, gives all members a council seat and a common wartime command while protecting their separate sovereignties.

These concessions cost Edran support among hard-line Aenoran nobles. They make the Grand Alliance possible. His greatness is measured by the coalition he builds, the responsibilities he accepts, and the outcome of the war, even though his final decision will later damage that achievement.

### Why these alliances mattered

**Taryn** gives the West Lands a voice shaped by centuries of wars with Aenor. Her participation should not erase those wars. She fights Volgan because an unrestricted Volgan would be worse for her people, and she remembers Edran's promises after victory.

**Sirael** represents elves whose communities do not recognize a hereditary crown. Her support demonstrates that the Grand Alliance consists of independent societies, not one expanding empire.

**Rovek** prevents the Dark Lands from becoming a single evil faction. His soldiers know the terrain and the behavior of Volgan's allies, and they have a direct interest in defending their independence.

**Senra** and the island peoples provide maritime supply and transport. Their effort is essential to bringing distant forces together across Erdae's stormy and separated seas.

**Lorien** adds one of the most powerful armies in the war. He arrives as an ally, not an Aenoran subordinate. His relationship with Edran begins in respect and ends in serious disagreement.

## 6. The Battle of Dardan

**Established outcome:** The war's decisive confrontation is the **Battle of Dardan**. It kills **millions**, leaves a devastated wasteland, results in Volgan's defeat, and ends with other spirits stripping his power and banishing him to the **Void**. It is the pivotal battle of the war. Its precise position on the map, tactics, duration, participating named commanders, and banishment mechanism have not yet been established.

### Proposed place and strategic importance

**Dardan** could be placed in the southern approaches of the **Middle Lands**, at a broad plain adjoining an old inland road and a passage from the coast toward the Great Lake and the crownlands. The area should be close enough to Volgan's southern invasion routes to concentrate his main army and important enough that losing it would end any hope of retaking the central realm. This placement is **proposed** and requires future confirmation on the detailed map.

Dardan was formerly fertile, with settlements and roads that could supply a huge gathering of armies. Ancient stone formations below its hills, unnoticed by most mortals, could also make it suitable for a binding attempted by Great Spirits. Neither the landscape features nor the magical properties are canon yet.

### Proposed course of the battle: the Thirteen Days of Dardan

**Days 1–3: The lines close.** Volgan brings his central host against the Grand Alliance. Orc and goblin legions attack its forward positions, eastern and southern cavalry seek the open flanks, and corrupted elves break conventional defensive spells. Edran holds the mixed center. Taryn's Western forces defend one approach while Rovek's Dark Lands veterans prevent Volgan's scouts from finding an unguarded passage.

**Days 4–6: The crossings.** Dark dragons set supply depots and temporary defenses aflame. Brunek's dwarven engineers and giants from Hiloron defend the crossings and build replacement passages under fire. Senra's ships deliver food, weapons, and wounded men where inland roads cannot cope with the size of the armies. Thousands die merely keeping the coalition in position long enough to fight.

**Days 7–9: The earth opens.** Volgan releases the Damned Spirits into the battle. Creatures and disembodied powers emerge from beneath the hills, bringing down sections of the defensive line. Maerul and other Great Spirits hold the breaches while the dwarves collapse infected tunnels. Some of those spirits remain buried or sealed beneath the later wasteland of Dardan. This stage explains why the place is feared long after the armies have departed.

**Days 10–11: The host of the Gala elves.** Lorien commits his army after the Alliance has taken its worst losses. His soldiers counter the corrupted elven formations and help contain the dragons. Their arrival restores a collapsing flank. Sirael's elves from the Magical Lands fight alongside them, even though their customs and histories differ. The combined intervention makes it possible for Edran to counterattack, but the coalition is still badly outnumbered in several sectors.

**Day 12: The binding.** Ithren and other allied Great Spirits begin a dangerous rite beneath the battlefield. Volgan cannot simply be banished by a single spell. He has spent an immense portion of his active strength holding together his armies, summoning the deep beings, and forcing his will into the contested territory. The allied spirits exploit that concentration of power. To weaken him further, the Alliance must break the formations and bonds protecting him while the rite is prepared. Volgan's allied Great Spirits attempt to stop it.

**Day 13: The fall of Volgan.** Edran launches the final attack to hold open the way for the allied spirits. Lorien, Sirael, Brunek, and the surviving commanders take heavy losses. Volgan's guard is separated from him, and Ithren's rite takes hold. Several Great Spirits combine their power to **strip Volgan of the strength he can exercise in Erdae and cast him into the Void**. His essential existence is not destroyed. The effort also damages those who perform it. Ithren is proposed to become bound to the sealed threshold at Dardan, while Maerul loses much of the ability to manifest. This preserves the established immortality of Great Spirits while giving their victory a serious cost.

Volgan's army disintegrates as a unified force. Some units flee, surrender, or fight to the end. Other forces remain organized under surviving commanders. **The battle kills millions** when the full armies, support hosts, supernatural destruction, and subsequent collapse are considered, though no precise casualty figure is canon. Cities and fields around Dardan are ruined and the area becomes a wasteland. The names of individual days and the described military sequence are proposals.

### Why victory requires everyone

Dardan should not be a victory granted by a god, a single artifact, or one overpowering warrior. Edran's coalition makes it possible to survive the engagement. The elves provide military power and specialized knowledge, dwarves and giants hold crucial positions, island peoples keep the armies supplied, independent peoples of the West and Dark Lands protect the flanks, and allied Great Spirits carry out the banishment once Volgan is vulnerable. Each element matters.

The [[Holy Seven]] and the supreme Creator do not intervene. [[Zol]] does not intervene either. Zol's northern realm was already desolate, and his local power was still too restricted to make him a practical participant in this war.

## 7. The failed conquest of the Dead Lands

Volgan's banishment was an immense victory, but **the Dead Lands were not destroyed or conquered**. The king of Aenor wanted to enter and secure them so that Volgan's dominion could never threaten Erdae again. The elven leaders opposed the idea. The Alliance pressed forward anyway.

**Elzon** had gathered and prepared the surviving forces of Volgan. The invasion met fierce resistance and was **driven back with heavy losses**. The conditions of the Dead Lands and the exhaustion of the Alliance made further conquest too costly. The attack was abandoned. Many elves died in the campaign, and the king's decision badly damaged relations between men and elves, particularly given how much their forces had already sacrificed at Dardan.

### Proposed account: Elzon's defense

Elzon has survived Dardan by keeping a reserve outside the battle's central collapse or by escaping with surviving formations. His kind of being has since been established: he is a **Great Spirit**, the **Great Necromancer**, and a servant of Volgan. The story only requires him to be a capable commander who can lead the remnants without Volgan.

Having expected the possibility of Volgan's defeat, Elzon had prepared strongholds and fallback positions along the approaches to the Dead Lands. He withdrew surviving troops to defensible coasts, passes, and fortresses. He destroyed usable supply stores near the frontier and exploited weather, heat, and difficult terrain. The Alliance advanced into territory it could not feed or occupy without extraordinary losses. The defending armies had fewer soldiers than before Dardan, but they no longer had to fight Volgan's enormous offensive campaign. They only had to hold their own ground.

Edran viewed the invasion as necessary to prevent a future return. Lorien judged it a mistake after the decisive threat had been removed. Sirael and other elven leaders warned that the remaining troops could not endure another major struggle. When Elzon's counterattack struck the overextended invasion force, those warnings were vindicated. Lorien's people paid for a decision they had opposed.

This becomes the **central stain on Edran's otherwise great reign**. His commitment to a permanent peace led him into an avoidable campaign. Survivors of the Gala elves and Magical Lands return home with conflicting memories: gratitude for victory over Volgan and bitterness about the lives lost afterward. The consequences reach far into the later ages.

## 8. The end of the age and its legacy

The war ends when the remaining members of the Grand Alliance abandon the invasion of the Dead Lands and return to their homes. **Year 1675** closes the First Age. The exact year of Dardan within the final period of the age remains undetermined. The following Second Age begins with rebuilding a kingdom, healing lands, restoring trade, and responding to the devastation of the war.

The victory has lasting consequences:

- **Aenor survives**, but its capital, institutions, population, and foreign relationships have been severely damaged. The great kingdom must be rebuilt, and Edran's concessions to allies cannot simply be forgotten.
- **Volgan survives in exile in the Void.** A later return is possible, but its date, means, and any conditions upon him remain unwritten. Do not have him continuously ruling on the battlefield during the Second Age without first establishing how he returned.
- **Elzon and the remaining Dead Lands armies survive.** The Dead Lands remain an independent threat and a possible source of future rulers, cults, and restoration movements.
- **Dardan remains devastated**, remembered as the place where the largest forces of the age destroyed one another and Great Spirits brought down Volgan. Its exact location and how long it stays a wasteland remain open.
- **Relations between the races deteriorate after the victory.** The disastrous Dead Lands expedition weakens the trust of the Gala elves and other elven peoples in Aenor's kings.
- **The West Lands grievance survives.** The older dispute over Aenoran settlement, claims, and independence remains unresolved. Cooperation against Volgan did not erase centuries of conflict.
- **Eindil's extraordinary claim remains dormant.** When he emerges in the Fourth Age, his identity as son of Ein and grandson of Aen is a stronger blood claim than later descendants can make. He is also a living connection to a time most of the Fourth Age considers myth.

The second greatest king after Aen can therefore be remembered as a heroic victor who also made one of the most costly political decisions of the era. Different peoples preserve different accounts of him. Aenoran records praise the savior of the crown. Gala and Magical Lands chronicles remember his unnecessary pursuit into the Dead Lands. Dark Lands and Western chronicles may emphasize the price Edran promised for their loyalty.

## 07 - History/The Second Age of Erdae.md

# The Second Age of Erdae

*The Age of Long Grief, the Return of Volgan, and the Rise of the Order of Elzon*

**Length:** **2,133 years, established**, and the longest of Erdae's numbered ages. The Second Age follows the [[The First Age of Erdae|First Age]], which lasted 1,675 years. It begins after the defeat of [[Volgan]] at [[Battle of Dardan|Dardan]] and [[Elzon]]'s repulse of the [[Grand Alliance]] from the [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]]. It ends in **Second Age year 2133**, when **Elzon takes Aenor and establishes his order**, beginning the [[Ages of Erdae|Third Age]]. The Fourth Age follows later, when [[Eindil]] awakens. Eindil's **total age is at most approximately 5,000 years**. The old 100,000-year estimate is superseded.

> **Read before treating new detail as canon.** The owner's Second Age account on 17 October 2026 establishes the major figures, alliances, supernatural developments, general sequence, final outcome, and relative length of the age. The specific battles, town names, plague names, ceremonies, characters created for this account, technical rules of necromancy, and motivations developed below are **proposed expansions**. In the story passages, invented details are presented as a cohesive draft, not as separately confirmed canon. See [[Canon Policy]] and [[Second Age Canon Register]].

## I. Established outline

- The Second Age is remembered for **long grief and rebuilding**, yet it is also called the **Dark Age** because wars, plagues, predation, corruption, and supernatural disasters persist for generations. It is the longest age.
- Aenor retains a **great king**, even while the kingdom is exhausted after Dardan. This king is **Edran**, who formed the Grand Alliance. He **dies from disease** during the Second Age. His earlier costly attack on the Dead Lands causes the elves to refuse military aid when Elzon later invades Aenor.
- Many **orcs and goblins continue fighting for Volgan** even after his banishment. The independent [[Erdae and Its Regions|Dark Lands]] return to turmoil.
- [[Elzon]] is an **immortal Great Spirit, Volgan's servant, and the Great Necromancer**. He survives the First Age and deliberately impedes the recovery of Aenor with raids, alliances, and increasingly extensive networks.
- [[Volgan]] is genuinely freed from the Void by Elzon. His **soul or spiritual presence is returned to Erdae**, but his previously stripped power needs to be recovered. Volgan grants Elzon greater autonomy.
- [[Zol]], an Old God whose presence in Erdae is constrained by the [[The Fading|Fading]] and local magic, is first **perceptibly active** in this historical account when Elzon borrows some of his **ancient magic**. This does not establish Zol as Elzon's willing patron or an ally in the war.
- [[Agon]] and other demons are active in the [[Nether]]. More demons emerge through portals and raid men and Aenor. The **King of Demons** resides in the Nether, which lies in the southern Dead Lands. Agon's relationship to that king is not settled.
- [[Afrit]] is a separate power. In the First Age he **secretly organized demons and other forces against Volgan**, contributing significantly to Volgan's defeat, but his involvement went unrecognized. During the Second Age, he exploits the devastation to gain worship, influence, and power. **His ascension is a defining development near the end of the age**, but he does not take Aenor from Elzon, nor does this settle his later Fourth Age story.
- Elzon eventually instigates a new **war coming from within and from many directions**, turning men, elves, and dwarves against each other and corrupting survivors. He personally rises in power and animates the dead, so Aenor's battlefield losses also enlarge his forces.
- **Elzon and Volgan win.** Elzon takes Aenor and creates his order, ending the Second Age. What the regime will be called officially and how it rules belong to the development of the Third Age.
- The [[Abyss]] is located within the Dead Lands. [[Roka]] and his **Necklace of the Abyss** belong to it. Afrit takes and uses this necklace in the **Fourth Age**. It is not established as a tool of the Second Age war.

## New canonical refinements: the long but creative Second Age

The Second Age **ended in year 2133**, making it longer than the First Age. Its duration is explained by the slow recovery from centuries of war, repeated famines and plagues, and the painstaking growth of Elzon's threat. Yet it was not an uninterrupted collapse. Human communities developed better agriculture, famine prevention, plague treatment, military organization, fortifications, and other useful techniques. These innovations become crucial in the [[The Third Age of Erdae|Third Age]] and the long peace of the Fourth.

**[[King Edran]]** rules the early rebuilding years and eventually **dies of disease**. Because Edran ordered the disastrous post-Dardan invasion of the Dead Lands against elven advice, **the elven kingdoms decline to assist Aenor when Elzon later invades**. Independent elven communities defend their own Magical Lands, a different action from renewing an alliance to save the human crown. This supersedes earlier draft passages suggesting elven fighting companies joined Aenor's last defense.

Elzon conquers Aenor at the **end of 2A 2133**. His rule, loss of sustaining power, and eventual permanent death belong to the [[The Third Age of Erdae|Third Age]], with [[Afrit]] publicly recognized as a New God after its great liberation.

## II. The age that followed victory

The last armies returned from the Dead Lands with little to celebrate. They had broken Volgan at Dardan but failed to destroy his kingdom. Villages throughout Aenor had lost entire generations, fields lay untended, roads had become burial grounds, and refugees crowded into cities whose stores could barely feed them. The dead could not all be named or buried. Many survivors no longer knew whether relatives in distant provinces were alive.

The king of Aenor inherited a kingdom that was still worth saving. He began restoring granaries, bridges, mills, defensive walls, and the old roads from the Middle Lands to the coast. His first laws tried to return soldiers to the land and prevent powerful nobles from taking abandoned farms for themselves.

**Proposed detail: the Registers of the Lost.** The court sent scribes into ruined provinces to record deaths, recover the names of missing people, and settle inheritance disputes. The effort gave surviving families proof that their loved ones had existed. Centuries later, those lists would carry a second, terrible significance, because the necromancers of Elzon learned to use the names and places of the dead as a means of finding remains and graves. The registers did not themselves create necromancy. They became valuable intelligence in the hands of those who already possessed it.

The Grand Alliance did not immediately vanish. Its veterans maintained friendships and some rulers honored their wartime promises. But the failed invasion of the Dead Lands had soured the partnership. The [[Lorien|Gala elves]] mourned their heavy losses. Elves in the [[Erdae and Its Regions|Magical Lands]] no longer trusted every Aenoran request for soldiers. The West Lands still remembered Aenor's attempted conquests and the eastern and northern rulers had their own frontiers to defend. There was no longer a single army that the king could summon by decree.

This loss of unity was the first gift left to Elzon by the Alliance's victory.

### Proposed episode: the Council of the Empty Chairs

Representatives of the former Alliance gather to consider a permanent system for guarding the Dead Lands. Seats remain vacant for commanders who died at Dardan or on the failed campaign. The human king wants a common border army. Some elven envoys will offer scouts and healers, but refuse another invasion. Western rulers insist that Aenor first abandon disputed territorial claims. No lasting settlement emerges.

The council's failure should be a political event, not a declaration that every elf hates every man. Aid, trade, and friendships continue. The alliance simply becomes incapable of acting on the scale required when a new threat emerges.

## III. Elzon, the enemy who remained

[[Elzon]] had defended the Dead Lands when Volgan himself was gone. As a [[Spirits and Their Ranks|Great Spirit]], he could think in centuries, cultivate allies across the lifetime of whole dynasties, and rebuild after defeats that would destroy a mortal commander's career. As the **Great Necromancer**, his ability to command the dead would eventually change the character of warfare on Erdae.

At first, he does not march openly on Aenor. He is too strong to be an ordinary raider, but his forces are too scattered to repeat Volgan's first invasion. Instead, he builds what later historians might call a second kingdom without clear borders. Its roads are smugglers' routes, its forts are concealed shrines and cavern strongholds, and its servants work inside foreign courts.

### Proposed development: the Ash Networks

A loose confederation of orc warbands, goblin clans, surviving dark captains, spies, thieves, necromancers, human collaborators, and scattered demonic agents gathers around Elzon. Their allegiance varies. Some genuinely worship Volgan, some fear Elzon, some want land or wealth, and some have lost any place in the peacetime world.

Elzon rarely orders the same kind of operation twice. One year several frontier bridges are burned. In another year a port governor disappears and food shipments are diverted. Elsewhere a failed local uprising drives refugees into lands that cannot feed them. The attacks are small by the standards of Dardan, but they deny Aenor the stability necessary to rebuild.

The most dangerous of these operations is not a battle. It is the destruction of trust. Aenoran armies begin treating suspected collaborators as enemies. Retaliation helps Elzon's recruiters. Provincial nobles raise private garrisons and demand powers once reserved for the crown. Whole districts become too expensive to administer.

### The Dark Lands fragment again

The Dark Lands were never one nation. Independent rulers, monster peoples, corrupted courts, and rival warlords struggle for power. Some support Elzon while others resist him because they remember Volgan's attempts to dominate them. Orcs and goblins do not serve one master by nature. Their varying loyalties make the wars more complicated than a simple contest between two united races.

**Proposed episode: the War of the Black Roads.** Elzon's followers attempt to control the routes through the Dark Lands. An independent confederation opposes them. Aenor briefly supports the confederation, only to discover that its leaders demand concessions the king cannot accept. This could later explain why parts of the Dark Lands remain divided between hostile, neutral, and independent powers.

## IV. The years of sickness and open gates

The Second Age's darkness is not limited to armies. Disease repeatedly spreads through crowded cities, displaced camps, destroyed farmland, and trade routes. Some plagues arise through ordinary suffering and poor conditions. Others are supernatural, connected to sorcery, corrupted places, or creatures emerging from breached realms. Their exact origins are difficult for scholars within the world to distinguish.

**Proposed plague examples:**

1. **The Grey Breath:** a terrible sickness spreading through overcrowded winter settlements after failed harvests. Some rulers blame demons, but no proof survives that a supernatural force created it.
2. **The Lantern Blight:** a proposed magical affliction near unstable gates, where sleep becomes disturbed, fires burn strangely, and the dead seem present to their families. Its manifestations are fictional magic, not a medical illness.
3. **The Grave Fever:** the name people give to several outbreaks associated with mass graves and battlefields. Elzon's agents occasionally exploit these outbreaks without necessarily creating them.

The suffering leaves fertile ground for new religious movements, desperate bargains, and false prophets. The King's servants attempt to maintain traditional laws while local governors increasingly make bargains with powers they barely understand.

### The Nether and Agon

The [[Nether]] lies in the southern [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]]. It is a demonic domain containing the seat of the **King of Demons**, whose personal name and relationship to Volgan are not yet defined. [[Agon]] is one of the known demons active during the Second Age. His rank and allegiance are open.

Portals open across parts of Erdae. Some are established deliberately by cults or powerful demons. Others can be proposed as the result of damaged magical boundaries remaining after earlier wars. Demons strike settlements, steal resources or captives, bargain with frightened rulers, and contest one another's influence.

It is important that these attacks are not all directed by Elzon. Nether demons have interests of their own. Some cooperate with him, some may fight against him, and others treat Aenor's weakness as an opportunity unrelated to Volgan's return. Agon can be developed as a major figure within these conflicts, without prematurely making him the King of Demons.

**Proposed episode: the Burning Gate of Nezer.** A portal is opened in a contested border stronghold. The local human garrison, an elven relief company, and a band of independent dark-country soldiers fight together to close it. Their victory is later forgotten by the royal historians because the same three groups resume their disputes within a generation. The event illustrates that cooperation remains possible despite the political breakdown.

## V. The prophecies of the return

Rumors of Volgan's survival spread long before most men know that Elzon intends to bring him back. Priests disagree about the Void. Veterans insist they felt Volgan's presence after Dardan. Prophets speak of a darkness that has not finished its work.

Some warnings are founded on genuine supernatural disturbances. Others are fear, inventions, or deliberate propaganda spread by Elzon's agents. The fact that Volgan later returns should not make every prophecy truthful.

**Proposed surviving prophecy:**

> The king cast out shall return without a crown. His servant shall open what the victors closed, and the graves of men shall become his gates.

The words are intentionally ambiguous. They might have been recorded long before the return, distorted through translation, or composed after the events by chroniclers. Different religions can dispute their authenticity.

### Proposed episode: the Watchers of Dardan

A small order of mortals and spirits guards the ruined battlefield at Dardan, where the Great Spirits once performed Volgan's banishment. The Watchers discover that signs used in the original binding have begun to fail. A few surviving allied spirits recognize the danger, but there is too little agreement among the kingdoms to organize a new alliance.

This proposal can link with [[First Age Proposed Characters|Ithren of the Threshold]], an earlier proposed Great Spirit bound by the rite at Dardan. If adopted, Ithren warns that the binding can be attacked at its edges, but cannot leave the place where it was made. His fate remains optional.

## VI. Elzon reaches the Void

**Established outcome:** Elzon truly frees Volgan from the Void and brings his soul or spiritual presence back to Erdae. Volgan's former power has not simply returned in full, and Elzon receives wider autonomy as a result.

**Proposed method:** Elzon cannot force the Void open through raw power. The alliance at Dardan sealed the path between Volgan and Erdae. Over many generations he studies the scar left by that banishment. He searches for survivors who witnessed it, corrupted relics touched by the binding, and dormant passages beneath the Dead Lands. He destroys several incomplete attempts before finding a means of reaching his master.

The return requires three distinct elements:

1. **A trace of Volgan.** Elzon preserves an enduring mark of his master's spirit left in the Dead Lands. The nature of this mark is undecided. It might be a name that cannot be spoken, an object touched before Dardan, or a surviving fragment of Volgan's power.
2. **A weakened threshold.** Elzon's servants undermine the ancient banishment by exploiting fractures in the rites that originally closed it. This is a breach toward the **Void**, not an ordinary gate from the **Nether** and not the **Abyss**.
3. **Ancient death-magic.** Elzon draws on magic associated with [[Zol]], powerful enough to reach across a boundary that ordinary sorcery cannot cross. How he gains access to this power, and whether it costs Zol anything, are open questions.

Elzon accomplishes the return at enormous cost. In the proposed telling, several of his oldest necromantic strongholds are consumed, allied commanders are sacrificed or destroyed, and Elzon loses the power to repeat the same ritual easily. The rite is performed in secrecy because every rival power, including some demons, would seek to control its result.

The result is a **return of presence, not immediate recovery of strength**. Volgan is no longer imprisoned in the Void. His mind and will can act in Erdae again, and he can slowly recover influence, but the damage caused at Dardan remains meaningful. He cannot immediately rebuild the host he lost.

Volgan grants Elzon greater autonomy. In practical terms, Elzon is no longer merely the keeper of the Dead Lands during his master's absence. He may create his own networks, command campaigns, negotiate with spirits, and hold conquered realms under his own order while remaining loyal to Volgan. The Great Necromancer becomes the principal architect of the next war.

## VII. The first stirring of Zol

[[Zol]] arrived in Erdae long before Aen or Volgan. He is the only known [[Old Gods|Old God]] present there and the God of Death. He is still immensely powerful, grows stronger very slowly each day, and is hampered by both the [[The Fading|Fading]] and the special nature of magic in Erdae. He cannot simply take a body and conquer a kingdom. His servants, including Great Spirits, Lesser Spirits, and some New Gods, help sustain his presence.

The Second Age is when his presence becomes noticeably active to others through Elzon's use of **old magic**. This does not require Zol to have awakened from unconsciousness or to have lost all power earlier. It refers to Erdae's inhabitants encountering a sign of his influence on a new scale.

**Proposed manifestation:** In the far north, there are nights when sounds stop around ancient graves and the sea surrounding Zol's Mistake lies unnaturally still. In distant lands, necromancers sense a presence behind their ordinary rites, and a few dreamers describe a face-like absence watching from beyond the living world. No army sees Zol standing upon a battlefield.

Elzon may have stolen, bargained for, copied, or been granted access to fragments of Zol's ancient workings. None of those explanations has been chosen. His use of old death-magic should **not** automatically mean he serves Zol, that Zol supports Volgan, or that Zol wants the conquest of Aenor. Such a pact would need an explicit decision.

There is an important cosmic distinction. **Elzon controls and animates the dead through sorcery**. **Zol is the Old God of Death itself**. Elzon's successful necromancy does not place him on the same plane as Zol.

## VIII. Afrit's patient ascent

[[Afrit]] used the First Age war to destroy a rival far stronger than himself. He secretly encouraged demons, spirits, and other beings to oppose Volgan, and their contribution to the Grand Alliance's victory went unrecognized. Afrit did not act from loyalty to Aenor. He expected an exhausted kingdom to be easier to conquer later.

The Second Age seems to offer him everything he needs. Survivors fear the Nether, noble houses quarrel, priests cannot prevent disease, and people who have lost their families are willing to bargain with powers promising safety or restored fortunes.

Afrit's methods differ from Elzon's. He wins followers through cults, contracts, worship, corruption, and favors. He does not need to occupy every village with an army. Instead he creates people and institutions that rely on him.

**Proposed development: the Houses of Refuge.** In ruined towns, Afrit's hidden followers provide grain, medicine, shelter, and protection from wandering monsters. Many recipients never learn the source of the gifts. The first obligations seem harmless, but each promise creates influence that can be transferred from a family to a guild, temple, or local ruler. More ambitious leaders willingly take larger bargains in return for soldiers or wealth. This develops into a possible origin story for the [[Afrit|Worshipdom]], whose precise geography is still undecided.

Afrit remains an independent schemer. His earlier secret opposition to Volgan does not oblige him to work with Elzon, and Elzon's success threatens Afrit's plans for Aenor. They may obstruct each other or temporarily exploit the same events, but they are not a united command.

### The ascension near the end of the Second Age

**Established:** Afrit's ascension is a defining event near the end of the Second Age.

**Proposed interpretation:** The ascension is the point at which the scattered cults and bargains of Afrit become a durable supernatural power that can act openly across borders. In a ceremony called *the Night of a Thousand Oaths*, priests and rulers bound by separate compacts discover that their obligations have formed a single network. Afrit is raised to a vastly greater degree of independent influence, capable of challenging the power arrangements that emerge after Aenor falls.

This does **not** establish that Afrit becomes a New God. His formal rank remains undecided. It also does **not** give him Roka's Necklace of the Abyss or Aenor's royal crown. Those belong to his unfinished Fourth Age story. The ascension can occur just before Elzon's final victory, at the same time as the conquest, or during the immediate transition between ages. Its exact date is open.

Afrit may have expected to inherit a weak human throne. Instead, he watches Elzon destroy the kingdom before he can take it. This gives their future rivalry a natural political cause.

## IX. The war from within

By the time Elzon openly launches his final campaigns, Aenor has already been fighting for generations. Several provinces are ruled by exhausted hereditary houses. Some regions obey the crown nominally but no longer supply its army. The West Lands are unwilling to accept a new Aenoran military supremacy. Old alliances survive through individual rulers, not dependable institutions.

Elzon's second great war differs from Volgan's earlier invasion. It begins almost everywhere at once, and many of its participants do not recognize that their struggles are connected.

### Proposed sequence of the great unraveling

**The Thousand Small Wars.** Elzon's agents supply rival noble houses, monster leaders, and rebellious governors. Border attacks draw the remaining royal armies away from major roads and cities. Opportunistic rulers use the confusion to seize land.

**The Broken Oaths.** Corrupted leaders and forged messages create confrontations between men, elves, and dwarves. Some disputes begin with genuine grievances left from the First Age. Others are created by sorcery or deliberate fraud. Several alliances collapse without either side fully understanding why.

**The Opening of the Passes.** Orc and goblin armies pass through regions previously thought secure. Some descend from the Dark Lands and some rise from deep underground. The Damned Spirits released in the First Age are not necessarily under one command. Those who serve Elzon spread terror, while others make the roads equally dangerous for his own soldiers.

**The March of the Unburied.** Elzon finally appears in full power, no longer hiding his supernatural command. He raises the dead from battlefields, cemeteries, abandoned forts, and roadsides. His armies now include warriors whom Aenor's soldiers may recognize as dead relatives or comrades.

The true strength of this campaign is cumulative. Every defeat robs Aenor of its soldiers and veterans. A portion of those same losses becomes available to Elzon.

### Proposed limits of necromancy

If Elzon could raise every dead body instantly without restrictions, Aenor would have little meaningful opportunity to resist. To preserve tension and strategy, I suggest that necromancy requires **recoverable remains, a magical link, and enough sustained power to maintain control**.

The weakest dead may move only near a necromantic source. More powerful revenants require a remembered name, binding mark, or preserved fragment of their former will. Elzon's strongest commanders can act independently, but each demands more sorcery. Different cultures develop defenses: guarding burial places, destroying forbidden inscriptions, rescuing the fallen before an enemy can seize the field, and breaking the shrines that sustain undead hosts. Destruction of remains might prevent one method of reanimation, although no universal religious practice should be declared infallible.

These are suggestions, not permanent laws of ZIN magic. Their purpose is to make victories, rescues, and tragic failures possible in the story and any later game adaptation.

## X. The fall of Aenor

Elzon's campaign culminates in the conquest of Aenor. The end should not come because the kingdom loses one enormous battle. Its government, military, food supply, and allies have been gradually worn down until the final assault reveals how little of the old state remains.

### Proposed final campaign: the War of the Hollow Crown

**First turning point: the famine marches.** Two important grain-growing provinces are cut off by raids and local revolt. Refugees move toward the royal heartland. The king must choose between reinforcing the frontier and feeding the capital. Elzon attacks the columns of supplies instead of wasting his forces on the heaviest walls.

**Second turning point: the loss of the burial roads.** Necromancers take control of the roads connecting old battlefields to the great cities. Night patrols find fresh graves disturbed. The crown discovers that an army it believed destroyed has reappeared farther inland, reinforced by the dead of recent skirmishes.

**Third turning point: the divided relief.** The North Lands send help, but their force cannot arrive in time or in sufficient strength. The elven kingdoms refuse to send military aid to Aenor, remembering Edran's disastrous attempt to take the Dead Lands. Elven forces concentrate on protecting their own territory. Individual healers may still serve refugees, but there is no elven expedition to rescue the human crown. Western kingdoms either refuse the royal summons or fight separate campaigns at sea. These choices need not make any of them cowardly. They have obligations and enemies of their own.

**Fourth turning point: the last field army.** Aenor's royal host meets Elzon's advancing undead near the approaches to the capital. The battle begins in the normal way. Fallen soldiers reappear among the enemy, and the defenders' commanders realize that holding the field no longer prevents its loss. Several regiments refuse to abandon wounded comrades, only to be surrounded.

**Fifth turning point: the capital falls.** Elzon enters the royal city after its defense has collapsed. The historical name of the First and Second Age capital has not been fixed, even though the Fourth Age political table uses **Parim**. The conquest therefore should be recorded as *the Fall of Aenor* until the earlier capital is established. The reigning king's identity and fate are also open.

### Proposed survivor: the Keeper of the Last Seal

A royal archivist, tentatively named **Arelon**, carries fragments of Aenor's oldest genealogies and laws out of the fallen city. He does not rescue all the royal treasures. He saves the records that identify the descendants of Aen and the rights of the crown. These could later become politically significant when [[Eindil]], direct grandson of Aen, returns in the Fourth Age. The name, existence, and survival of Arelon's archive remain proposals.

### The order of the conqueror

Elzon establishes **his order** over the conquered kingdom. Its official institutions have not been fixed. A proposed governing structure would include undead garrisons, necromantic officers, surviving mortal administrators who submit, and puppet rulers required to furnish labor, food, information, and bodies. Elzon acts with the autonomy Volgan granted him.

**Proposed title: the Pale Regency.** This would be a later historian's name for the occupation, emphasizing that Elzon rules in the name of a master who has returned but has not recovered his former greatness. The owner's confirmed designation is simply **Elzon's Order**, and the optional title is not canon.

Not every subject is killed. Elzon benefits from retaining farms, roads, craftsmen, and terrified but living officials. The conquest is a political and supernatural takeover. Pockets of human resistance, independent elves, dwarves, and other peoples remain, leaving material for the Third Age.

## XI. The end of the Second Age

The old capital is lost, Elzon's Order replaces the crown, and the spirit of Volgan has returned to Erdae. Afrit has risen as an independent power, even though the throne he coveted has passed into the Great Necromancer's hands. The Nether remains a source of raids and rival demonic interests. Zol's presence is now known to have been felt, but the nature of his involvement remains uncertain.

Aenor's collapse marks the end of the **Second Age** and the start of the **Third Age**.

The historical name *Age of Rebuilding* describes what the survivors intended to accomplish. The names *Age of Long Grief* and *Dark Age* describe what later generations remember. All three can be used in different chronicle traditions.

The point of the Second Age is that the world loses over many generations. Even good rulers and brave people cannot reverse the combined effects of destruction, political mistrust, disease, opportunistic gods and spirits, and Elzon's patience. This allows the eventual Fourth Age restoration to carry the weight of an older broken history.

> **Duration:** At least 1,200 years, established. A detailed working calendar of **1,260 years** is proposed. It begins when [[Elzon]] conquers Aenor in Second Age year 2133 and ends when he dies permanently, allowing human monarchy to return.

[[Volgan]] remains in the [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]], too weak to travel beyond them, but instructs his servant Elzon, a Great Spirit and the Great Necromancer. He tries to alter the environment of occupied territory, which might eventually make Aenor another Dead Lands. The elves, Varner, and stubborn mortal resistance obstruct this goal. His decline is prolonged rather than immediate. He maintains dominion for centuries with changing armies and governments.

[[Afrit]] accumulates worship, allies and institutions throughout the occupation. At the end of the age he genuinely helps save people from the catastrophe accompanying Elzon's death. The Deliverer, publicly called **Aurevan** in the new proposed naming scheme, is proclaimed and worshipped as a New God. He does not take the human throne yet. ## Why the long rule holds together

An army of fading corpses could not simply remain at full size for twelve centuries. Institutions and defensive strategies evolve on both sides.

Elzon's fall follows from his inability to transform Aenor's magical environment before his defenses collapse. Varner weakens the curse at the end, while Afrit prepares to inherit the population's gratitude and political dependence. During [[The Fourth Age of Erdae|the Fourth Age]], Varner and Golbran survive for centuries longer and finally die during the last war involving Zol, Volgan, and Afrit.

## 07 - History/The Fourth Age of Erdae.md

# The Fourth Age of Erdae

*The Long Peace, the Kingdom Behind the Throne, the Journey of Eindil, and the War of the Last Crowns*

> **Status:** The core history in this chronicle comes from the world creator's account of 17 October 2026 and is established ZIN lore. Names, dates, dialogue, subsidiary characters, precise magical mechanisms, and military explanations devised to connect that account are **proposed expansions** until approved. The reference map depicts the Fourth Age, but the central ocean between the West Lands and Middle Lands should be wider.

## Prologue: the peace that followed Elzon

Elzon's death ended the Third Age. The Great Necromancer had ruled Aenor for many generations, though the elves of the Magical Lands had never submitted and men continued to resist him. The good human king who took the restored throne inherited ruined roads, cursed fields, divided provinces, and a people who could scarcely remember independence. **Harion the Restorer** is a proposed name for that king. He did not create the Fourth Age's prosperity alone. The cures, irrigation works, crop rotations, military engineering, public sanitation, and stores against famine developed during the Second Age gave later governments practical ways to rebuild. Varner's work against undead curses made former grave-fields inhabitable again.

For more than a thousand years, the Middle Kingdom grew. Cities spread around the Great Lake. Ports expanded. Teachers, physicians, and engineers found patronage, and schools and libraries became established in places once ravaged by plague. Trade strengthened with the North Lands, West Lands, islands, Magical Lands, and eastern states. Dangerous border regions remained, but the great invasions of orcs and goblins had ceased. Aenor became as formidable as it had ever been.

The peace was real. Its vulnerability lay in who gradually acquired control of the institutions that sustained it. The Deliverer, credited with saving Aenor during Elzon's fall, had retained a network of healers, priests, relief houses, patrons, informants, and supernatural servants. For much of the Fourth Age he was publicly known by a human and reverential name. **Aurevan**, or **Aurevan the Deliverer**, is a proposed public name. **Afrit** was the name he used in his hidden circle and in his increasingly demonic identity. The majority of worshippers never knew the latter name.

Aurevan was praised as a New God of Deliverance, Protection, and Renewal. His blessings could be real. He could heal, shield, grant wealth, and prevent disasters. Yet every sincere institution honoring him provided another channel for his influence. Priests educated princes. Charitable houses recorded families and movements. Royal councilors depended on his favor. Over centuries, kings increasingly made decisions that happened to align with his wishes. Some advisers understood what they were doing. Most considered it an honorable service to their country.

The change was difficult to see from one reign to the next. By the middle centuries of the Fourth Age, the god's representatives could help appoint ministers, decide which noble houses prospered, direct spies, and govern without signing a single royal decree. Aenor's kings wore the crown, but its effective ruler was often elsewhere. That is why the kingdom could remain genuinely prosperous while its independence was slowly lost.

## I. The hidden birthright of Afrit

The truth later revealed in Castle Yeldenne changes the interpretation of several ages of Erdae's history. **Afrit and Eindil were brothers, both sons of Ein and grandsons of Aen**. They belonged to the ancient royal line of the First Men, who were more naturally capable of magic and lived far longer than later men. Their exact birth dates are not established, but they are approximately the same age. Eindil's many years in supernatural sleep and Afrit's long magical survival explain why one awakens ignorant of history while the other has spent ages engineering it.

The brothers were not born as unequal orders of being. Afrit had once been a man of Aen's line. He desired the throne of men, and Ein refused to grant it to him. The full meaning of Ein's refusal, the precise law of inheritance, and the circumstances of the betrayal remain to be written. **Proposed explanation:** Ein named Eindil his heir because he trusted the younger brother's character, despite Afrit believing himself more deserving. Afrit betrayed Eindil and helped put him into the enchanted sleep that eventually ended in the Cold Lands. Ein repudiated Afrit, and Afrit turned to corruption, demonic pacts, worship, and artifacts to obtain by power what his father had denied him.

This makes Afrit's role in the First Age more significant. His secret aid against Volgan at Dardan could have been part of a very long design to remove rivals and weaken the kingdom that he intended to inherit. Centuries later, he helped undo Elzon's reign and was acclaimed a god. His public ascent gave him what neither birth nor conquest had secured: willing obedience across the human kingdoms.

Afrit's demonic form is genuine, but it is an acquired state, not proof that he was created as a demon. His extraordinarily long life depends on accumulated supernatural power and arrangements not yet fully understood. Stripped of sustaining magic and enchanted objects, he is still a living, mortal descendant of the First Men who can bleed, suffer, and die. Whether his public position as a New God corresponds to true divine rank remains an open cosmological question. Public worship does not by itself make him one of the New Gods created by the Holy Seven.

## II. Roka's necklace and the gathering of powers

At some point in the Fourth Age, Afrit sought the greatest surviving artifacts of Erdae. Above all he coveted the **Necklace of the Abyss**, held in the depths of the Dead Lands by **Roka**, a huge being of the rhinoceros-headed people of the Abyss. The true history of that people and the necklace's creation remain unknown.

Rather than enter the Abyss himself, Afrit sent a master thief. **Sevrin the Unending** is a proposed name. Afrit promised the thief eternal life if he returned with the necklace. The thief crossed hostile lands, reached Roka's domain, obtained the artifact by means not yet established, and brought it back. Afrit fulfilled the bargain by using the necklace's magic to grant him unending life. **Proposed constraint:** the thief ceased to age, but remained capable of pain, capture, and injury. Afrit gave him a gift that also left him dependent upon Afrit's continuing control of the artifact.

The necklace greatly increased the amount of power Afrit could draw and direct. It also strengthened him outside Aenor, though it was at its greatest potency where centuries of worship and magical preparation had made the Middle Kingdom his principal seat. He amassed additional objects collected through trade, theft, excavation, and bargains with servants across Erdae. Proposed artifacts include the **Ring of Doors**, which helps him cross between places and reach his private refuge, the **Crown of Witnesses**, which magnifies his public authority through prepared oaths and institutions, and the **Bracelet of the First Seal**, which preserves part of his personal shield. The names and exact effects of these lesser artifacts remain proposals.

Afrit's court developed two faces. Publicly he was the benevolent Aurevan. Privately he maintained a council of selected followers, spies, sorcerers, agents in foreign courts, and creatures who knew his true nature. Through that council he built ties in the East Lands and South Lands as a precaution against losing Aenor. The future invasion of the eastern host was therefore neither entirely spontaneous nor entirely under his control.

## III. The Golden King and the empty throne

The Golden King ascended the throne late in the age. He was greedy, extravagant, and deeply concerned with his own grandeur. He hoarded treasure, sold offices, and favored those who flattered him. Aenor continued to function because institutions created by earlier generations were strong, while those surrounding the throne quietly governed in his name. Among them were Aurevan's people.

The Golden King founded **the Lost Cause**, publicly intended to guard the kingdom against the return of dark powers. Its councils and alliances were real enough, but their founder used the institution for prestige, and his corruption weakened trust in it. Some of its officers later became genuine defenders of Aenor.

Afrit decided the time had come to stop ruling through human kings. He arranged the Golden King's death, concealing his part in the murder. His agents presented the succession as a crisis that no ordinary claimant could resolve. Temple authorities proclaimed Aurevan the one protector whose blessing had preserved the kingdom for centuries. Members of the royal council transferred the seals of government to him. Military commanders swore obedience, many sincerely believing they were preventing another civil war.

Aurevan accepted the crown and declared himself **King of Aenor, First of His Name, and New God of Men**, adding other titles gathered through his cult. Most of the capital celebrated. Much of the kingdom obeyed. There were dissenters, rival claimants, and scholars who questioned his genealogy, but Afrit's informants and guards quickly silenced those judged dangerous. It was possible to praise him voluntarily, serve him from fear, and still remember the freedom of earlier kings. These responses existed side by side.

In private the new king allowed his demonic form to emerge more frequently. The outward figure of the Deliverer remained familiar to the people. Only his inner council routinely called him Afrit.

## IV. The stranger in the Cold Lands

Far to the northwest, beyond most of Aenor's knowledge, ancient ice released a man who had slept through kingdoms and ages. He remembered almost nothing of his former life. His name was not initially clear even to himself.

Cold Lands tribes discovered him alive where no unprotected traveler could have survived. They fed him, sheltered him, and later entrusted him to western travelers moving south. The stranger had extraordinary strength, quickness, and endurance, but what distinguished him was his unwillingness to misuse them. Villagers remembered him carrying survivors from floodwaters, restraining raiders without slaughtering their captives, and settling disputes that established leaders had allowed to grow into blood feuds.

**Proposed western episodes:** At the **Bridge of the Three Oaths**, he stopped two rival lordships from burning each other's grain stores during a bad harvest. At **Girdon's Winter Ford**, he held a collapsing crossing long enough for refugees to escape. In **Gunwaith**, he submitted himself to the laws of a kingdom after defeating a noble's hired champions, earning respect because he did not demand exemption from judgment. These places, incidents, and names are proposals pending geography review.

People began giving him names, among them *the Man from the Ice* and *the King Without a House*. In one western confederation, several rulers chose him as a temporary war-king to negotiate a peace they could not maintain alone. He accepted to end the conflict and laid down the title once the peace was secured. That explains the tradition that he was a king in the West Lands without creating a permanent unified kingdom that contradicts the map's many western states.

He wanted to learn who he was. Stories and broken genealogies pointed toward the First Men and the old kingdom across the great central sea. He planned to sail to Aenor, but a violent sequence of storms and unfamiliar currents carried his ship far south. After wreck and hardship he reached the **Islands of the South** and lived among their inhabitants. He helped them settle disputes and survive dangers of their own, while hearing accounts of the long-lived elves who dwelt farther to the southwest. **Proposed island legend:** On one island, the fisherfolk refused to cross a reef at night because drowned voices were said to call ships onto the rocks. Eindil recovered children stranded there during a storm. The islanders later said the sea had gone silent when he carried them ashore. Farther south, a ruined shrine stood beneath the tide. Eindil found an inscription naming Aen that the island priests had repeated for generations without understanding it. Neither wonder identified him, but each made his search harder to abandon.

Among the islanders circulated a saying that, when the tales of men grew too old for any man to explain, they should be carried to Gala. The stranger eventually traveled to the **Lands of the Sun**.

## V. Lorien and the Mirror of First Rain

The **Gala elves** were among the few peoples whose rulers still remembered the time of Dardan. Their king **Lorien** had led them into the Grand Alliance against Volgan, and survived through the Second and Third Ages. His memories included the great victory, Edran's ruinous attack into the Dead Lands, the death of Edran's soldiers and elven allies, and the later refusal of the elven kingdoms to fight Elzon's war on behalf of Aenor. Lorien had not ceased caring about the world, but he distrusted the repeated desire of human rulers to turn a common victory into a permanent dominion.

He received the stranger and spoke with him for weeks. He told him of the War of Men, Aen's unnatural birth, the founding of Aenor, the long peace, westward conquest and bitter resistance, the ancient peoples before Aen, Dardan, and the death of Elzon. The stranger learned how small his own memories were against the age of the world. Lorien recognized signs of the First Men, but refused to identify him on strength alone.

**Proposed device:** Lorien led him to the **Mirror of First Rain**, a spring enclosed in an old elven courtyard. Its water does not show an infallible future. It reveals true traces of the past and a series of possible futures to those who have the courage to look without demanding certainty. During the vision Lorien saw the old royal line and recognized **Eindil, son of Ein, son of Aen**. Eindil saw a burning capital, armies from west and east, death moving from the north, Volgan's renewed strength, and himself carrying a shard of broken blue metal against a darkness almost beyond comprehension.

The vision gave him a name and a duty, but did not show him every choice he would need to make. Lorien warned him that prophecies can be used to frighten people into committing the very deeds they predict.

Lorien directed him north to **Castle Yeldenne in the Cold Lands**, where the guardian Horos kept **Glo**. The crystal contained a portion of the power of **Glernaen**, an actual New God of Power and Wisdom. Aenor could not be saved through raw strength alone. Eindil would need the means to resist both spirit magic and the old force gathering in Zol's Mistake.

Lorien provided an exceptional ship, armor, a fine sword, provisions, and a multinational crew of western friends, island navigators, and Gala elves. The crossing took months. They called at distant islands, exchanged supplies, and weathered cold seas. Lorien's ship was exceptionally fast and seaworthy, though it could not make its passengers safe from every enemy.

## VI. The road to Yeldenne

Afrit's hidden agents operated even in the Lands of the Sun. One reported that a man claiming descent from Aen had left Lorien with a ship and was headed toward Yeldenne. To Afrit the report was terrifying. His brother, whom he had believed lost, had returned to the same world whose crown he had spent ages seeking.

Afrit left Aenor in the hands of a trusted councilor, **Chancellor Merovan** being a proposed name. He arranged vessels to transport his own party north, kept other ships in reserve, and used his network of informants to establish where Eindil would land. His own party reached the approach to the castle first.

An ambush met Eindil's companions after they came ashore. Afrit's attackers struck as the travelers moved inland. The company fought bravely, but every one of Eindil's crew was killed. The vessel that had carried them remained beyond his reach, its safety unknown. Eindil survived by force of arms and escaped alone across miles of snowbound country. He had lost the people who had helped him travel across the world and had no knowledge that his enemy was already at the castle.

## VII. The hall of Horos

Castle Yeldenne stood cold and almost silent. In its hall an immense armored knight sat between stone pillars. His armor was yellow-green, his blade blue, and a green light shone from the gem at the center of his chest. The edges of his helm rose in dark, branching forms. His blue eyes remained visible in the shadow. This appearance follows the surviving drawings of Horos.

Afrit entered wearing the outward form of Aurevan, carrying the Necklace of the Abyss and his gathered artifacts. From the throne came a voice.

> **Horos:** "Who walks here?"
>
> **Aurevan:** "I am Aurevan, First of His Name, King of Aenor, Protector of Men, Deliverer of the Kingdom, and a New God before whom nations kneel."
>
> **Horos:** "Those are the names you have taken. I asked who walks here."
>
> **Aurevan:** "I have come for Glo."
>
> **Horos:** "Afrit, son of Ein. I know why your father denied you."

Horos knew that the visitor was Eindil's brother. He knew that the promise of a divine crown had never satisfied him and that the throne was not truly his by right. He warned that Glo had been entrusted to the worthy and could not be seized merely by occupying the castle. Afrit's usual power was less effective inside Yeldenne because Glernaen's old protections disrupted the channels maintained by his artifacts. Horos demanded that he depart.

Afrit refused. He attacked.

At first Horos seemed to command the encounter. He was larger, exceptionally skilled, and carried Glernaen's power in both his sword and living crystal. Afrit nevertheless had spent millennia preparing for struggles against beings stronger than himself. The Necklace of the Abyss amplified his magic. Wards splintered, the hall cracked, and Horos discovered that the intruder could inflict real injury. Horos eventually seized Afrit by the throat and raised him from the ground.

At that moment Eindil entered the hall. Afrit used the **Ring of Doors**, or another proposed escape artifact, to pass behind the great guardian. The movement cost him more power inside the castle than he had expected, but he drove a blade beneath Horos's helm and into the back of his neck. Horos fell. Eindil rushed forward and stabbed Afrit through the abdomen. For the first time in many years the false god bled visibly, his divine presentation broken by the castle's protections. Eindil moved to finish him, but Afrit escaped through a last opening into his private refuge.

Horos was dying. He recognized Eindil and revealed the concealed history of the two sons of Ein. Afrit had once betrayed his brother over the inheritance their father denied him. Horos had guarded Glo for ages and rejected every claimant who sought it for conquest. Eindil, who had given up a western crown and come to the castle after losing his companions, was the first whom the guardian would willingly entrust with it.

Glo was not an ornament on the throne. **It was the living crystal at the heart of Horos's own existence.** He opened his armored breast, took the green crystal from his chest, and placed it in Eindil's hands. In doing so he released Glo from its old bond to Castle Yeldenne. Horos disintegrated into dust. His final gift conveyed a portion of Glernaen's power to Eindil and the right to carry the guardian's blue sword.

The castle was left empty. Eindil departed with Glo, the sword, and knowledge that Aurevan, Aenor's adored king, was his own brother Afrit.

## VIII. The unheeded warnings

Afrit returned to his capital and recovered near the center of the worship, wards, and accumulated artifacts that sustained him. The wound from Yeldenne remained proof of his vulnerability, though he kept that knowledge from most of the court.

Eindil reached the Middle Lands and attempted to warn their people. Afrit's agents had already circulated claims that the stranger was a foreign impostor, a traitor, and a threat to the peace. The populace knew Aurevan as its divine guardian. Very few wished to believe that their protector had murdered their king and stolen their inheritance.

Eindil spoke of what the Mirror had shown and of dangers he could now recognize in reports from across the world: Volgan rebuilding his armies, death stirring in Zol's Mistake, western kingdoms planning a great crossing to end their ancient rivalry with Aenor, northern rulers withdrawing from their treaties, and powerful eastern coalitions preparing to invade the Middle Lands and Magical Lands. The North Lands were not one unanimous enemy. Nor were all eastern states united. Each region contained rulers with different motives, but enough forces were moving at once to threaten Aenor's survival.

Some men believed Eindil, especially among frontier troops, scholars of the First Men, and branches of the Lost Cause. **Alleria of Selen**, a proposed veteran ranger and renowned archer, investigated his story and secretly protected his movements. Most of the court denounced him. Eindil did not initially try to seize a crown by force. He wanted men to unite before the invasion began.

## IX. The War of the Last Crowns

The danger unfolded through several wars that overlapped rather than a single attack on one day.

**From the west**, fleets crossed the enlarged central ocean. Some western kings sought revenge for ancient Aenoran conquests. Others wanted to take the capital before a weakened kingdom could again threaten them. The western army landed, seized ports, and eventually occupied the capital, claiming the human throne.

**From the north**, rulers in the North Lands withdrew from Aenoran alliances, while forces associated with Zol moved from the black northern wasteland, through islands and straits toward the settled kingdoms. Zol's dead were different from Elzon's resurrected corpses. They were death-bound beings sustained by the Old God's direct magic. Ordinary weapons might break their shapes, but could not end their existence. Only potent magic could truly destroy them.

**From the south**, Volgan emerged from the Dead Lands, reconquered Mirolon, and marched north with an army greater than those seen since the first age. He commanded creatures, great spirits, demons, and vast war hosts. The forces had been accumulating for centuries while Volgan recovered from his earlier exile.

**From the east**, the eastern coalition crossed seas and mountain approaches to threaten the Magical Lands and the Middle Kingdom. Their commanders expected wealth, territory, revenge, or deliverance from their own rulers' crises. Some were secretly connected to Aurevan's long-established networks.

Afrit looked at these converging attacks and decided that Aenor could no longer serve his ambitions. He abandoned the kingdom he claimed to protect. His council and prepared agents helped him move east, where he exploited his influence over several rulers and took command of an eastern dominion. He intended to rule there if the Middle Kingdom fell. The title **King of the East** was proclaimed by Afrit and his allies, although the actual East Lands contained states that still resisted him.

Aenor was left without its crowned ruler. Eastern armies clashed with its defenders, who retreated toward the western-held capital. Western commanders accepted surrenders and expected to retain their conquest, but the dead pouring in from the north and Volgan's forces to the south made occupation impossible. Some eastern formations fought Zol's dead and Volgan's troops, suffered devastating losses, and retreated toward the East Lands. The western fleet prepared to evacuate. Some soldiers escaped, while others remained stranded beside the Aenorians they had just conquered.

As the living armies turned on one another, Zol's forces collided with Volgan's. The two ancient powers had not yet settled on common command. For a time each destroyed the other's servants while the mortal kingdoms were crushed between them. Their later temporary cooperation would be even worse.

## X. The return of the king without a kingdom

Eindil returned to the main battlefield riding a dragon accompanied by **Varner**, the Lesser Spirit wizard who had helped end Elzon's reign, and **Golbran**, Varner's small purple humanoid servant of unknown origin. **Proposed explanation:** the dragon had been entrusted to Eindil by elven allies of the Magical Lands, whose ancient relationship with dragons made such a pact possible. The beast was a free ally, not an animal brought under human domination.

Eindil leapt from its back and struck the earth with Horos's blue sword. Glo released a wave of Glernaen's power across the ground, breaking the magic sustaining an entire front of Zol's dead. Thousands fell in moments. Where ordinary soldiers had struck again and again without effect, the sword could end these beings decisively. Eindil cleared a path through the northern host and gave the armies of men time to regroup.

The witnesses knew him from rumor, warning, and the stories of the west. Aenoran survivors hailed him as king. Stranded western soldiers, northern companies, men of Selen, and even eastern troops whose commanders had abandoned them joined a temporary coalition. Their agreement rested on one immediate purpose: preventing Volgan from consuming the remaining free lands. Eindil accepted command without demanding that the western and northern kingdoms surrender their independence.

Volgan himself appeared at the southern front. A blast of his magic struck Eindil and knocked him to the ground. The Great Spirit looked across the ranks of western soldiers standing beside their former enemies and spoke so that the survivors could hear him.

> **Volgan:** "I have seen your kingdoms rise from the ashes of other kingdoms. I have waited through your victories and your oaths. You swear that men are united, and in a hundred years their sons come to me asking help against their brothers. Why should this time be different?"
>
> **Eindil:** "Because these men have chosen to stand together while there is still something left to save."
>
> **Volgan:** "Then I will bury them together."

The ensuing fight was no simple exchange of blows. Eindil shattered Volgan's advance guard and cut through enchantments that had ruined whole companies. The Great Spirit answered by turning the surrounding earth against him and striking through the protections of Glo. Eindil discovered how much of the power at Dardan had required an entire alliance of spirits. He could hold Volgan for a time, but could not defeat him alone. The Great Spirit cast him down, gravely wounded.

Varner stood against the darkness rolling down from Zol's armies. He lifted curses, opened escape routes, and preserved as many living souls as he could. The effort cost his life. Golbran exhausted almost all of his own magic sustaining Varner's last wards, and collapsed. Those who saw him thought him dead, but a fragment of his life remained. **This resolves the two sacrifices in the account:** Golbran spends nearly all his strength here and gives the last of his life only during the final struggle with Afrit.

## XI. The gathering of the New Gods

Eindil's spirit reached a divine realm, understood by him as Heaven. The New Gods gathered around him. **Glernaen's crystal Glo was the bridge that allowed this encounter.** The gods knew that a descendant of Aen had tried to unite the divided peoples of Erdae without claiming dominion over them.

In this proposed version of the encounter, Eindil could not see every god clearly. One appeared as a figure bearing a lamp that had never gone out, and spoke of the common people Eindil had protected on his travels. Another wore the likeness of rain over ploughed fields and remembered Aenor's long centuries of hunger. Others spoke of promises kept, wars prevented, and those whom kings had forgotten. Their individual names and domains remain unwritten. Last came **Glernaen**, the New God of Power and Wisdom, whose voice Eindil recognized in the living crystal.

> **Glernaen:** "Power has been offered to you more than once. When you might have ruled the West, you made peace and left its crown behind. When you learned whose blood you carried, you asked the people to prepare, not to kneel. Tell us now, son of Ein, what will you do if you rise?"
>
> **Eindil:** "I will return to them."
>
> **Glernaen:** "Then carry what we can give. You may yet fall, and we cannot promise to raise you again."

The New Gods could not remain in Erdae to fight the war themselves. Its magic and the limits between divine realms constrained their direct manifestation. Instead, each offered a measured part of their power through Glo. They restored Eindil to life, renewed his armor, and gave him the strength to face Volgan again. His eyes shone with lightning and the sword of Horos blazed with an intensity it had never held in the old guardian's hand.

The New Gods' intervention was extraordinary and costly. **Proposed rule:** they could make this one collective offering only because Glo already carried Glernaen's power and because Eindil freely accepted the burden. It was not a spell they could simply repeat whenever a favorite champion died. Their gift made him mighty, but not invulnerable or equal to an Old God.

## XII. The destruction of Volgan

Eindil returned to the battlefield and broke the southern host. He destroyed commanders and magical engines on which Volgan's army depended, then confronted the Great Spirit again. Volgan had united his surviving forces with Zol's in a temporary alliance against the restored champion and the remaining nations.

This time Eindil's strength exceeded what Volgan could withstand. He drove the blue sword through Volgan. The Great Spirit's visible body broke apart, releasing a violent burst of magic that shattered much of the command structure of his host. His servants screamed and fled. Some supernatural bonds sustaining the southern army collapsed. **Volgan's physical manifestation is destroyed.** Whether his underlying Great Spirit essence perishes completely is deliberately left open, since unlike Elzon his absolute death has not yet been explicitly established.

The victory did not end the battle. Zol himself came forth.

## XIII. The Old God beneath the black sky

Zol had been present in Erdae long before Aen or Volgan. The Fading had made the ancient Old God unable to take ordinary physical form. Erdae's local magic was hostile to his nature, and maintaining a presence there required the slow feeding of servants. His power had grown over ages, including through the spiritual consumption of Elzon, but remained difficult to exercise.

**Proposed explanation for this exceptional manifestation:** the immense death of the War of the Last Crowns, ancient routes of power prepared by Zol's servants, and the breaking of Volgan's summoned forces temporarily aligned enough of Erdae's magic for Zol to embody a fraction of himself. This cost him strength and could not last indefinitely. He appeared in a form that the mortal witnesses could perceive, not in the fullness of his unfaded cosmic existence.

Eindil charged. Zol stopped him with one finger, took Horos's sword, and crushed it into glittering shards. He seized Eindil by the throat and began drawing out the combined gifts of the New Gods. The power in Eindil's armor dimmed. With his remaining strength he caught a shard of the shattered blade and drove it into the body Zol had made.

The fragment still carried Glernaen's power through Glo. The wound did not kill Zol, but destabilized the form he had struggled to create. A storm swept across Erdae and the sky darkened. Zol struck back with terrible force. Eindil fell, gravely wounded, while Zol's dead overwhelmed the remaining defenders.

At last **Lorien** arrived with Gala elves and some of the great lords and spirits who had survived the long history since Dardan. They had not come to restore Aenoran empire. They came because an Old God's victory would destroy the freedom of the living, elves and men alike. The oldest warriors spent the remnants of their own power holding the black presence back. Several great spirits were destroyed or broken. Lorien challenged Zol directly and died in the struggle.

Their sacrifice forced the embodied Old God to expend more power than he could sustain. Zol withdrew into the darkness of his domain, leaving an enormous wasteland and a continent full of dead. He was wounded and constrained, not permanently destroyed. His remaining hosts lost coherence or withdrew from the immediate field. When surviving soldiers helped Eindil to his feet, there was no longer a serious doubt among the people present that he had earned the crown of Aenor.

## XIV. The second betrayal

But the fourth age had not ended. Afrit returned from the East Lands with a new eastern host. He had watched the destruction of Volgan and Zol's withdrawal, and he believed Eindil's wounds had finally created the opportunity he needed. He demanded that the exhausted kingdoms recognize him as ruler of both East and Middle Lands.

A further battle followed between Afrit's eastern coalition and the defenders who had just fought for Erdae. Afrit found Eindil weakened by the struggle against Zol. The two brothers confronted one another in the fighting near a high cliff. Afrit severed Eindil's arm, drove a blade through his body, and told him that he had hated him since their father's refusal. He cast him over the edge and believed him dead.

With Eindil gone, Afrit seized the capital once more. He proclaimed himself king over Aenor, the East Lands, and ultimately every people of Erdae. That proclamation was larger than the territory he actually controlled, but his surviving army and his court treated it as a universal claim. His old council resumed its work, and opposition was punished.

**Proposed explanation for Eindil's survival:** he fell into water below the cliff and was caught among rocks. Glo's remaining life-preserving magic slowed his death without healing his severed arm or terrible wounds. Alleria of Selen and surviving scouts found him and carried him into hiding. He survived through exceptional care, time, and the limited remaining strength of the crystal. Afrit had not searched the ravine because he had seen a mortal body fall from a height no man could ordinarily survive.

## XV. The palace of the false god

Eindil entered the royal palace in battered armor, missing an arm and bearing wounds that would have killed most men. Horos's blue sword had been destroyed by Zol, so he carried a simpler replacement weapon. He understood now that Afrit's strength depended on objects, accumulated worship, and the prepared places where his magic was strongest. He could not hope to overpower every defense directly.

Alleria of Selen had already reached the palace with allies from the remaining resistance. Her bow gave Eindil openings he could not make alone. Golbran, who had survived Varner's last stand in a greatly weakened condition, followed secretly through old service passages. He had recovered enough to use one final working, but not enough to survive it.

Afrit struck first with overwhelming force. Eindil survived by changing the battle from one of power to one of position, attention, and vulnerability. His knowledge of Horos's fight showed him how teleportation and other artifacts could be disrupted at close range.

**First**, Alleria shot an arrow into Afrit's eye at the instant he exposed himself to direct the defenses. His supernatural protection began healing it, but the blow broke his concentration. Eindil seized Afrit's hand, cut off a finger, and tore away the **Ring of Doors**, preventing the easiest form of his escape.

**Next**, Alleria and the surviving palace rebels destroyed the prepared seals that let the **Crown of Witnesses** draw strength from the chamber. Eindil damaged the **Bracelet of the First Seal** by forcing Afrit's own redirected spell against it. These names and mechanisms remain proposed. Each loss made Afrit more physically vulnerable, but he remained extraordinarily dangerous.

**Finally**, only the **Necklace of the Abyss** remained as his chief source of supernatural power. Realizing that he could lose, Afrit opened an emergency passage to his private dimension using the necklace itself. Eindil grabbed its chain at the instant of transit and was carried into that realm beside him. The realm magnified Afrit's connection to his last artifact. He battered Eindil to the floor and raised his weapon to pierce the wounded man's throat.

Golbran had followed through the passage by holding the same momentary magical opening. He climbed behind Afrit and used the final remnant of Varner's teachings to sever the necklace's binding, tearing it from the false god's neck. Without it, Afrit's accumulated demonic power and supernatural protection collapsed. The ancient brother of Eindil stood exposed as a mortal man. Eindil cut his throat.

Afrit bled and struggled for breath. The man who had served as the god of men, killed their king, and demanded dominion over all Erdae died without his miracles. There was no final teleportation and no sustaining artifact left to save him.

Eindil himself was dying. Golbran gave the last of his life and magic to close Eindil's most dangerous wounds. The small creature died beside the two brothers. Its true origin remained unknown. Eindil was alone.

He took the Necklace of the Abyss and put it around his own neck. The act was necessary, in this proposed version, to reopen the passage from Afrit's private refuge to Erdae. It did not mean that the necklace was harmless or that he had accepted Afrit's methods. He returned to the palace carrying evidence of the false king's fall and an artifact of unresolved danger.

## XVI. The king of men

Eindil was crowned King of Aenor. His claim came from his descent as son of Ein and grandson of Aen, but his subjects also knew what he had done. His coronation was attended by survivors whose banners had recently faced one another across battle lines. The ceremony did not erase their grievances. Among the witnesses were envoys, wounded soldiers and families searching for those who would never return. He had ended conflicts in the west, crossed the southern seas, won Glo through the sacrifice of Horos, resisted Volgan and Zol, and destroyed Afrit, the brother who had stolen his future.

He inherited a kingdom that had been occupied more than once in a single war, a capital full of ruins, an exhausted army, and foreign peoples who had alternately invaded and helped defend it. A number of western, northern, and eastern soldiers had fought beside him. Their participation did not make them his subjects. The old human divisions remained, and the surviving kingdoms would need treaties instead of forced submission.

Aenor now had a rightful king, but its victories had been costly. Varner, Horos, Golbran, and Lorien were dead. The New Gods had spent part of their power to restore their champion. The great magical blade had been broken. Volgan's visible form had been destroyed, but the condition of his surviving spiritual essence remained unknown. Zol had withdrawn after a rare and devastating manifestation, not been slain. The Necklace of the Abyss remained in human hands.

**The Fourth Age ends with Eindil's coronation.** The form, title, and promises of the Fifth Age have not been established. Peace cannot be assumed merely because a rightful king has returned.

## Draft dating and chronology

Only the First Age's **1,675 years**, the Second Age's **2,133 years**, and the requirement that the Third Age last **at least 1,200 years** are established durations. The Second Age must remain the longest of the known ages. A coherent proposed model is:

| Age | Duration | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| First | 1,675 years | Ends with Dardan and the failed Dead Lands campaign |
| Second | 2,133 years | Ends in 2A 2133 with Elzon's seizure of Aenor |
| Third | **1,260 years proposed** | Several centuries of occupation, gradual necromantic decline, Varner's resistance and Elzon's final destruction |
| Fourth | **1,760 years proposed** | Roughly a thousand years of broad peace, generations of Afrit's hidden rule, late Golden King, Eindil's journey and the final war |

In the example Fourth Age, peace is strongest in years 1 to approximately 1,000, Afrit exercises increasingly effective political rule in the middle centuries, and his public seizure of the crown occurs late. The war and Eindil's coronation conclude the age. All detailed dates are editable.

**Eindil and Afrit are approximately the same age, but neither has a fixed numerical age.** The earlier upper limit of 5,000 years was merely a rough estimate and is revoked. Eindil's total age must fit all the ages he spends asleep. Afrit's own survival across the ages depends on magic and corruption. The older claim that Eindil slept 100,000 years is also not active canon.

## Key distinctions and continuity rules

- Afrit's royal and public divine title is not his demonic name. **Aurevan** is a proposed public name. The ordinary populace does not know him as Afrit while he rules.
- Afrit and Eindil are brothers, sons of Ein and grandsons of Aen, and nearly the same age.
- Aen's blood explains Afrit's exceptional original nature, but divinity bestowed by the Holy Seven is a separate cosmic order.
- Glernaen is a real New God of Power and Wisdom. Horos is a Great Spirit who safeguards Glo, the crystal at his heart.
- The Castle Yeldenne encounter is in the Cold Lands. Afrit wounds Horos mortally, Eindil wounds Afrit, Afrit flees, and Horos gives Glo and his sword to Eindil before turning to dust.
- Lorien helps reveal Eindil's identity, equips his voyage, and ultimately dies opposing Zol. The Gala elves' earlier estrangement from Aenor after Edran's mistake remains historically important.
- Zol's directly sustained dead cannot be permanently defeated by mundane arms. The magic of Glo and the New Gods can destroy them. Zol's own physical appearance is rare and temporary because of the Fading.
- Volgan is defeated by Eindil with the aid of the New Gods. His incarnation collapses. His final spiritual fate is unresolved.
- Varner dies in the final war. Golbran spends nearly all his magical strength defending Varner, survives that event, and finally dies giving Eindil his remaining life after Afrit's defeat.
- Afrit briefly becomes king of an eastern coalition after abandoning Aenor. His title does not mean every eastern kingdom submits to him.
- Afrit's final death is literal. Eindil is crowned at the conclusion of the Fourth Age.
- Eindil wears the Necklace of the Abyss when he returns. The consequences of that choice are still open.

## 01 - Cosmology/He Who Was, Is and Will Be.md

# He Who Was, Is and Will Be

**Established:** The supreme supreme Creator, described as the God of all gods and of the universe. His name or title is **He Who Was, Is and Will Be**.

He stands beyond [[Holy Seven]], [[Old Gods]], [[New Gods]], and all other beings in [[Hierarchy of Being]]. There is no described rival, peer, personality, physical manifestation, clergy, or cult.

The Creator is deliberately too remote to have a practical role in Erdae's wars or politics. The precise account of how He created the Seven and existence remains unwritten.

**Do not invent:** a personal name, symbol, appearance, motives, miracles, direct dialogue, or specific doctrine without a later lore decision.

## 01 - Cosmology/Hierarchy of Being.md

# Hierarchy of Being

The ZIN universe contains an extraordinarily remote divine level above the ordinary gods and spirits. The ordinary five orders remain useful for describing the beings inhabitants of Erdae might encounter.

## Cosmic level

1. **[[He Who Was, Is and Will Be]]**: supreme creator of all existence and all gods. Beyond the ordinary orders.
2. **[[Holy Seven]]**: seven cosmic creators of ZIN, associated with the origin of the Old and New Gods. Effectively absent from ordinary history.

## The five ordinary orders

| Order | Status | What belongs here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gods** | Immortal | [[Old Gods]] and [[New Gods]] are two generations within this one divine order. |
| **Great Spirits** | Immortal | First rank of spirits, including [[Volgan]]. |
| **Lesser Spirits** | Immortal | Lower-ranked spirits, some powerful enough to be mistaken for deities. |
| **Great Folk** | Immortal | Elves, angels, demons and other immortal beings who are not spirits. |
| **Common Folk** | Mortal | Men, dwarves and other mortal beings. |

**Only Common Folk are mortal**, according to the source snapshot's five-order scheme. The status of ghosts, revenants, and roaming dead as an *order* remains unresolved.

## Gods are not equivalent to worshipped beings

Ordinary Erdae people can mistake powerful Lesser Spirits for Gods, and powerful spirits can receive worship. This does not establish that they belong to the divine order. The idea that worship alone cannot *create* godhood remains a sensible **working rule**, not a formal universal law yet.

## Power is not the same as practical control

[[Old Gods]] possess more fundamental power than [[New Gods]], yet the [[The Fading|Fading]] restricts their manifestation. A [[Spirits and Their Ranks|Great Spirit]] such as [[Volgan]] can exercise enormous political and military power and once overcame a New God despite being of a lower *order*. **Hierarchy records type of being, not automatic victory in a confrontation.**

## Unknowns

- Whether the Holy Seven were directly created by the supreme Creator, and how that act occurred.
- How many Old and New Gods exist beyond Erdae.
- How the immortal Great Folk originally came into being.
- What order, if any, should contain the restless dead.

## 01 - Cosmology/Holy Seven.md

# Holy Seven

## Established cosmological role

The **Holy Seven** are the seven overwhelmingly powerful cosmic creators of the ZIN universe and the creators of both the [[Old Gods]] and the [[New Gods]]. They exist below [[He Who Was, Is and Will Be]] and above the ordinary divine orders.

They are intentionally unimportant to day-to-day ZIN history because they are too vast and removed from it. Most inhabitants of [[Erdae and Its Regions|Erdae]] do **not** even believe they existed. Trace references may remain in the most distant myths. By contrast, ordinary people generally know of Old and New Gods.

Their individual names, fields of power, number of interventions, and appearances are **not established**. Avoid turning them into active patron gods or major antagonists.

## 01 - Cosmology/Magic and Sustenance in Erdae.md

# Magic and Sustenance in Erdae

## Confirmed relationship

Erdae has a form or condition of magic that is unusually difficult for [[Zol]] to draw upon or operate through. This local incompatibility, combined with [[The Fading]], substantially limits how freely he can act even though he remains immensely powerful.

Zol needs help from his servants to **feed** or otherwise **sustain his presence** in Erdae. The servants include **Great Spirits, Lesser Spirits, and even New Gods**. They are not all members of one cosmic order.

**Without this support, Zol could fade away from Erdae.** With it, his power grows very slowly, day by day. He has no active physical form and cannot readily conquer the continent directly.

## What is not established

The word **feed** is retained because the actual supernatural mechanism is not defined:

- What a servant collects or transfers to Zol is unknown.
- Whether feeding entails worship, souls, death, ritual, magic, sacrifice, artifacts, or something else is unknown.
- Whether the New Gods obey willingly, under compulsion, or for their own reasons is unknown.
- Whether Erdae's special magic differs by geography or by realm is unknown.
- Whether Zol has more compatible magic outside Erdae is unknown.
- Whether this process can ever restore his physical form is unknown.

Do not quietly resolve these points into a specific magic system.

## Useful conceptual separation

**Stored power**, **ability to channel power**, **presence in Erdae**, and **physical manifestation** are different aspects. This is an editorial model to help make the confirmed rules consistent, not an extra canon formula.

# New Gods

## 01 - Cosmology/Old Gods.md

# Old Gods

## Established

The **Old Gods** are the original, almost unimaginably ancient generation of gods. The [[Holy Seven]] created them before the [[New Gods]]. Originally they possessed far more power than the New Gods.

Their strength and ability to exercise power have faded across immense spans of time. They are generally unable to take active physical form and rarely influence the world openly. Yet even diminished Old Gods remain enormously powerful.

**[[Zol]] is an Old God and the only Old God in Erdae.** This does not prove that no other Old Gods exist elsewhere in ZIN.

[[The Fading]] describes the process restricting them. [[Zol]] is an important exception to any simplistic assumption of continued decline: he **gains** power slowly every day, but Erdae's magic makes it difficult for him to channel and manifest that power.

## Ideas explored but not fixed

- Each Old God might be bound to an underlying principle of reality rather than a narrow religious domain. For example, Zol governs **Death** itself, while a New God could govern mourning or funerary ritual.
- Their inability to act might derive from a growing entanglement with the foundations of existence.
- Their physical presence, dreams, visions, and influence on sacred places may have changed between ages.

These are proposed interpretations. The underlying cause of [[The Fading]] is deliberately unresolved.

## Religion

The Old Gods are known in myth and religious tradition, despite being old and often regarded as lost or inactive. [[Religious Knowledge and False Gods|Accounts may conflict]] about whether they died, slept, left, or became hidden.

## 01 - Cosmology/Religious Knowledge and False Gods.md

# Religious Knowledge and False Gods

## Established

People in Erdae are generally familiar with the existence or stories of **New Gods** and **Old Gods**. Almost nobody knows the [[Holy Seven]] as credible historical beings. The supreme Creator, [[He Who Was, Is and Will Be]], is even further beyond ordinary religious knowledge.

**Mortals often confuse powerful Lesser Spirits with gods.** Belief about a being's rank is not reliable proof of its true cosmic order. Great Spirits can likewise be worshipped.

## Working interpretation

A god's place in the divine order comes from its origin, not simply the number of worshippers it receives. For example, the powerful and much-feared [[Volgan]] remains a **Great Spirit**, even if people call him a god. This principle was proposed earlier and fits the current lore, but the exact rule for whether a being can ascend to godhood is undecided.

## Possible religious contradictions (proposals)

- One religion says the Old Gods are dead, another says they are sleeping, and a third considers them hostile.
- A town builds a temple to a local Lesser Spirit believing it a New God.
- Rare manuscripts use [[Holy Seven|names such as "The Seven Before Names"]], which later scholars dismiss as fiction.

No specific faiths, priesthoods, schisms, or doctrines have yet been established.

## Afrit and public recognition

Afrit becomes the central example of this distinction. At the end of the Third Age, many people praise him as the **New God who saved Erdae**. Some see undeniable miracles. Nevertheless, the true order of Afrit's being has not yet been defined. A powerful religious cult's belief that he is a New God can be sincere and historically important without serving as objective proof of his origin.

## 01 - Cosmology/The Fading.md

# The Fading

## Established effect

**The Fading** is a phenomenon that limits the ancient [[Old Gods]]. Across extremely long periods, their ability to exercise power and sustain an active physical manifestation has diminished, even though they retain vast underlying power.

The Fading helps explain why [[Zol]] cannot simply take a body, conquer Erdae, or use his enormous power freely. Erdae's unusual magic also resists or fails to support his form of divine power. See [[Magic and Sustenance in Erdae]].

## Important qualification: Zol's power is growing

Zol currently **gains power every day, very slowly**. This is compatible with the Fading as a lasting constraint. The amount of power he possesses and the amount he can express or direct in Erdae are separate matters.

Without servants who sustain him, his influence could fade **out of Erdae**. The lore does not say that a lapse in feeding would destroy him throughout the universe.

## Unknown mechanism

No cause for the Fading is yet canonical. It may be connected to age, the universe's history, a metaphysical law, or something else entirely. Avoid treating any of these theories as fact.

The previously suggested theory that Old Gods' essences become inseparable from fundamental forces is filed as a proposal in [[Idea Bank]].

## 02 - Spirits/Spirits and Their Ranks.md

# Spirits and Their Ranks

The ZIN order of spirits has **two confirmed ranks**:

1. **Great Spirits**: the higher spirit order, immortal and capable of exceptional power. [[Volgan]] and [[Elzon]] are Great Spirits. Elzon is also the Great Necromancer. Great Spirits can rule domains, command followers, or serve higher powers, but rulership is not an additional rank.
2. **Lesser Spirits**: the lower immortal spirit order. Some are still powerful enough for mortals to mistake them for gods.

Both ranks sit below the divine order in the formal [[Hierarchy of Being]]. Yet a spirit can defeat or surpass an individual New God, as happened with Volgan. Formal rank is not a reliable combat-strength chart.

## Three different descriptors

An individual spirit can be described independently by:

- **Rank:** Great Spirit or Lesser Spirit. This is canonical.
- **Origin:** see proposed classifications in [[Spirit Origins]].
- **Nature:** see [[Spirit Natures]], including [[Damned Spirits]].
- **Condition or manifestation:** see [[Spirit Manifestation States]].

These labels can overlap. They do not automatically introduce new orders between Gods and the Great Folk.

## Explicitly rejected category

**"Sovereign Spirits" is not a separate classification.** A Great Spirit such as [[Volgan]] may become a sovereign and rule a realm. That describes political status, not a spirit species, rank, or inherent nature.

## Open issues

The exact dividing test between Great and Lesser Spirits, their creation, relations to angels and demons, and whether a spirit can change rank remain unwritten.

## 03 - Figures/Aen.md

# Aen, First of His Name

**Status: established.** [[Aen]] is the legendary founder of [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Aenor]] and is widely recognized as the greatest man ever to live. Men existed in great numbers and many generations for thousands of years before him. Nevertheless, he is called the **First Man** and **First of His Name**. He was born through magic and power **without a direct father**. He was not the first biological human.

The men of that far earlier world were more physically powerful, more naturally attuned to magic, and substantially longer-lived than later men. Aen himself lived for **more than four hundred years** and had **hundreds of sons**. His blood is regarded as the most royal of all mankind.

Aen fought in the [[War of Men]] for hundreds of years, uniting the rival kingdoms of men into a single kingdom. His victory and Aenor's foundation mark **Year 1 of the First Age**, which lasted 1,675 years. The War of Men took place **before** the First Age. Aen's victory brought centuries of peace to the central human realm, even though later colonial expansion remained violent on distant frontiers.

## Line of descent

**Aen → Ein → [[Eindil]].** Ein is Aen's son, and Eindil is his grandson. When Eindil appears in the Fourth Age after waking in the Cold Lands, he possesses direct royal descent from the founder of Aenor. His legitimacy becomes one of the central facts of that age.

## Questions

- Where did Aen appear, and what power caused his magical birth?
- When did he die relative to Year 1, and who succeeded him?
- What exactly does the name *Ainu* denote relative to Aen, the First Men, and earlier humans?
- Was Aenor explicitly named in his honor? The connection is strong, but the etymology has not been declared canon.

## 03 - Figures/Afrit.md

# Afrit

**Public Fourth Age name:** proposed **Aurevan**, the Deliverer, New God of Men and eventual King of Aenor. **True name known to his inner council:** Afrit. Ordinary people do not call him Afrit while he rules. See [[Aurevan the Deliverer]].

**Origin, established in the Fourth Age:** Afrit is a son of **Ein**, a grandson of **Aen**, and brother of [[Eindil]]. The two brothers are approximately the same age, but no exact number of years has been fixed. Afrit was originally a man descended from the powerful First Men. After his father denied him the throne and he betrayed Eindil, he sought dominance through corruption, demonic magic, bargains, worship and artifacts. His demonic form develops over time. Underneath his protections he remains mortal and can be killed.

During the **First Age**, he secretly assembled demons and other beings who helped oppose [[Volgan]] at [[Battle of Dardan|Dardan]]. His aim was to remove a rival and position himself for the crown. During the **Second Age** he gained worship, networks and supernatural power, including an important ascension near its end. During the **Third Age** his followers supported the resistance against [[Elzon]], and he helped protect lives during Elzon's demise. The people began to regard him as a New God who saved them. The actual cosmic validity of his divinity remains unresolved. Human adoration cannot by itself prove creation as a god by the [[Holy Seven]].

For most of the **Fourth Age** Afrit governed indirectly through priests, schools, relief institutions, councilors and royal puppets. Prosperity endured during much of this process. He accumulated servants and enchanted artifacts. He sent a master thief to take [[Necklace of the Abyss|Roka's necklace]] and kept his promise of eternal life to the thief. He murdered the [[Golden King]], seized the Aenoran crown openly under his public name, and proclaimed himself King of Men and New God. He continued to build political alliances across East and South Lands as a reserve dominion.

After learning of Eindil's journey, he arrived first at Castle Yeldenne, where Horos named him as Eindil's brother. He fought Horos, mortally wounded him, and was then pierced by Eindil before escaping to his private dimension. In the last war he abandoned Aenor, acquired an eastern kingship, and returned after Zol and Volgan were repelled. He cut off Eindil's arm, struck him, and cast him from a cliff, then took Aenor again and proclaimed universal rule.

Eindil, helped by Alleria and Golbran, stripped away Afrit's rings, wards, and other artifacts. Golbran removed the Necklace of the Abyss, leaving Afrit vulnerable. Eindil **literally kills Afrit** in their last struggle. There is no surviving divine incarnation or resurrection established.

**Proposed names and mechanisms:** [[Aurevan the Deliverer|Aurevan]], the Ring of Doors, a prepared Crown of Witnesses and Bracelet of First Seal, the thief Sevrin, councilor Merovan, and details of Afrit's private realm. The original betrayal's exact manner is unwritten. His brotherly status and final mortal vulnerability are established.

## 03 - Figures/Eindil.md

# Eindil

**Established identity:** Eindil, son of [[Ein]], grandson of [[Aen]], the founder of united human kingship, and brother of [[Afrit]]. More precisely, Eindil is *son of Ein, son of Aen*. The wording in this note does not mean that Eindil is directly the son of Aen.

He comes from the ancient First Men, whose magical gifts, longevity and physical abilities were greater than those of later men. He is the strongest man remembered in the Fourth Age and is of royal blood closest to the original royal line. [[Afrit]] is approximately the same age. **Neither brother has a fixed number of years of age.** A previous upper estimate of 5,000 was explicitly described by the owner as rough and is no longer a limit, and the even older 100,000-year imprisonment account is superseded.

Eindil was held in supernatural sleep for many ages by means not yet canonically defined. He awakened in the Cold Lands in the late Fourth Age and did not remember his origins. Local tribes found him and West Lands travelers took him south. He aided communities, brought peace between rivals, and briefly served as a western king. He sailed for Aenor, was diverted to the Islands of the South, visited the Gala elves, and learned of his heritage from Lorien's histories and vision. He then voyaged to Castle Yeldenne. All the companions on his voyage there died in an ambush set by Afrit.

At Yeldenne he learned that the public god-king of Aenor was his brother. [[Horos]], a Great Spirit, gave him [[Castle of Yeldenne and Glo|Glo]], a crystal containing part of New God [[Glernaen]]'s power, and his blue sword. Eindil warned men of the gathering armies, returned with a dragon, [[Varner]] and [[Golbran]], and became the center of the last united defense of Aenor.

He defeated Volgan's manifested body after the [[New Gods]] revived him and gave him extraordinary power through Glo. Zol crushed Horos's sword and nearly killed him, but Eindil wounded the Old God's temporary incarnation with a fragment. Lorien and ancient allies forced Zol to withdraw. Afrit then returned, cut off Eindil's arm and threw him from a cliff. Eindil survived, took on Afrit in the palace with Alleria and Golbran, and killed his brother after removing the artifacts that sustained him.

Golbran gave his life to preserve Eindil. Eindil wore the Necklace of the Abyss and returned to be crowned **King of Aenor**, ending the Fourth Age.

## 03 - Figures/Elzon.md

# Elzon

**Cosmic order:** **[[Spirits and Their Ranks|Great Spirit]]** (established)  
**Epithet:** **The Great Necromancer** (established)  
**Allegiance:** Devoted servant of [[Volgan]]  
**Principal domain:** [[Volgans Dead Lands|Dead Lands]], later the conquered realm of [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Aenor]]  
**Historic role:** Defender after Dardan, architect of Volgan's return, victor at the end of the Second Age

## First Age

After [[Volgan]] was defeated at the [[Battle of Dardan]] and banished to the Void, Elzon organized his remaining forces in the Dead Lands. He had prepared the defense and repelled the subsequent attempted invasion by the [[Grand Alliance]]. Many attackers, including elves, died and the invasion failed. The victorious king is [[King Edran]], whose name is now established.

## Second Age

Elzon is a Great Spirit, a powerful and patient necromancer, and a servant of Volgan. He directs raids and constructs networks intended to prevent Aenor's reconstruction. He develops alliances with orcs, goblins, corrupt rulers, monsters, and other dark actors, without automatically ruling all such peoples.

Elzon eventually **frees Volgan from the Void and returns his soul or spiritual presence to Erdae**. Volgan is not immediately restored to full power and grants Elzon greater autonomy.

Elzon also **borrows old magic associated with [[Zol]]**, a development linked to the first perceptible stirring of that Old God in Erdae's known human history. **How Elzon obtains the magic and whether Zol wills it remain unknown**. This does not establish that he is a servant of Zol.

Elzon later openly takes the field, **raises the dead**, and turns some of the men killed while resisting him into soldiers of his own. As wars spread from within and from all directions, Aenor's allies become divided, corrupted, or unable to offer adequate support. At last Elzon **conquers Aenor and institutes his order**, which begins the **Third Age**. Elzon and Volgan are the victors of the Second Age.

## Proposed details

His early spy and raid organization might be called the **Ash Networks**. His regime after the final conquest could be known as the **Pale Regency**. Necromancy may require preserved remains, sustaining shrines, and the work of necromantic officers. These names and magical rules are **proposals**, not confirmed facts.

## Open questions

Where did Elzon learn necromancy, what was his existence like before serving Volgan, how does he keep a growing undead army under control, how did he obtain Zol's magic, and what independent goals does his new autonomy permit?

[[The Second Age of Erdae]] · [[The Return of Volgan]] · [[The Fall of Aenor]] · [[Elzons Order]] · [[The Necromancy of Elzon]] · [[Nether]]

## Third Age rule and final death

**Established:** Elzon's victory occurs in **Second Age year 2133**. In the Third Age he is the effective ruler of Aenor while still obeying orders from [[Volgan]], who is too weak to leave the Dead Lands. Elzon's undead host weakens with time and his own power runs down in Aenor because the local magic does not replenish him as the Dead Lands do. He seeks the power necessary to transform Aenor into another sustaining dominion. Men continue resisting, and the independent Magical Lands defend themselves, aided by [[Varner]], a Lesser Spirit able to lift the curse of the dead, and his purple servant [[Golbran]]. Elzon's rule ends in his **literal and permanent death**.

**Proposed cause and sequence:** the [[Black Roots]] would be his effort to change Aenor's magic. During [[The Last Tithe]] the human resistance and Varner break his supporting spells. Elzon overdraws the death-magic he earlier borrowed from [[Zol]], allowing his spirit to be consumed entirely. Afrit then helps contain the disaster and is hailed as a New God. Zol himself remains dormant and gains strength through the involuntary absorption, without intervening openly in the Third Age.

## 03 - Figures/Horos.md

## 03 - Figures/Zol.md

# Zol

**Established:** [[Old Gods|Zol is the sole Old God present in Erdae]], the God of Death. He first arrived before Aen and before Volgan. His northern domain is [[Zols Mistake|Zol's Mistake]]. The Fading and incompatibility of Erdae's magic prevent him from exercising his cosmic power freely. ## 04 - Peoples/The Ainu.md

# The Ainu and the First Men

**Established:** Human civilizations existed for thousands of years before the First Age and before [[Aen]]. The earliest generations of men were generally stronger, had longer lives, and were naturally better at magic than later men. Some possessed power resembling that of gods without being true gods.

Aen was born from magic and power and had no ordinary father. The title **First of His Name** refers to his unique origin and place as founder of the unified human realm. It does not mean that he was the first biological human to exist. He lived more than 400 years, fathered hundreds of sons and eventually won the centuries-long War of Men.

The line most important to the Fourth Age is **[[Aen]] → [[Ein]] → [[Eindil]] and [[Afrit]]**. Eindil and Afrit are brothers and approximately the same age. Their exact ages have not been fixed. Both survive across many ages, Eindil through supernatural suspension and Afrit through magic and corruption.

## Former estimates

An older compendium placed the disappearance of the Ainu and Eindil's suspension at more than 100,000 years, and an intermediate correction suggested Eindil might be no older than about 5,000 years. **Neither figure is currently binding**. The creator explicitly stated that 5,000 years was only a rough estimate. The current Four-Age framework can span more than 6,000 years, with the First Men's magical longevity and Eindil's suspension accounting for survival. No exact birth years should be inferred.

**Open:** Whether the name *Ainu* refers to all powerful early humans, only Aen's dynasty, or an earlier culture remains unknown.

## 05 - Geography/Middle Lands and Aenor.md

# Middle Lands and Aenor

**Aenor, the Middle Kingdom**, was previously called **Rush, the Rush Kingdom**. It is the central great power of Erdae. Its current map-design capital is **Parim**.

The **Middle Lands**, previously Rush Land, contain the Middle Kingdom's central plains, forests, mountains, and Great Lake. Associated named regions include **Selen, Kilinti, Kingson, Hiloron, Konori, Hunhun**, and **Herez**. **Aenor Isle** (previously Rush Islands) lies off the west coast and contains **Asgard**.

The older original story recognizes dominions including Selen's rangers, Hiloron's giants, Kilinti's ents and forest peoples, and the riders of **Huhun**. The revised map's country system treats Aenor's surrounding Middle Lands regions as **satellites**. Whether each original dominion is constitutionally a satellite or an internal division remains an open implementation/lore detail.

The current working table assigns Aenor **26 satellites**, with listed examples **Selen, Kilinti, Kingson, Hiloron, Konori, Hunhun, Asgard, Herez**. The **Cursed Lands** and **Bloody Forest** resist its control.

Its ruling [[Golden King]] founded **The Lost Cause**, built a strong army, and misgoverns the realm. [[Afrit]] secretly causes his death and hopes to capture the crown. [[Eindil]] may emerge as a leader in the war that follows.

**Names to keep distinct:** The snapshot uses both **Huhun** in historical discussion and **Hunhun** in the working satellite list. Whether both refer to the same place is not proven in the provided text, so this vault preserves both and flags the ambiguity in [[Open Questions]].

**Open:** Is Aenor named for **Aen the Ainu**?

[[Erdae and Its Regions]] · [[Great Powers and Relations]] · [[Kingdoms Factions and Conflicts]]

## Aenor in ancient history

**Newly established:** [[Aen]] founded Aenor by unifying human kingdoms after the centuries-long [[War of Men]]. His victory began **Year 1 of the First Age**. The kingdom experienced centuries of relative prosperity, exploration, and the settlement of territories beyond the Middle Lands. Later misrule and revolt left it vulnerable to [[Volgan]], who seized the capital during the great war. The later [[Grand Alliance]] saved the kingdom.

Aenor's first settlement and expansion into the West Lands provoked **wars with pre-existing inhabitants**, creating hostilities still visible in the Fourth Age. The sea between West Lands and Middle Lands must be **significantly wider** than the supplied approximate Fourth Age map depicts, which makes ancient control more difficult.

**Fourth Age lineage:** [[Eindil]], directly descended as Aen's grandson through Ein, awakens in the Cold Lands and is eventually crowned king in Aenor.

[[The First Age of Erdae]] · [[Fourth Age Map of Erdae]]

## Second Age defeat

After the First Age, Aenor survives under a great king and tries to rebuild. Generations of raids, plagues, demonic portals, division among its former allies, and the Great Spirit [[Elzon]]'s necromancy reverse this recovery. **At the end of the [[The Second Age of Erdae|Second Age]], Elzon takes Aenor and establishes his order**, inaugurating the Third Age.

The historical name of the capital during this period is **not yet confirmed**, even though the Fourth Age map-design seat is Parim. The later restoration of an Aenoran monarchy and the continuity of Aen's heirs before [[Eindil]]'s coronation remain to be written.

## The Third Age and restored monarchy

[[Elzon]] conquers Aenor in **Second Age year 2133**. Through the Third Age, men resist while he rules in [[Volgan]]'s name. He lacks a natural source of sustaining magic in Aenor and cannot permanently support his undead army without changing the land itself. The elven kingdoms do not send rescue armies to Aenor because of [[King Edran]]'s failed Dead Lands campaign. Elzon's eventual permanent death ends the occupation.

In the Fourth Age a genuinely good human king restores the monarchy, followed by a long peace under successive rulers. [[Afrit]] is praised and worshipped as a New God and secretly grows influential in the kingdom. See [[The Third Age of Erdae]] and [[The Fourth Age of Erdae]].

## 05 - Geography/Volgans Dead Lands.md

# Volgans Dead Lands

*Canonical regional name: **Dead Lands**. This note also refers to the area as Volgan's Dead Lands for clarity.*

The Dead Lands lie **south of the Middle Lands** and are the principal dominion of [[Volgan]], the Great Spirit and main dark lord.

The region includes **Mirolon, Nan, Demon, Dolon, Nezer, and Nether**. In the working great-power table, Volgan's seat is **Nafeher** and his satellites or allies include **Demon, Dolon, and Nezer**, while **Mirolon** opposes him.

Volgan's realm includes grave hosts, Netherborn demons, orcs, ogres, monster clans, and necromantic orders. His authority does not automatically extend to independent Dark Lands factions or northern monsters.

The dead here and the dead in [[Zols Mistake|Zol's Mistake]] have no settled taxonomy or relationship. Volgan is not an Old God, and his necromancy should not automatically be taken as authority over Death itself.

[[Erdae and Its Regions]] · [[Great Powers and Relations]] · [[Peoples of Erdae]]

## First Age campaign

**Newly established:** The Dead Lands were difficult for earlier human explorers to access because of fierce storms at sea and oppressive heat farther south. In the latter First Age, [[Volgan]] invaded **Mirolon** and made it the opening of a vast campaign against Aenor and its allies. After the [[Battle of Dardan]], Volgan was banished to the Void. The [[Grand Alliance]] attempted to conquer the Dead Lands, but **[[Elzon]] prepared and led the remaining defenders** and drove them back, causing heavy losses and lasting divisions between elves and men.

**Important distinction:** Mirolon is Volgan's First Age conquest target and his rival in the *working* Fourth Age political table. Its political status must not be treated as identical across all ages.

[[The First Age of Erdae]] · [[Battle of Dardan]]

## Second Age additions

The [[Nether]] is established in the **south of the Dead Lands** as the domain or residence of the **King of Demons**. The [[Abyss]] is also in the Dead Lands and is associated with [[Roka]] and the [[Necklace of the Abyss]], which Afrit takes and uses during the **Fourth Age**.

The [[Nether]], [[Abyss]], and the **Void** that imprisoned Volgan are distinct named places or realms, with any deeper connections still undecided.

During the Second Age Elzon retrieves Volgan's soul or presence from the Void and coordinates a new war of raids, internal collapse, and necromancy. His victory ends in the fall of [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Aenor]] and begins the Third Age.

## Third Age after the reconquest of Aenor

During the Third Age, Volgan cannot leave the Dead Lands, while [[Elzon]] rules Aenor in his name. The Dead Lands' native magical conditions sustain Elzon, whereas Aenor's magic does not. Elzon eventually dies and Volgan remains alive. This does not change the location or independent identity of the [[Nether]] and [[Abyss]], or imply that [[Zol]] arrives physically in the Dead Lands.

*Canonical displayed name: **Zol's Mistake**.*

Its name derives from [[Zol]], the Old God of Death and the only Old God present in Erdae. The exact event or transgression that makes this land a **"Mistake"** has not been explained.

The dead wandering the land have no established classification or allegiance. Do not assume they all belong to Zol, [[Volgan]], or any single category until decided.

## 07 - History/Afrits Ascension.md

# Afrit's Ascension

**Established:** [[Afrit]] becomes considerably more influential through the Second Age and his **ascension near its end** is one of the age's defining events. In the earlier [[The First Age of Erdae|First Age]], Afrit secretly rallied demons, spirits, men, and elves against [[Volgan]], materially helping bring about the dark lord's defeat. People did not recognize Afrit's role.

Afrit aided the struggle to remove a rival and weaken Aenor, not because he was a trustworthy ally of the [[Grand Alliance]]. During the Second Age he uses worship, cults, bargains, favors, and corruption to gain power. He remains independent of [[Elzon]] and Volgan.

## Proposed nature of the ascension

Afrit's secret contracts and cults can be imagined as individual threads that eventually form a single supernatural network. A coordinated rite called *the Night of a Thousand Oaths* would be the visible moment of ascension. Worshippers from different regions bind themselves under the same name without necessarily realizing whom they serve. Afrit acquires an ability to project influence through the web at an unprecedented scale.

The [[Afrit|Worshipdom]] may grow from aid organizations and local cults that became dependent on him during the plague years. This is proposed and its location remains open.

## Limits

- The ascension does **not** automatically make Afrit a [[New Gods|New God]]. His cosmic order is still unknown.
- **Elzon** takes Aenor at the end of the Second Age. Afrit's goal of gaining Aenor's crown remains unfinished.
- Afrit only takes [[Necklace of the Abyss|Roka's Necklace of the Abyss]] in the **Fourth Age**, so it is not the cause of the Second Age ascension unless future lore explicitly revises chronology.
- Afrit's exact powers and relations with the [[Nether]]'s King of Demons are not established.

## His later recognition as a New God

People worship him as their Deliverer. Whether he truly belongs to the divine order created by the Holy Seven is undecided. He does not rule Aenor openly and his concealed influence expands through the long peace of the Fourth Age. See [[Afrit the Deliverer]] and [[The Third Age of Erdae]].

## 07 - History/Battle of Dardan.md

# Battle of Dardan

**Established:** The decisive battle of the First Age war against [[Volgan]]. It claimed **millions of lives** and left a wasteland behind. The [[Grand Alliance]] defeated Volgan, and other spirits stripped his power and banished him to the **Void**. The aftermath included an unsuccessful invasion of the Dead Lands and the end of the First Age in **Year 1675**.

**The battlefield's precise position on the map, the battle duration, and the banishment process are not yet fixed.**

## Proposed fuller account

The battlefield is placed in the southern approaches of the Middle Lands, close to the old route from the southern coast to the crownlands. Volgan concentrates an army of corrupted elves, orcs, goblins, allied eastern and southern men, demons and devils, dragons, dark Great Spirits, and Damned Spirits released from beneath the mountains. The Grand Alliance brings its armies from multiple regions across Erdae.

The suggested *Thirteen Days of Dardan* include the collapse of its first lines, fighting at river and road crossings, the eruption of Damned Spirits from beneath the hills, the commitment of [[Lorien]]'s Gala elves, and a joint rite by allied Great Spirits. The rite can only succeed after mortal and immortal allies break the defenses around Volgan. It strips the strength he can project into Erdae and banishes him to the Void, at significant cost to the participating spirits. Volgan is not annihilated.

## 07 - History/Second Age Progress and Recovery.md

# Second Age Progress and Recovery

The [[The Second Age of Erdae|Second Age]] lasted **2,133 years**, making it longer than the First Age's 1,675 years. It is remembered as the Dark Age and Age of Long Grief because repeated wars, famine, diseases, raids, and corruption prevented long, uninterrupted recovery after Dardan. Yet it was **not entirely a period of suffering**.

**Established achievements:** Aenor and other regions made substantial progress in **warfare and military technology, disease treatment and cures, farming and famine prevention**. These discoveries and methods improved the survival of later generations and contributed to Aenor's ability to recover in the Fourth Age.

## Proposed examples to develop later

- **Farming:** rotation of crops, drought-resistant plants, irrigation canals, maintained seed reserves, improved storage structures, and communal harvest rules to reduce the risk of hunger after a bad season.
- **Medicine:** specialist healers, cleaner water supply, isolation of certain contagious illnesses, remedies developed from regional plants, shared case records, and magical healing where appropriate. Not every plague is magical and not every cure is miraculous.
- **Military practice:** improved layered fortifications, siege defenses, organized supply columns, messengers, roads, reliable reserve forces, and weapons designed to halt fast raids or resist the undead.
- **Political cooperation:** agreements to open roads and trade grain even when rulers distrust one another, temporary neutral hospitals, and community institutions that endure after royal authority fails.

These techniques are inventions of people living during the Second Age. Although religious histories of [[Afrit the Deliverer|Afrit the Deliverer]] may later attribute recovery to a god, the original achievements remain those of generations of engineers, farmers, healers, and soldiers.

## The death of King Edran

[[King Edran]], the great king of the [[Grand Alliance]], guides rebuilding in the early Second Age but eventually **dies of disease**. His exact age, year of death, and the particular illness remain undecided. His death is one episode in a long series of human successions and renewals, not proof that Aenor collapses immediately.

## 07 - History/The Necromancy of Elzon.md

# The Necromancy of Elzon

**Established:** [[Elzon]] is a **Great Spirit** and the **Great Necromancer**. During the Second Age he summons or reanimates the dead on a huge scale, including fallen fighters of Aenor. This increases his own forces as his enemies lose men. He also borrows ancient magic associated with [[Zol]], though how that magic was obtained is unknown.

## Proposed battlefield rules

To prevent necromancy from becoming an automatic victory in every battle, the following are candidates for later adoption:

- A corpse must be reachable, sufficiently intact, or otherwise bound through a necromantic object, name, or place.
- Elzon can raise huge numbers, but lesser necromancers depend on sources of power, prepared marks, and dedicated ritual sites. This gives defenders infrastructure to attack.
- Ordinary undead and powerful named revenants differ in how much will, memory, and sorcery they retain.
- A society might guard graves, retrieve its dead, destroy sustaining shrines, or learn counter-rites. No single defense should be assumed universal.
- Reanimation of a body is **not automatically command of its true soul**. Whether souls are imprisoned at all, or merely memories and bodies are used, remains a major theological question.

## A dangerous distinction

Zol, as God of Death and the only [[Old Gods|Old God]] in Erdae, possesses a vastly greater kind of divine authority. His [[The Fading|Fading]] and the unusual magic of Erdae limit what he can manifest directly. Elzon's immense applied necromancy does not give him Zol's rank or a right to rule death in the cosmic sense.

[[The Second Age of Erdae]] · [[The Return of Volgan]] · [[Spirits and Their Ranks]] · [[Zol]]

## Third Age limitations and permanent death

Established limits: undead raised by Elzon fade with time, and he loses power when he stays in Aenor rather than the magically sustaining Dead Lands. He cannot fully conquer the Magical Lands, where [[Varner]], a Lesser Spirit, can lift the curse of the dead. Men refuse to submit entirely. Elzon desires to convert Aenor into another region that naturally sustains his magic.

The proposed [[Black Roots]] and the [[The Last Tithe|Last Tithe]] explain his failure and **literal death** when he draws too deeply on old magic associated with [[Zol]]. These mechanics are proposed, while his eventual permanent death and broad limits are established.

## 07 - History/The Return of Volgan.md

# The Return of Volgan

**Established:** The Great Necromancer [[Elzon]], a Great Spirit and servant of [[Volgan]], eventually frees his master from the **Void** during the Second Age. Volgan's soul or spiritual presence returns to Erdae, and he grants Elzon greater autonomy. Volgan needs time to recover the power stripped from him at the [[Battle of Dardan]]. The return is real, not merely a misleading prophecy.

## Historical significance

Volgan was **banished**, not destroyed, in the First Age. If the defeat at Dardan had not seriously constrained him, the Grand Alliance's enormous sacrifice would lack weight. Likewise, if the banishment were wholly irreversible, the Second Age's central story could not occur. Elzon makes a real but difficult breakthrough after centuries of work.

With Volgan restored in presence but not in full strength, the campaign against Aenor is Elzon's to design. Volgan's recovered will gives his surviving followers a focus, while Elzon's expanded authority lets him organize a separate order.

## Proposed explanation: a broken seal

A powerful binding at Dardan originally closed the path to the Void. Elzon seeks three things: a surviving spiritual trace of Volgan, weak points in the binding, and ancient magic potent enough to reach across the boundary. In this telling Elzon draws on [[Zol]]'s old death-magic. The rite consumes hidden strongholds or enormous stores of power.

Zol is no ordinary patron. His nature as an [[Old Gods|Old God]], his immense residual power, his slow increase in strength, and [[The Fading|the Fading]] make his place in Erdae unlike that of Elzon or Volgan. Elzon's borrowing does **not** confirm Zol's permission or establish an alliance between them.

This event crosses a boundary into the **Void**. It does not require passage through the [[Nether]] or the [[Abyss]]. Those are distinct settings until further lore says otherwise.

## Unresolved

How Elzon accessed Zol's magic, the costs paid, where the rite occurred, how Volgan manifested afterward, and why the Allied Great Spirits could not reseal the prison are all open.

## 07 - History/War of the Last Crowns.md

# War of the Last Crowns

**Established principal conflict:** The final conflict of the Fourth Age combines Western invasion and the seizure of Aenor's capital, Eastern and southern armies, North Lands' political separation, Volgan's advance from the Dead Lands, Zol's direct dead armies, Afrit's flight to the East, Eindil's return, and Afrit's eventual final defeat. **War of the Last Crowns** is a proposed collective historical name.

### Principal strategic phases

1. Aenor is beset from four directions. West Lands rulers sail across the wide central sea to seize the capital. Eastern coalitions threaten the Magical Lands. North Lands rulers withdraw from Aenor's alliance. Zol's dead advance from the far north. Volgan retakes Mirolon and moves from the southern Dead Lands toward Aenor.
2. The publicly worshipped god-king, secretly Afrit, abandons his subjects and assumes control of an eastern coalition through networks cultivated for ages. The western army occupies Aenor's capital.
3. Eastern and Aenoran forces retreat as Volgan and Zol's dead overwhelm the region. Westerners find their conquest untenable and begin evacuating. Some eastern and western troops remain stranded. Zol and Volgan initially clash before briefly joining forces against Eindil.
4. Eindil returns on a dragon with Varner and Golbran. With Glo and Horos's blue blade he destroys Zol's stronger-than-necromantic dead, and the surviving peoples make a desperate common army. He is beaten by Volgan, but Glernaen and the New Gods restore him through Glo.
5. Eindil defeats Volgan's embodied manifestation. Zol appears bodily for a rare moment, overpowers the renewed champion, shatters the blue sword, and nearly conquers the field. Eindil wounds Zol's body with a shard. Lorien and ancient spirits die forcing the Old God to withdraw.
6. Afrit returns from the East, wounds and casts Eindil from a cliff, and claims Aenor. Eindil survives, and Alleria and Golbran help strip Afrit's artifacts at the palace. Afrit dies, Golbran dies saving Eindil, and Eindil is crowned with the Necklace of the Abyss in his possession.

### Distinctions

- Zol's dead derive from the Old God's own magic. Mortal arms cannot end them permanently without magic. Elzon's earlier reanimated corpses were comparatively vulnerable, requiring continuing magical support.
- The western armies do not all become subjects of Aenor after cooperating with Eindil. Competing western and northern interests remain.
- Volgan's manifested body is destroyed. His final spiritual fate is unresolved.
- Zol's manifestation is temporary and costly in the presence of the Fading. He is forced to withdraw, not killed.
- Afrit's final death is literal.

## 17 October 2026: Cosmology and First Age

- Established the Supreme Creator, Holy Seven, Old and New Gods, Great and Lesser Spirits, and Erdae's peoples.
- Zol is an Old God limited by the Fading and the local magic. Volgan is a Great Spirit; Damned Spirits live deep underground.
- Replaced an older thematic age scheme with the numbered Ages. First Age fixed at 1,675 years, beginning when Aen wins the War of Men.
- Expanded Aenor's founding, western expansion, Volgan's invasion, Dardan, Edran's Grand Alliance, and Elzon's defense of the Dead Lands.

## 17 October 2026: Second and Third Ages

- Fixed the Second Age at 2,133 years. Recorded disease, famines, progress in warfare, medicine and farming, Edran's death of illness, and elves' refusal to aid Aenor against Elzon.
- Confirmed Elzon as Great Spirit and Great Necromancer, creator of undead hosts, conqueror of Aenor, and later permanently destroyed after long occupation.
- Recorded Volgan's return from the Void and inability to leave Dead Lands during the Third Age.
- Established Varner as Lesser Spirit, Golbran as small purple companion, and Magical Lands remaining unconquered.
- Afrit becomes the publicly worshipped New God credited with saving Aenor after Elzon's death, while his true divine classification remains unconfirmed.

## 17 October 2026: Fourth Age and chronology revision

- Third Age revised to last **at least 1,200 years**, with **1,260** a working precise count. Former **128-year** model superseded.
- Eindil's former **5,000-year limit** revoked as a rough estimate. An even older 100,000-year estimate also stays superseded.
- Afrit and Eindil confirmed as **brothers**, both sons of Ein and grandsons of Aen, approximately the same age.
- The Fourth Age now contains over a millennium of prosperity, Afrit's concealed control through religion and royal councils, the theft of Roka's necklace, the Golden King's murder and Afrit's open kingship.
- Eindil's journey from Cold Lands through West Lands, Islands of the South, Lorien's Gala elves and Horos's Castle Yeldenne has been chronicled. Horos's original three drawings preserved.
- The final wars pit Aenor against Western, Northern, Eastern, Volgan and Zol-linked forces. Volgan's embodied form is destroyed, Zol retreats after Lorien's sacrifice, and Varner dies.
- Afrit attacks his brother, retakes Aenor, and is later killed after Eindil, Alleria and Golbran strip his artifacts away. Golbran sacrifices his last strength to save Eindil, who returns with the Necklace of the Abyss and is crowned.

Dates, secondary names and magical mechanisms not directly specified by the creator remain proposed and are listed in the canon registers.

## 18 October 2026: Eindil naming revision

- Renamed **Einendil** to **Eindil** at the creator’s request. This affects his personal note, journey note, all current histories, genealogies, Obsidian links, timelines, and consolidated lore.
- All other character facts are unchanged, including Eindil and Afrit being brothers, both sons of Ein and grandsons of Aen, approximately the same age with no fixed numerical maximum.
- The older original source and superseded Third Age draft are preserved unchanged as historical documents. Their previous spelling is not current canon.

## 09 - Development/Idea Bank.md

# Idea Bank

> **None of the ideas in this note should be presented as established canon without adoption.** They are preserved from the earlier lore discussion so no potentially useful proposal is lost.

## The Seven's mythical names

The Seven Before Names, The Seven Who Never Were, The Unremembered Seven, The First Hands, The Nameless Seven, The Seven Beyond Time, The Forgotten Makers. See [[Holy Seven]] for interpretations and the suggested division between ancient and skeptical usage.

## Possible god-domain structure

Old Gods may embody fundamental principles of existence, while New Gods govern narrower aspects of those principles. Zol could govern **Death itself**, and some New God might govern mourning or funerals. There is no actual named New God of mourning in canon.

## Possible explanations for the Fading

The Old Gods may have become so bound to the underlying forces of reality that they can no longer freely move through and command the material world. This idea is unconfirmed and must not be turned into the factual cause of [[The Fading]].

Conflicting faiths might believe Old Gods died, slept, withdrew, or were replaced. The actual historical explanation need not be known to worshippers.

## Potential spirit classification language

- **Origins:** Elder, Worldborn, Godwrought. See [[Spirit Origins]].
- **Natures:** Wild, Veiled, Elemental, Damned. See [[Spirit Natures]].
- **Conditions:** Embodied, Unbound, Bound, Dormant, Shattered. See [[Spirit Manifestation States]].
- **Damned Spirits:** sealed cavern civilizations, sunless roots of mountains, ruined underground temples, curses, lost spirits. These are only motifs.

Earlier **Blighted Spirits** meant cursed or corrupted beings, and the idea was redirected toward deep-dwelling **Damned Spirits**. The separate "Sovereign Spirits" classification was explicitly rejected. Do not add it back.

## Possible Zol-related political interpretation

The map-design capital **Furviatlon** might house a hierarchy of priests, New God servants, or spirit administrators acting for Zol. No such office or agent has yet been canonized. His servants' sustenance mechanism is likewise **unexplained**.

## Possible rule for divinity

A spirit that attracts worship is often mistaken for a god, but worship alone may never grant true godhood. This would make the difference between [[Volgan]] and the [[New Gods]] clear while still allowing one to surpass another in effective might. **Ascension rules remain undecided.**

## Possible ongoing story hooks

- Different religions disagree on the nature of Zol's growing power and dependence on servants.
- A god who serves Zol has reasons to conceal that allegiance.
- Remnants of the cult of the unknown New God overpowered by Volgan survive in a forgotten region.
- A Damned Spirit emerges from the deep underground and is worshipped as a New God.

All these hooks are optional and should not be interpreted as events that already happened.

## Second Age optional development (17 October 2026)

- [[Second Age Proposed Timeline]] now reflects the **established 2,133-year length**. Only internal phase dates and sample lifespan calculations remain proposals.
- [[The Second Age of Erdae]] contains possible named events including the *Council of Empty Chairs*, *War of the Black Roads*, *Burning Gate of Nezer*, *War of the Hollow Crown*, and optional plague names.
- Possible mechanisms for the [[The Return of Volgan|return from the Void]] include weakening the Dardan boundary and using magic Elzon drew from [[Zol]]. The mechanism is not established even though the return itself is.
- *Night of a Thousand Oaths* is a proposed explanation for [[Afrits Ascension|Afrit's ascension]].
- *Pale Regency* is a suggested later name for [[Elzons Order|Elzon's Order]].
- An escaped royal archivist could provide genealogical continuity into the Fourth Age, when [[Eindil]] appears as Aen's direct grandson.

## Third Age ideas now developed

The detailed Third Age includes suggested **Black Roots** as Elzon's attempt to convert Aenor into self-sustaining necromantic territory, **Lantern Chain** as Varner and Golbran's decentralized anti-curse network, **War of Unbinding** as the final liberation campaign, **Last Tithe** as Elzon's permanent destruction via a dangerous conduit to Zol, **Harion the Restorer** as the first good restored human king, and **Afrit the Deliverer** as the publicly revered savior. Names, dates, and mechanics remain proposals except where the owner has confirmed the broad outcome.

## 09 - Development/Second Age Proposed Timeline.md

# Second Age Working Timeline

**Canonical year count:** The Second Age is exactly **2,133 years**, ending with the fall of [[Middle Lands and Aenor|Aenor]] to [[Elzon]] in **2A 2133**. A prior *2,300-year model* was a draft and is **superseded**. The 2,133-year total is now established, but the phase boundaries below are speculative.

| Second Age years | Proposed period | Broad development |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 240 | The Long Mourning | Edran's recovery effort, rebuilding, disease and the eventual death of the king |
| 241 to 660 | Wars of the Remnants | Persistent orc and goblin campaigns, rising local autonomy, elven refusal to renew the old alliance |
| 661 to 1,150 | The Hard Centuries | Repeated hunger and plague alongside major improvements in agriculture, irrigation, quarantine, medical treatment, and military defenses |
| 1,151 to 1,550 | The Open Gates | Nether demonic raids, Elzon's networks, Afrit's worship and influence |
| 1,551 to 1,900 | The Return | Elzon restores Volgan's spiritual presence from the Void and uses ancient magic tied to Zol |
| 1,901 to 2,080 | The War from Within | Plagues, subversion, necromancy, and internal invasions weaken Aenor |
| 2,081 to 2,133 | The Fallen Crown | Final conquest, late ascension of Afrit, Elzon occupies Aenor |

Only the last date and full duration have been established. The exact year of Edran's death, Volgan's return and Afrit's Second Age ascension are open. The long age includes considerable cultural, agricultural, medical, and military development, not just uninterrupted suffering.
