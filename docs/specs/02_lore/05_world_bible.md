# World bible: the peoples, cultures and countries of Erdae

**Status:** Draft. **Date:** 19 October 2026. **Priority:** 2. **Phase:** 2 (names and cultures feed phase 1 map names and every later system).
**Data:** [tools/content/data/world/cultures.json](../../../tools/content/data/world/cultures.json), [country_cultures.json](../../../tools/content/data/world/country_cultures.json), [languages.json](../../../tools/content/data/world/languages.json), [demography.json](../../../tools/content/data/world/demography.json).
**Companions:** [06_languages.md](06_languages.md), [07_demography_and_settlement.md](07_demography_and_settlement.md), [08_world_in_game.md](08_world_in_game.md).

## Purpose

Erdae should read as one real world where everything is accounted for. This bible says who lives where and why, what they believe, how they rule, marry, bury, eat, build and fight, what their neighbours think of them, and how all that looks in the game. It covers the ten peoples, the 53 cultures inside them, the 24 languages they speak, and all 305 countries. The lore vault stays canon. The bible only adds, and says so.

Rules the bible keeps:

1. Where the vault is silent, the bible proposes. Where the vault speaks, the vault wins ([Canon Policy](../../lore/vault/Canon%20Policy.md)).
2. No New God is named except Glernaen [E]. Faiths describe what mortals believe, and mortals can be wrong (the vault says they mistake Lesser Spirits for gods).
3. Open questions stay open: the dwarves' homeland, the relation of the Gala and Sunhot elves, who governs Zol's Mistake for Zol, the King of Demons' name, the name of Roka's people, the Golden King's own name, the place of Dardan, whether Volgan's spirit survives, what "Ainu" means, and whether Aenor is named for Aen.
4. The start is fixed: 1 Thaw 4A 1748, peace, the Golden King reigns, Eindil has not appeared, Afrit is unrevealed.
5. Player-facing words avoid modern political terms. This bible uses "council", "league" and "guild" for what other texts would call a republic or a federation.

## The creative frame in six lines

1. **A world of names.** Aen's War of Men joined the kingdoms of men. Aenor's First Age ships then charted the world and stamped their own endings on it: *-dor* for lands, *-waith* for shores. The names on the map are **chart forms**. Every people has its own older name, and the western grudge against Aenor begins with who got to name the coast.
2. **Names are dangerous.** The Registers of the Lost, kept after Dardan, taught Elzon's necromancers how to find the dead by their names. Since the Third Age every people of men keeps a **use-name** for the world and a **hearth-name** for the house, and buries the dead under a **grave-name**.
3. **The long peace is real.** More than seventeen centuries of peace [E] made Aenor rich and fat, filled the Great Lake basin and let the East build its caravan roads. The population is the largest of any age.
4. **The elves are the old keepers.** They were never conquered [E]. They hold their land by memory, by song and by dragons, and they remember the dead of Dardan.
5. **The monsters are societies.** Orcs, trolls, giants, yeti and ents are not armies of one dark lord [E]. Each has homes, laws and a faith. Volgan's Dominion is a separate thing.
6. **Everything the dead cannot say is left blank.** Zol's dead have no language, no faith and no names in this bible, because the vault gives them none.

Dragons are not a people and form no country [E].

### Men

The mortal common folk are the great majority of Erdae. The ancient First Men were stronger, longer-lived and more magical than those of today [E]. Aen, their last great king, was fatherless and born of magic [E]. Today's men descend from several stocks: the **Ainic** peoples who share Aen's language family (Aenorans, the Leren, Dondor, Mirolon), the **Boreal** peoples of the north-west who were there when the charts came (the Cold Lands, the West Lands and Triton's Islands), the **Eastern** peoples of the river, desert and road kingdoms, the **islanders** of the south, and the **Orran** survivors of the beaten kingdoms. The bible gives them 31 cultures.

### Elves

The Great Folk are immortal [E]. The elves of the Magical Lands are friendly, democratic and very old [E] and were never conquered, not even by Elzon [E]. The elves tell many stories of how the Great Folk began and hold none of them to be fact. They remember Dardan and the Elven Refusal [E]. They command dragons [E]. The bible gives them five cultures in the Magical Lands (council, hill, lake, pass and rift-coast elves) and the Gala court. The Gala elves share the Sunhot lands, but their relation to the Sunhot elves is open [O]; nothing here states it.

### Sunhot elves

They are the strongest and among the most peaceful of the peoples [E]. They live in many small sanctuary realms in the far south-west. Their lands are rich and their numbers few. The bible treats them as the keepers of the Sea of Gods, because the lands there carry holy names (Heaven, Flame, Godland, Dreamland [E labels]), and gives them three cultures: the Lands of Light, the western sanctuaries and the eastern isles.

### Orcs

The orcs and goblins of the Dark Lands are many societies, not one obedient host [E]. The holds differ and no two are alike [E]. Many fought for Volgan after Dardan, many did not [E]. The bible gives them four cultures (Volcano Clans, Iron Clans, Wild Holds, Sea Orcs) and the goblin warrens as a minor people.

### Trolls

The troll tribes of the Troll Forest are slow, hardy and hungry [E]. They live in one country that must stay separate [E].

### Giants

The hill giants of Hiloron, a Concord dominion [E], and the ice giants of Titanos in the Cold Lands [E] are a few and enormous. The Forgotten Giants are named but unplaced [O].

### Yeti

Furred clans of the Yeti Lands [E], at home in the coldest winters. Their speech is sung.

### Ents

The tree-folk of Kilinti [E]. Their moot takes years to say a sentence. A few men live at the forest edge.

### The dead

Two realms hold the dead: Volgan's Dominion (grave hosts, necromantic orders) and Zol's Mistake (the dead sustained by Zol's own magic, which ordinary weapons cannot end) [E]. The vault gives no relation between them [O]. The living there are few.

### Demons

Corrupted angels in the existing account [E]. The Nether, in the south of the Dead Lands, is the seat of the King of Demons [E]; his name is open [O]. Demon, Dolon and Nezer are sworn to Volgan [W].

### Aenor, the Middle Kingdom

The kingdom lies where the Great Lake meets the Vale of Aenor, the best-fed ground in Erdae. Aen's victory in 1A 1 joined the plain kingdoms here [E]. The roads of Aen ran out from the lake in every direction, and the lake has fed the capital ever since. The kingdom has fallen once, in 2A 2133 [E], and was rebuilt from the forests of Selen and the isle of Asgard after Elzon died. Harion's Charters of Parim made the surviving dominions into a Concord of twenty-six, each with its own lord and law. Parim, on the lake bluff where the River Aenar enters, is the Fourth Age seat; the older capital's name is open [O].

Now it is the richest and fattest realm of the world, under a king who sells offices. Its strengths are institutions older than any king: the Concord courts, the universities, the Houses of the First Light that keep the grain, the hospitals and the birth rolls. [secret: the rolls hold every hearth-name, and the Houses answer to a master that no king has ever seen] It trusts its dominions, which it has bought with charters, and the Deliverer, who has never failed it. It fears Lerendor's pride, the West's old claims and, in legend, a Volgan that no one has seen in a hundred years. Its borders follow water and the Wall: the Great Lake and the Aenar make the core, the Wall Accord of 4A 96 fixes the east, the Narrow Strait the north, the Crown Ocean the west.

### The Worshipdom [secret hub]

Three valleys in the Zilin Hills, east of the Great Wall, were a hermitage of the Deliverer about 4A 1520 [P]. The elves of the Magical Lands let it stand because pilgrims were harmless and healers welcome. It grew by hostels: a bed, a loaf and a lamp for every pilgrim. Pilgrims became couriers, couriers became confessors, and confessors learned what each court wanted. It holds almost no army and needs none.

Its rulers know what the world does not: that the Deliverer is Afrit, that the Hidden Council meets here and in Parim, and that the old king must die. [secret] The public face is a quiet temple-state led by a High Confessor, with a pilgrim guard in white tabards. Its border is whatever the elves allowed, three valleys that the Magical Forest lets it keep. It trusts its pilgrims. It fears the elven wardens, who ask no questions and remember Varner. Everything it does at the start must be a kindness.

### Lerendor and Lerenwaith

Two northern crowns, one Leren speech, one sea. The Leren sailed with Aenor's first ships and stayed independent. In 4A 411 [P] their royal houses married, and the united crown made Sonargur its seat on the strait [W]. The Treaty of the Narrow Strait of 4A 512 [P name; E "old alliance"] binds the North to defend Aenor. A toll quarrel in 4A 1716 [P] has made the Leren court doubt the treaty.

The crown is proud, quick to insult and strong at sea. Its Speakers' Hall of shipowners decides what the king may spend, and its fleet decides what the strait costs everyone else. It trusts the little Leren Compact and Eekedor [W]. It fears Aenor's disdain, the orc holds across the Dark Straits and, from the black north, whatever the fishermen report. Its border is water: the Narrow Strait on the south, the Northern Dark Strait on the east, and the Loron highland between its two halves.

### Volgan

A Great Spirit of immense age and the main dark lord [E]. He once served a New God and overcame it [E]. His First Age conquest began at Mirolon; he fell at Dardan and was banished to the Void [E]. Elzon freed his presence in the Second Age [E]. Through the Third Age he stayed in the Dead Lands. In the Fourth Age he has rebuilt his strength behind the Dead Lands' storms for a century and means to take Mirolon and march north [E].

His Dominion is a mixed host: grave hosts, Netherborn demons, orcs, ogres, monster clans and necromantic orders [E]. It is not the Dark Lands, whose holds are independent [E]. He trusts Demon, Dolon and Nezer, who are sworn to him [W]. He fears nothing he admits to. His border is the storm belt on the coast and the heat in the interior; Mirolon's northern passes are the one gate he must take.

### Zol's Mistake

The black north was already desolate before Aen [E]. Zol, the Old God of Death and the only Old God in Erdae, cannot take a body; his servants sustain his presence [E]. His power grows slowly [E]. The realm is nearly empty of the living and roamed by the dead, who are sustained by his own magic and cannot be ended by ordinary arms [E]. The seat is Furviatlon [W].

Who administers the realm for Zol is unknown [O], and this bible does not say. It has no court, no declared faith and no named leader: its leader is an emblem [S4]. Its border is the black sea and the black earth. Fishermen of the Ocean of Zol tell of black ships, and nobody at court believes them.

### The Magical Forest

The oldest of the elven realms, never conquered [E]. It rules by an elected council of houses [P] and a First Speaker who may serve for centuries. In the First Age its war-speaker Sirael [P] fought at Dardan beside Lorien. After Edran's folly in the Dead Lands the elves refused to send armies for the humans of Aenor [E]. In the Third Age they held their own wood against Elzon and the Pale March [E]. The Wall Accord of 4A 96 kept the Pale March as the Great Wall [P], and the elves have lived beside it since.

Now it is peaceful, learned and watchful. Its songs record every house and its loremasters remember Varner. It commands the dragons of Dragon Mountain, as elven powers can [E]. It trusts the other Kindred and Gala. It fears men who break alliances. Its borders are the Great Wall on the west and the high passes on the south; the rift sea is its east.

### The Gala Elves

King Lorien led the Gala elves into Edran's Grand Alliance and fought at Dardan [E]. He is of extraordinary age and has watched Aenor fall and rise [E]. His court, in the far south-west, keeps the Mirror of First Rain, a spring that shows true traces of the past and uncertain futures [P]. The Gala elves share the Lands of the Sun with the Sunhot elves, but their relation to the Sunhot elves is open [O].

The Gala court is small, old and proud. It does not trust human kings who turn a common victory into a crown [E, Lorien's view]. It fears the return of an Old God more than any war. It trusts the Sunhot realms to remember. In 4A 1752 [P] a stranger will come to its shore, driven there by storms.

### Coldor

The oldest people of the north-west, the Winter-Crown folk, were there when Aen's charts arrived, and Aenoran ships gave their land a name. In 4A 850 [P] Coldor's kings united the frozen interior under one crown, and Isengard refused. The western ports of the Cold Lands trade with the West Lands across the neck.

Coldor rules by grain and fire: it feeds the clans in hard winters and keeps the Frost-Hall's fire lit. It leads the Northern Accord with Horidor [W]. It trusts Horidor and the grain its fields send. It fears Isengard and the Long Dark. Its borders are mountains: the Frozen Mountains and the Cold Mountains hem it in, and the Fandan River marks the line with Isengard.

### Noris

The camel kings of the Noris desert hold the wells. Their tribes crossed the dry plain beside the rift sea in the first millennium and learned where water sleeps. When the caravan roads opened between 4A 600 and 780 [P], the wells became toll-houses and Noris became rich. Its seat is Furvior [W]. Noris leads the Noris Treaty with Gondolin [W].

The king is war-leader of the tribes, chosen by the well-lords from one line. The desert is its border: the Salt Road runs from its wells to the Hot Lake pans, and the Sand Road southwest across the Sun Desert. It trusts Gondolin and its wells. It fears Stabilo, whose Gold Road undercuts it, and the temples in every city that no one thinks to question. [secret]

### Stabilo

The merchant houses of the southern cities grew on the Gold Road. They hold their power by a bench of houses, and the Prince of Stabilo is only first among them. Their seat is Postondoroth [W]. They lead the Stabilo Pact with Drak [W]. Their law is the ledger, and their crime is the false weight.

Stabilo trusts Drak and every contract it has a copy of. It fears Noris, whose camel riders can cut the roads in a week. Its borders are the roads themselves: the Gold Road to the north and the Sand Road to the west, with the Hellfire Range at its back.

### Gunwaith

Hill clans met Aenor's settlers in the First Age and fought them for generations. Gunwaith made peace with the grudge, not with the claim. Its commander Taryn [P] fought at Dardan under Gunwaith's own banner and demanded recognition for the West before she took a sword for Edran. After the Gunwaith-Girdon wars from 4A 1702 [P], the Truce of the Grey Ford of 4A 1745 [P] was signed.

The kingdom is the northern crown of the West Lands. It is led by clan chiefs and bound by the Lawspeaker. It trusts Buldor's companies and the standing stone. It fears Aenor's tariffs, Girdon's wardens and a day when the old claims are renewed. Its borders are the neck to the Cold Lands on the north, the Grey River at the Grey Ford on the south, and the Crown Ocean on the east.

### Goldoroth

Orc warlords united the inner Dark Lands about 4A 1450 [P] around the Fire-Mountain above Cocanoor [W]. The Volcano Clans march under a Warking acclaimed by the chiefs. Goldoroth wants the whole region and has not the strength to take it. Its wars with Dondor began at the union and have been an armed peace since.

It trusts nobody. It fears Dondor, who holds the iron road, and the free holds, who will not pay. Volgan's envoys court it and it has not yet chosen. Its borders are the ring of the Goldoroth Mountains and the Darkness ridge on the north.

### Mirolon

The lake-country of Mirolon was Volgan's first conquest in the First Age [E]. Freed after Dardan, it became an elective warden-realm [P] of lake towns and beacon towers. It fortified its northern passes about 4A 1650 [P]. In 4A 1736 [P] its envoy warned Parim that the Dead Lands were arming and was laughed at.

The wardens know what Parim does not. They trust their scouts and their cords. They fear Volgan in silence. Their border is the Mirolon Passes on the north; the Mirolon Sound and the coast to the south; behind them is the Lake of Mirolon and its Great Swamp.

### Dondor

A kingdom of men in the northern Dark Lands, its Brutes hold the forest and the iron road against the orcs [W]. Its seat is Notbuton [W]. Dondor leads the Dark Sea Accord with Belonton [W]. Rovek of Dondor, a proposed commander [P], fought for the Grand Alliance in the First Age, and the kingdom remembers that not all of the Dark Lands served Volgan.

It trusts Belonton and its iron. It fears Goldoroth. The map gives it one state today and the working table calls it a great power; the foundation spec proposes growing it [owner short list 8]. Its borders are the Dondor Forest and the Dondor Pass on the south, the straits on its other sides.

### Yeldenne

A castle in Mp'E, the frozen north of the Cold Lands. Horos, a Great Spirit and enormous knight in yellow-green armour with a blue sword, keeps it [E]. In his chest he carries Glo, a green crystal holding part of the power of Glernaen, a New God [E]. It is Horos's own heart [E]. The castle's other inhabitants are not stated [O].

The castle trusts nobody. It fears whoever comes for Glo, and in the late Fourth Age three will [E]. Its border is a mountain: the Yeldenne Heights ring the castle on every side but the one the ice leaves open. Players read only "a crystal of old and holy power" until the guardian dies. ## 7. The world as a whole

### Why the borders lie where they do

Borders follow the first thing a person cannot cross with a cart. The geography model ([05_geography_model.md](../01_map/05_geography_model.md)) lists the natural lines; the bible gives the human reason. A border is a **water line** where one people kept a river (Coldor and Isengard on the Fandan River; Gunwaith and Girdon on the Grey River at the Grey Ford; the Narrow Strait between Lerendor and Aenor), a **mountain line** where a people held the passes (the Great Wall; Nolorim's passes; the Mirolon Passes; the Goldoroth Mountains), a **faith line** where a custom would not cross (the Veiled in their sour ground), a **charter line** where a king's seal fixed an old arrangement (the Charters of Parim; the Wall Accord) or a **toll line** where a road is worth more than a field (Noris and Stabilo on the caravan roads).
