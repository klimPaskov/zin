# Realm roots and signs: the West Lands

**Status:** proposed (owner decision K67, 25 October 2026, asked for this lore; none of it is adopted yet). **Priority:** 2 (lore). **Phase:** 2 (text), with play hooks for later implementation.
**Source of the lore:** the `roots` and `sign` fields of every realm in [`tools/content/data/world/history/west_lands.json`](../../../../tools/content/data/world/history/west_lands.json), labelled `proposed`, and the new events that join the roots to 4A 1748. The history file is the text of record; this file summarises it and turns it into play hooks.
**Companions:** [10_fallen_realms.md](../10_fallen_realms.md), [06_heraldry.md](../../08_art_and_assets/06_heraldry.md), the dossier [west_lands.md](../../../lore/regions/west_lands.md#roots-and-signs) and the vault note [Roots of the West Lands](../../../lore/vault/07%20-%20History/Roots%20of%20the%20West%20Lands.md).

## How to read this file

The rules of the [Middle Lands file](middle_lands.md#how-to-read-this-file) hold here: every sign is one sewable emblem born of a deed, a relic or a custom; no god's device, letter, cross, real flag or named person; branch names are working labels; no fallen realm returns as a tag and no hook grants free cores or answers an open question.

The West's roots share one grievance and differ in everything else. The downs clans walked south out of a failing northern country and hold that land belongs to whoever sows it; the settler towns began as Aenor's and broke with it, each in its own way; the dwarves lost the deep roads; the small folk fed their neighbours. The signs keep those stories apart, so that the West no longer reads as one region in one style.

## Realm by realm

### Gunwyn (GNW), tree `gunwaith`

**Roots.** The clans keep the song of Faenn-Cheulu, the lost northern country of sun and fields where the winters lengthened until they left. The rooks led them south over the neck to where barley would grow, and the Rookwyn are the clan that walked behind the rooks. Land belongs to whoever sows it, the king rules by the clans' law, and Gunwyn has fought every war as a people once driven out who swore not to be driven out again.
**Sign.** The rook with the barley-ear: we sow it, so it is ours.

**Focus branches** (extending the 117-focus tree):
1. *The Song of Faenn-Cheulu* (beside the Lawspeaker's Rock): the clan singers become a branch of memory. Gathering the song's verses across the clans (missions to Werlendor, Lambin, Thenon and the Neck Road) builds a cultural spirit and, at its end, a knowledge-gated discovery: that the Coldic, Gunwyn and Tritonic peoples share words for king and reef, the first step of the Sunfields' discovery in the fallen realms spec. It answers nothing about the Sunfields beyond that.
2. *Sown, Not Found*: the doctrine that land belongs to the sower becomes the West's answer to Aenor's claims. A decision family that lets the king publish the Sown Right in every western court (relations with the guild ports fall, the clans rally), and that gives a strong casus belli against any Aenoran settlement claim during the Crossing question.
3. *The Rook-Hide*: Taryn's banner of the war against Volgan; raising the rook-hide again calls the western host under the Charter of Free Banners, with a war-support gain and an obligation to fight under the clans' own commanders.
4. *Never Driven Again*: the war-defence branch: when an enemy lands on the downs, the clans burn nothing and give up no field; strong defensive spirits on sown states and a refusal of any peace that cedes a downs state.

**Restorable identity: the Clans of the Rook.** Name: *the Rook Kingdom of the Downs* (cosmetic on GNW). Entitlement: the walk over the neck. Who: Gunwyn after the Song branch. Requirements: the Oath-Ring held, the truce of the Grey Ford made permanent or Girdon brought into a lasting compact, and Werlendor reconciled or subject. Changes: the king's acclamation by the clan song before the Lawspeaker; a spirit for sown-state defence; the Neck Road becomes a seasonal route for the clans' trade north, and the Coldic courts give an opinion bonus for the shared words.

**Events and decisions.** The rooks returning to the Oath-Ring in spring (a good or bad omen read by the singers); a guild-port envoy citing Aenor's letters of finding; the song sung at a funeral.

### Girdon (GIR), tree `regional_girdon`

**Roots.** The plain's oldest law is the ford-share and the loaf fed to the river at high water. In 1A 640 each warden carried a stone from his own ford to Arligon and laid it in the river, making one line of stepping-stones that any warden can cross dry; each took his ford's name. No king, because no stone stands higher; blood-price before revenge; and a stone pulled from the line at the Grey Ford in 4A 1702.
**Sign.** The five stepping-stones: the crossings are held together or not at all.

**Focus branches:**
1. *The Missing Stone* (beside The Blood-Price and A Lasting Peace): the Grey Ford's stone is still black on the banner. Paths: Gunwyn pays the old blood-price and the stone is relaid (a peace payoff and a ceremony event at Arligon), the wardens take it back by force (a war-goal on the Grey Ford), or the line is redrawn without it (a lasting grudge spirit).
2. *A Stone for Every Ford*: new fords and ferries can join the commonwealth by laying a stone at Arligon. Wennarith's ferry-wardens can be offered a stone (the union path the existing divergence names), and so can the Lothoro riders.
3. *Feed the River*: the river-loaf becomes a flood mechanic; wardens who keep the rite get fewer flood losses, and a warden who skips it in a flood year faces the river-court.

**Restorable identity: the Line of the Fords.** Name: *the Commonwealth of the Fords* (cosmetic on GIR). Entitlement: the oath at the ford-stone. Who: Girdon. Requirements: the Grey Ford's stone relaid by peace or war, Wennarith joined by laying its stone, and every warden of the Grey River's fords sworn. Changes: the Warden-Speaker presides over a larger river-court that judges Wennarith's crossings too; a spirit for river crossings and supply along the river.

**Events and decisions.** A warden's death and the lifting of his stone; a ford-keeper's town spared in a flood.

### Werlendor (WER), tree `shared_west_lands`

**Roots.** The Erg clans came south by sea along the coast while the downs clans walked, kept their own oath-stone and married the Rookwyn rather than bowed. Dyfan, they say, broke his king-torc for his two sons; the moot refused to share the realm, and Emric carried his half to Caer-Erg.
**Sign.** The half-torc: the realm was meant to be shared.

**Focus branches** (gated to WER in the shared West Lands tree):
1. *The Other Half*: the half-torc becomes the claim. Paths: demand the other half from Bulligon (a casus belli for the hall, which Gunwyn denies exists), offer a marriage that would join the halves (reconciliation), or forge a new whole torc of Werlendor's own and drop the claim (a stability gain and the Gunwyn opinion rises).
2. *The Boats along the Coast*: the Erg clans' sea memory becomes a coastal branch with fishing fleets, coast-steadings and raiding boats.
3. *The Coast-Steadings*: settle the steadings taken in 4A 1724, by trade, by return for the half-torc's recognition, or by holding them in war.

**Restorable identity: the Torc Made Whole.** Name: *the Two Halls of Bulligon* (a personal union of WER and GNW under one king, through marriage or conquest; owner question whether a union may be formed by conquest). Entitlement: Dyfan's broken torc. Who: Werlendor or Gunwyn. Requirements: both halves of the torc held, the Oath-Ring and Caer-Erg both held, the moot of the downs and the Erg stone both acclaiming. Changes: the torc rejoined in a joint flag of the rook and the joined torc; one king, two acclamations; the Erg's coast and the downs' riders under one crown.

**Events and decisions.** The half-torc worn at an acclamation; a Gunwyn singer claiming the torc was never broken.

### Lambin (LAB), tree `regional_lambin`

**Roots.** A banked field above Faennon was ploughed in common long before Aen, under the clans' law that no blood may be shed on sown ground, and its name echoes the song-name of the lost northern country of fields. Branorn set his plague-years granaries on the field's staddle-stones and sold to all.
**Sign.** The raised granary: bread for all and blood on none.

**Focus branches** (beside The Neutral Ground and Host the Peace Talks):
1. *The Heir Ploughs the Field*: the yearly ploughing of the common field becomes the lordship's legitimacy; an heir who ploughs with the clans watching keeps the neutral ground respected.
2. *No Blood on Sown Ground*: a decision to declare a battlefield of the West sown ground for a season, forcing a truce between any two western courts who accept Lambin's grain; the AI accepts it when hungry.
3. *Granaries for the Crossing*: Lambin's grain decides whether a western host can cross the sea; selling to the host makes the lord a partisan, refusing keeps the neutral ground.

**Restorable identity.** None; Lambin's power is that it is not a crown. Its ambition is the existing Peace of the West path, which this lore deepens.

**Events and decisions.** A feuding pair of clans meeting at the field; a court accusing the lord of feeding its enemy.

### Thenon (THN), tree `shared_west_lands`

**Roots.** The warm lowland's first folk grew olives and grafted boughs before the clans came over the neck, gave the hungry newcomers olives and married them. The Mother Olive of Tummin is the eldest graft. Thenon pays, bends and grafts rather than fights.
**Sign.** The grafted olive: we bend and bear fruit.

**Focus branches** (gated to THN):
1. *Grafted, Not Planted*: the lowland's custom of taking strangers in by marriage becomes a diplomacy branch of marriages into western courts, each a small permanent opinion and a dynastic claim.
2. *Pay in Olives*: tribute instead of war; a decision family that buys off a threatening neighbour with olives and cattle, at a cost in trade that rises each time.
3. *The Watch Kept*: the Ronbo Watch branch shared with Kradlon and Dobbum, with the grafted bough as the debt.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own; it shares the Ronbo Compact under Dobbum.

**Events and decisions.** A harvest that judges the king; the Mother Olive bearing fruit.

### Dobbum (DOB), tree `shared_small_folk`

**Roots.** The small folk have farmed the Ronbo basin longer than any western song, trust their hedges and their harvests, and believe that a household which shares is never robbed. In the famine of 3A 899 they carried the Famine Bread, stamped with each house's thumb, to the starving clans; the Ronbo Oath and the closed high passes followed.
**Sign.** The thumb-stamped loaf: what we give freely keeps us.

**Focus branches** (the Ronbo Watch branch):
1. *A Thumb on Every Loaf*: the Warden chosen by a thumb pressed into a loaf at Luddum; succession by the households' thumbs.
2. *Bread Without Price*: in a famine anywhere in the West, the basin can send the Famine Bread again; each sending creates a new sworn friend (a Watch guarantee) at a cost in the basin's stores.
3. *Kin in the Giants' Ring*: the letter chain with Tirlan from the other side.

**Shared formable: the Two Halves of One Table.** Name: *the Long Table* (a league of DOB and TIR, each keeping its tag). Entitlement: the grain-ship households of 1A 612. Who: Dobbum or Tirlan after the kin letters. Requirements: the letter chain finished, a ship found to carry a household each way, both basins safe. Changes: the two Wardens keep a place at each other's table; a shared spirit of harvest; Hirolon and the Ronbo Watch both treat an attack on either as an attack on their promise.

**Shared formable: the Ronbo Compact.** Name: *the Compact of the Famine Bread* (a defensive league of DOB, THN and KRA, each keeping its tag). Entitlement: the Ronbo Oath. Who: Dobbum. Requirements: the Watch at full strength, Thenon and Kradlon both sworn anew at the standing stone, Brundahrim's high passes still shut. Changes: the Watch becomes a joint force with its own commander; the basin pays it in grain and leaf; an attack on the basin calls all three.

**Events and decisions.** The stamped loaf in the moot-hall; a stranger asking to buy bread in a famine.

### Brundahrim (BRU), tree `shared_delving_holds`

**Roots.** The western end of the Stone Road and its western forge; when the western Sea-Door was sealed the hold lost its trade, kin and light, and carried out one fire, the Road-Fire, in an iron fire-pot. It blames Kunhrim, trusts no court, and made the West's first powder from that fire.
**Sign.** The fire-pot: the fire of the Stone Road still burns here.

**Focus branches** (Brundahrim's branch, beside The Fire-Hold):
1. *The Road-Fire*: the Road-Fire becomes a mechanic of the hold's forges; keeping it fed (charcoal, coin, saltpetre) gives a strong powder and bombard bonus, and letting it gutter in a siege is a disaster event.
2. *Kunhrim Must Own It*: the grudge path of the Stone Road moot; Brundahrim refuses every moot until Kunhrim owns the urging, and can name its price.
3. *The Fire-Pot over the Gate*: trade and closure as a visible state; the gate open (pot hung) or shut (pot taken down), with the AI shutting it at insults.

**Shared formable.** The Stone Road Reconvened (see the [Middle Lands file](middle_lands.md#kunhrim-kun-tree-shared_delving_holds)), with Brundahrim's condition that the Road-Fire lights the moot's forge.

**Events and decisions.** An elf at the Outer Gate; the Road-Fire guttering.

### Gromkerm (GRO), tree `shared_delving_holds`

**Roots.** The oldest of the western doors, which held its door open for the other roads' delvers before sealing it last; a hold that measures honour by doors. Its Outer Gate bore an iron fist-knocker until Saopon's hired companies tore it down in 2A 1712; the hold shut for three generations and recast the fist from the broken locks.
**Sign.** The clenched fist on the door: knock, and never force.

**Focus branches** (Gromkerm's branch, beside The Old Hold):
1. *The Wall of Grudges*: the names cut into stone become a ledger of grudges against Saopon's houses and Kunhrim; each grudge can be settled (a price paid) or kept (a modifier against that realm).
2. *Knock First*: no company within a bowshot of the gate; a mechanic that lets Gromkerm refuse any army passage and turns the bowshot rule into a siege defence.
3. *The Last Door Shut*: Gromkerm's claim to have held its door open for the others becomes its argument in the Stone Road moot, and its price for forgiving Kunhrim.

**Shared formable.** The Stone Road Reconvened, with Gromkerm's condition that Saopon pays for the looting first.

**Events and decisions.** A Saopon factor knocking on the empty staple; a debt struck off with a fist on the table.

### Saopon (SAO), tree `shared_west_lands`

**Roots.** The Kulolo boatmen poled dwarf iron to the lagoon and swore loads on their weight; the league of 1A 930 was sworn with a dwarf ingot on the guild book. Its shame is the looting of Gromkerm's gate.
**Sign.** The weighted book: a bargain is as heavy as its iron.

**Focus branches** (gated to SAO):
1. *Pay for the Looting*: Saopon can at last pay Gromkerm for 2A 1712 (a large cost in gold and a guild-house's pride); the payment ends the grudge, opens bombard trade and lets Gromkerm join the Stone Road moot.
2. *Iron on the Book*: every contract sworn with the ingot; a mechanic that makes Saopon's trade deals more binding and its broken deals costlier.
3. *The Fen Boundary*: the reed-beds dispute with Girdon, settled by the wardens' river-court or by Saopon's guild law.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own.

**Events and decisions.** The ingot missing from the book; a dwarf factor asking to see it.

### Buldor (BLD), tree `regional_buldor`

**Roots.** The plague emptied the valleys; the clerks of Toples kept rolls of the dead for heirs who never came; the landless men swore companies under banners sewn from the shrouds, and the first contract-book was written on the backs of the plague-rolls.
**Sign.** The seamed shroud-pennon: our dead march with us and our word is written.

**Focus branches** (beside The Company Hall and Open the Books):
1. *The Shroud-Banners*: each company's banner becomes its soul; a company whose banner falls in battle is dissolved (a real loss) unless a banner-chaplain carries the banner out, which a dedicated decision tries at a manpower cost.
2. *The First Pennon*: the Captain-General's pennon never carried to battle; carrying it once, in the great war, is a desperate decision with a huge war-support gain and the risk of the state's own end if it falls.
3. *Written on the Dead*: the contract-book's backs; a scholar reading the plague-rolls finds the names of the old valley lords, and Buldor can claim their lands as the heirs of no one.

**Restorable identity: the Free Companies of the Valleys.** Name: *the Valleys of the Banners* (cosmetic on BLD). Entitlement: the plague-rolls and the shroud-banners. Who: Buldor. Requirements: the plague-rolls read, the valleys south of the Fog and Eye range held, the first pennon never fallen. Changes: the companies settle the empty valleys as their own land; Buldor becomes a state of soldier-farmers with a weaker hiring trade and a stronger home defence.

**Events and decisions.** Two Buldor companies meeting in the field on opposite sides; the black seam sewn at a founding.

### Kradlon (KRA), tree `regional_kradlon`

**Roots.** The oldest standing settler harbour; it recorded Parim's dues on split hazel tallies, married into the Ronbo shore clans and broke its tally across in the Unpaid Year of 1A 912. It still argues with its origin, and now sells powder.
**Sign.** The broken tally: we owe what we choose to owe.

**Focus branches** (beside The Tariff Quarrel and Trade Before Grudge):
1. *The Tally Broken Again*: the tariff quarrel's climax; Kradlon can break a tally on Parim's tariffs (refusal to pay, a trade war) or split it (a new deal, the guilds divided).
2. *Half Clan*: the shore-clan half of the town; a branch that brings the Ronbo shore clans into the guild moot and sends more men to the Watch.
3. *Powder Owes Nothing*: the stamped powder-pieces; sell to all (gold, Brundahrim's displeasure if prices fall) or only to the West.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own; it shares the Ronbo Compact under Dobbum.

**Events and decisions.** The election on the day of the broken tally; a Parim tax-collector presenting an old tally.

### Corogon (CRG), tree `regional_corogon`

**Roots.** The first settlers of Oslodon landed Aenor's letter of claim to the West, sealed in black wax; every governor who used it made himself a prince. In 1A 940 the merchant houses swore never to break the seal and chose two consuls with a key each.
**Sign.** The sealed letter: no warrant rules here.

**Focus branches** (beside Consul Canak's Embassy and Tariffs and the Sea):
1. *The Two Keys*: the consulate's double rule; the two consuls can agree, deadlock or one can try to take both keys (a coup path that makes a prince and breaks the custom).
2. *Break the Seal*: a hidden branch. If Aenor's claim becomes the question of the Crossing, the consuls can open the letter; what it says is decided by a roll the owner may want to fix (a claim to the whole West, a claim only to Oslodon, or something stranger), and the West's reaction follows.
3. *A Cargo We Will Not Unload*: trade with Aenor without its claims; a merchant branch that keeps Aenoran trade and refuses every Aenoran letter.

**Restorable identity.** None; the consulate's ambition is that no one rules.

**Events and decisions.** An Aenoran captain bearing letters; the forgetful wizard Gwilid claiming to remember what the letter says.

### Varnondor (VRN), tree `regional_varnondor`

**Roots.** The cape clans kept fires on the headlands above their barrows to warn of settler sails; a cape that lets a ship pass unseen fails its dead. Oslin put the fires behind glass in iron lanterns in 2A 455.
**Sign.** The iron lantern: the capes are never surprised.

**Focus branches** (beside Lighthouses of the Capes and the Staging Yards):
1. *A Lantern on Every Headland*: each lit headland lantern gives naval intelligence on the approaches; the cape families keep them, and a dark headland is a failure the king must answer.
2. *Light Them Out*: the lanterns lead a western fleet east; the staging port path, with the lanterns as the Crossing's guide-lights.
3. *Lanterns for Parim*: the watch shared with Aenor; the capes tell Parim of every sail, at the clans' anger.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own.

**Events and decisions.** A ship passing unseen in fog; the king relighting the first lantern at his acclamation.

### Wennarith (WEN), tree `shared_west_lands`

**Roots.** The bend's ferry-families never cut a ferry-rope, because a cut rope drowns whoever is mid-river; the ferrymen swore themselves wardens in 2A 1250 on a length of the first rope. No host is carried to make war, and the warden-bond with Girdon dates from the Bend Flood.
**Sign.** The empty ferry-barge: every crossing for every traveller, none for an army.

**Focus branches** (gated to WEN):
1. *The Uncut Rope*: neutrality as a mechanic; Wennarith refuses passage to every army and loses trade, or carries one host and chooses a bank.
2. *A Stone at Arligon*: lay a stone in Girdon's line and join the fords' commonwealth (the existing union divergence).
3. *Ferries for the Fleeing*: in the great war, the ferries carry refugees and never soldiers; a stability and manpower gain from refugees.

**Restorable identity.** Shares the Line of the Fords under Girdon.

**Events and decisions.** A host arriving at a ferry-landing; the first rope's length sworn on.
