# Realm roots and signs: the North Lands

**Status:** proposed (owner decision K67, 25 October 2026, asked for this lore; none of it is adopted yet). **Priority:** 2 (lore). **Phase:** 2 (text), with play hooks for later implementation.
**Source of the lore:** the `roots` and `sign` fields of every realm in [`tools/content/data/world/history/north_lands.json`](../../../../tools/content/data/world/history/north_lands.json), labelled `proposed`, and the new events that join the roots to 4A 1748. The history file is the text of record; this file summarises it and turns it into play hooks.
**Companions:** [10_fallen_realms.md](../10_fallen_realms.md), [06_heraldry.md](../../08_art_and_assets/06_heraldry.md), the dossier [north_lands.md](../../../lore/regions/north_lands.md#roots-and-signs) and the vault note [Roots of the North Lands](../../../lore/vault/07%20-%20History/Roots%20of%20the%20North%20Lands.md).

## How to read this file

The rules of the [Middle Lands file](middle_lands.md#how-to-read-this-file) hold here: every sign is one sewable emblem born of a deed, a relic or a custom; no god's device, letter, cross, real flag or named person; branch names are working labels; no fallen realm returns as a tag and no hook grants free cores or answers an open question.

The North's crowns are Leren almost all, and the old risk was a region of ships and crowns that all look alike. The roots give each crown the one thing it did that no other did: the Leren followed a whale, Viktara weighed a sword, the cape took one fish from every boat, the margrave moved the stakes, the coast crowns forged one crown from two, the vale broke the envoys' axes, the Onp kept the herring's peace, the hills bound a horn, the dwarves cut a stair, the miners' king died with his pick, the lake judged on the deep water and the packmen came home with salt.

## Realm by realm

### Lerendor (LER), tree `lerendor`

**Roots.** The Leren descend from the northern kingdoms of Ainic-speaking men that stood beside Aen as partners. Their first captains learned the shoaled strait by following the strait-whale, and under the Ship-Bond a Sonarvik captain led the Aenoran hulls through behind a sounding whale; Sonargur stands where the whale turned. The Litany's peace of the whale, the strait dues and the Leren temper come from it.
**Sign.** The two flukes: we know the way through.

**Focus branches** (extending the 127-focus tree):
1. *Behind the Whale* (beside Steersmen of the Narrow Strait): every Leren pilot sails the channel once behind the whale; the branch turns the strait's pilots into a naval doctrine of the shoals, and the whale's coming in Thaw becomes the Ship-Blessing event with a naval bonus for the year.
2. *Partners, Not Subjects*: the Question of the Strait gains its oldest argument. A decision family sends the Sonargur court's chroniclers to Parim with the Ship-Bond rolls proving who led whom; the Golden Court's answer decides whether the treaty is honoured with pride, kept with resentment or broken with the rolls as a casus belli.
3. *The Whale Turns Its Back*: since the Toll of Parim the strait-folk say the whale has turned its back; a mood value of the strait's sailors that falls with every slight from Parim and rises with every honour, feeding the existing honour, supremacy and withdrawal divergences.
4. *Two Flukes, One Tail*: the two crowns of the union; Lerendor's North's estates can drift apart if the strait kingdom ignores them, and the branch keeps the tail whole through marriages and the Speakers' Hall.

**Restorable identity: the Crown of the Channel.** Name: *the High Crown of the Channel* (cosmetic on LER). Entitlement: the Ship-Bond pilot and the Sonarvik's harbour. Who: Lerendor. Requirements: both shores of the Narrow Strait held or bound by treaty (R'en, Fotan and the Aenoran shore), the strait dues taken from every hull for a year, the whale's peace never broken. Changes: Lerendor's king becomes Lord of the Channel; a spirit makes enemy fleets in the strait suffer heavy attrition from the shoals; the Leren Compact gains a strait fleet duty. No cores on the southern shore.

**Events and decisions.** A whale stranded inside the strait (save it at a cost, or the Litany is shamed); a foreign pilot claiming to know the channel; the Ship-Blessing.

### Eekedor (EEK), tree `regional_eekedor`

**Roots.** The North's granary; in 2A 1205 the grain-families met a raider host with the harbour steelyard and weighed the chief's sword against grain, silver and a siege's lives; the host took the silver and three captains stayed as guards. Everything can be weighed, and the Arbitration Court is the steelyard made a bench.
**Sign.** The steelyard: everything can be weighed.

**Focus branches** (beside The Arbitration Court and Ledgers at War):
1. *Weigh the War*: before a war near the vale, the council can weigh it; a decision that tells the player the cost of a siege in manpower and supplies and offers to buy the enemy off at a calculated price.
2. *Hired Guards*: the three captains who stayed; the vale's defence is hired, a branch of mercenary contracts with Buldor's companies across the sea and with northern swords.
3. *The Steelyard Bench*: the Arbitration Court's judgements become a service for crowns with feuds; each judgement brings a fee and a debt of the losing crown's opinion, in rivalry with Dolpar.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own; the vale's ambition is to be the North's bank and bench, which the existing tree already carries.

**Events and decisions.** A weight found false on the old steelyard (a scandal); two crowns bringing the same feud to Eekedor and to Dolpar.

### R'en (REN), tree `regional_r_en`

**Roots.** Cape fisher-clans took one fish from every boat under their rocks and called themselves cormorants; Torik of the Farnur, the Cormorant, refused the strait kings their payment for his forts in 1A 1240 and made himself prince. Two masters, owned by neither.
**Sign.** The cormorant with a fish: one fish from every boat, and no master.

**Focus branches** (beside Between the Two Masters and The Gateway Question):
1. *One Fish from Every Boat*: the light toll as a mechanic; keeping it light keeps every flag coming, raising it angers both masters.
2. *The Empty Beak*: shutting the strait at the cape forts; a decision that closes the gateway (the cormorant's beak empty on the banner variant) and makes R'en the war's first target.
3. *Twelve Centuries at the Gate*: the princes held Farnardur against the Order; a fortress branch whose payoff is a defence spirit on the cape.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own.

**Events and decisions.** A captain refusing to take the cormorant token; a marriage offer from both Parim and Sonargur in one season.

### Molot (MOO), tree `regional_molot`

**Roots.** The march faced raiders out of the Dark Lands who came through the marsh; Helmund of the Ostvar's men moved the path-stakes by night and drowned a host. The margraves show the true path only to friends, and stake the march between Sonargur and Parim the same way.
**Sign.** The crooked stake: the way through is ours to show.

**Focus branches** (beside The Balance of the North):
1. *Stake the Marsh*: each spring the margrave stakes the marsh-paths; a defence mechanic that makes the marsh nearly impassable to an enemy who has not been shown the path, and dangerous to a friend who is shown it wrong.
2. *Moved Stakes*: a war-trap decision that lures an attacking army into the marsh, with a chance of a great drowning and a risk of being seen.
3. *Kin of the Ostvar*: the claim of kinship with Fotan across the strait, an envoy chain shared with Fotan.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own.

**Events and decisions.** A stranger caught moving a stake; the margrave driving the first stake in spring.

### Keredor-Hellekton (KER), tree `shared_north_lands`

**Roots.** Two coast crowns grown from Leren coast-holds walled against the sea-orcs fought three generations over the pass of Belg; the Arbitration Court married their queen and king in 4A 1344, and the smiths forged one double crown of Keredor's silver and Hellekton's gold, worn twice at every crowning.
**Sign.** The double crown: two crowns, one realm, and neither above the other.

**Focus branches** (gated to KER in the shared tree):
1. *Turn the Banners*: the double crown's balance as a mechanic; every decision that favours one coast must be matched for the other within a year, or the neglected coast's council grows restless.
2. *The Night Ride*: the twice-crowning ride between the two halls; a succession event that can go wrong (the heir delayed, one hall crowning alone) and split the realm.
3. *Notle Remembered*: the debt to Sonargur for burning Notle; a Compact-duty branch against the sea-orcs of the Dark Shore.

**Restorable identity.** None; the double crown is already the realm's form.

**Events and decisions.** A dispute over which hall crowns first; a sea-orc raid on one coast while the court sits in the other.

### Tolvael (TLV), tree `regional_tolvael`

**Roots.** Timber-folk of the long vale whose Kerhil pine heartwood breaks common axes. When the coast crowns proposed to share the vale out in 1A 1360, every envoy's axe broke on a Kerhil log, and the landholders crowned Brann of the Agtos seated on it.
**Sign.** The unsplit log and the broken axe-head: the vale will not be split.

**Focus branches** (beside Tolvael Answers to No Crown and The Free Crowns of the North):
1. *The Kerhil Heartwood*: the hard pine as a craft secret; ship timbers and siege engines of Kerhil wood that strengthen the strait fleets, sold or withheld.
2. *Asked, Not Told*: every request from the High Crown is a decision of its own; asked politely, Tolvael gives more than was asked; told, it gives nothing.
3. *The Axe-Heads above the Door*: a new king strikes the log; the vale's younger sons get timber rights, and a branch keeps them from becoming rivals.

**Restorable identity: the First of the Free Crowns.** Name: *the Free Crowns of the North* (a league led from Agtosai, each crown keeping its tag; the existing focus of that name is its gate). Entitlement: the first crown that refused to be shared. Who: Tolvael. Requirements: the Call the Small Crowns focus, at least four free crowns sworn (Tolhon, Heodwa Lands, Dolpar, Mina or others), and none of them a subject of Sonargur. Changes: a league of free crowns that answers the High Crown together; the league's war-horn is not Heodwa's, and a war on one free crown is a war on the league.

**Events and decisions.** An envoy's axe striking the log; a younger son demanding land.

### Tolhon (TOL), tree `shared_north_lands`

**Roots.** The Onp fishers kept the Herring Peace long before the princes: no feud, burning or toll while the herring run. Ulvek of the Ishor swore it as the coast's first law at the bridge of Sirtum in 4A 260; the herring's return under the prince's ban in 4A 1610 is the principality's pride.
**Sign.** The herring over the arch: peace in the run, toll at the bridge.

**Focus branches** (gated to TOL):
1. *The Herring Peace*: no war is begun in the run; a mechanic that gives the coast a fishing bonus while it is at peace in the season and a stability loss if war comes in the run.
2. *The Summer Nets*: keep the ban or lift it; lifting it gives a short gain and risks the herring leaving again.
3. *The Bridge of Sirtum*: the toll-bridge and the Mustnom quarrel; let the packmen back across (news and goods) or keep them barred.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own.

**Events and decisions.** The herring late one year; a war declared on Tolhon in the run.

### Heodwa Lands (HEO), tree `shared_north_lands`

**Roots.** Three hill crowns that fought each other for centuries shared one custom, the war-horn on the hill-top. In 3A 806 they swore the Hill Pact with the dwarves on a great horn that the dwarves bound with three gold rings, so that whoever blows it at Pand calls the dwarves and all three crowns.
**Sign.** The banded horn: blow, and the hills and the mountain answer.

**Focus branches** (gated to HEO):
1. *Blow the Horn of Pand*: the Hill Pact as an active call; blowing the horn summons Hrakbrund and all three crowns' levies into a defensive war, once per war, and blowing it for a hill crown's own quarrel breaks the council.
2. *Three Rings, Three Voices*: the hill council's three crowns can disagree; a mechanic of council votes in which Heodwa, Nuatax and Agniy each have a ring and the Mulchy Lands a cord.
3. *The Coming-Down*: the shared branch with Hrakbrund's stair; a joint defence of the valleys.

**Restorable identity.** None; the hill council is the realm's form.

**Events and decisions.** A hill crown asking to blow the horn for its own feud; the horn found cracked.

### Hrakbrund (HRA), tree `shared_delving_holds`

**Roots.** The northern arm of the Stone Road, which sealed its road under the strait and has lived since by the men of the island; it learned that a hold should not wait in its mountain while the world burns, and cut the Coming-Down Stair after the Hill Pact.
**Sign.** The descending stair: the mountain comes down for its friends.

**Focus branches** (Hrakbrund's branch, beside The White Hall and The Hill Pact):
1. *The Coming-Down Stair*: the stair as a road that lets the dwarves reach any hill crown in a day; a spirit of fast reinforcement into Heodwa and a decision to march down in the great war.
2. *Bombards down the Stair*: the High Crown's bombards; sell more (the hold richer, Sonargur stronger) or hold them back for the hills.
3. *The Northern Sea-Door*: the sealed road under the strait; the names read at the door, and Fotan's question about the knocking, which the hold answers with silence.

**Shared formable.** The Stone Road Reconvened (see the [Middle Lands file](middle_lands.md#kunhrim-kun-tree-shared_delving_holds)); Hrakbrund is the hold most willing to sit, and its price is that the moot never forbids a hold to trade with men.

**Events and decisions.** An elven envoy at Sonargur cooling the hold; a young dwarf's first walk down the stair.

### Mina (MIA), tree `regional_mina`

**Roots.** Leren miners dug the Etonia ore hills from the Second Age and walled up the smooth square workings no man cut. Stenolf of the Korn opened the Delvings in 4A 640; in the gallery fall of 4A 1567 the king went down to the rescue and died, his crown hung on his pick.
**Sign.** The crowned pick: our kings dig.

**Focus branches** (beside The Delvings and Deep Shafts):
1. *The King's Season*: every king works a season in the galleries; a yearly decision that costs the king's time and gives the miners' loyalty and output, and a king who skips it is not crowned on the pick.
2. *The Square Workings*: the walled-up workings; leave them walled (the custom), open one (a dangerous mission whose findings the owner should decide, with the dwarves of the High Mountains asking that it stay shut), or sell the knowledge to Hrakbrund.
3. *Iron for Whom*: the existing iron for Sonargur or Parim question, sharpened by the Gallery Fall's memory: a king who sells the miners' iron to a war they do not want faces a strike in the galleries.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own; it can join Tolvael's free crowns.

**Events and decisions.** A gallery breaking into the square workings; the coronation on the pick.

### Dolpar (DOL), tree `shared_north_lands`

**Roots.** The deep lake that its people say has no bottom; in the Raiding Years the four walled towns judged their quarrels on the water, four boats lashed together in the lake's middle, and a judgement sworn on the deep water sinks whoever breaks it. The lake-court of 4A 1580 revived it.
**Sign.** The four-oared boat: judged on the deep water, and sunk if broken.

**Focus branches** (gated to DOL):
1. *Judged Afloat*: the lake-court's judgements as a service in rivalry with Eekedor; each judgement given afloat is more binding (a stronger opinion effect) and cheaper than the vale's.
2. *One Oar Each*: four towns, four voices; a council mechanic in which the Hold, Stork, Cinargo and Solenkovi each pull, and a town that pulls against the others slows the league.
3. *The Lake Has No Bottom*: a folk mystery branch of the lake's depth; divers and soundings that find only darker water, and a lake-court custom of dropping a broken judgement's seal into it.

**Restorable identity.** None of its own; it can join Tolvael's free crowns.

**Events and decisions.** A crown that broke a lake-court judgement; a diver who went too deep.

### Mustnom (MUS), tree `shared_north_lands`

**Roots.** The dwarf goblins are a people of their own who say only that they came out of the hill when the road called. In 4A 1497 the first packmen came home from the long road's end with a sack of salt; the boss poured a pinch to thank the road and kept the rest, and the Boss's Due was born.
**Sign.** The pack-frame and the salt-bag: the road gives and the cave takes.

**Focus branches** (gated to MUS):
1. *The Long Road*: the packmen's road as a network across the island and, by Leren ships, beyond; each road opened gives trade and news.
2. *Salt at Every Crossroads*: the pinch of salt left for the road's spirits; a mood mechanic of the packmen's luck on the road.
3. *News Three Days Ahead*: the packmen's intelligence; sell news to Sonargur, to Parim or to whoever pays most, with the risk of being caught selling to both.

**Restorable identity.** None; Mustnom's ambition is the road, not a crown.

**Events and decisions.** The Homecoming before winter; a band that does not come home.
