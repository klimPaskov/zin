# The fallen realms of Aoaz

**Status:** Adopted by the owner on 25 October 2026 (decision K64, on the design addendum [k62_fallen_realms.md](../../plans/deep_history/k62_fallen_realms.md)). **Priority:** 2 (lore). **Phase:** 2 (text). The mechanics that use these realms are not written yet; the addendum's section 9 names their future spec homes.
**Companions:** [05_world_bible.md](05_world_bible.md) (the peoples and the ages), [04_fourth_age_history_for_play.md](04_fourth_age_history_for_play.md) (the Fourth Age as its peoples remember it), [knowledge_layers.md](../../lore/references/knowledge_layers.md) (the register of hidden facts, section 3.6 for the six realms), and the vault notes under [07 - History](../../lore/vault/07%20-%20History/) (one note per realm).

## Purpose

Aoaz's peoples are old, but its realms are young under their old names.
This spec says what the six realms of the deep past were, which heirs keep their traces, what each layer of knowledge may say about them, and how the game shows the years before Aen.
It is the play-facing summary; the vault notes hold the full lore, and the addendum holds the reasoning and the options the owner answered.

## Decision K64

On 25 October 2026 the owner adopted all six fallen realms and took the recommended option of every question in section 8 of the addendum, except where K64 says otherwise.
The adopted core is short.
Great realms of men stood before Aen, and none had one crown over all men.
Muzerar sank because its mothers overdrew the land's magic.
What broke Tarkia's bridge stays a mystery, with the temple and elven tellings in rivalry.
The Kalioth Crown ruled Herez, Noleksun and the South Pass for about a century under Volgan, and Erco may take its diadem in play, rare and risky.
Tromahrim lies under the Sea of Monsters, and a Sea-Door can be opened at great risk.
The Sunfields fell to lengthening winters.
Dates before Aen appear in game text only as "long before Aen".
The map gains one islet at the Mothers' Mound.
Chart islanders look like lean, mixed, weathered sailors of their own culture.

Nothing in K64 answers an open question of the vault (section 6 below).

## Ground rules

- **Aen made the first united kingdom of men (E).** No fallen realm of men had one crown over many peoples of men. Muzerar was ruled by a moot of mothers and had no crown. Tarkia and the Sunfields lie so far back, and so far from the Middle Lands, that they were realms of other peoples of men.
- **Open questions stay open (O).** What "Ainu" means, where Dardan was, why Zol's Mistake is called a mistake, which god Volgan served, where the dwarves first came from, whether the Gala and Sunhot elves are kin, how Eindil came to sleep, who built Castle Yeldenne, who carved Aen's name in the shrine beneath the tide, and how Aen was born. No realm answers them.
- **No exact age of any being (E, V23).** Dates are given for events and never for the life of a being. The Eldest Cairn's count measures years since an event, and a succession of keepers lays it.
- **The Magical Lands were never conquered (E), and the Dark Lands were never one nation (E).** No fallen realm is placed in the Dark Lands, in Zol's Mistake or in the burning lands beyond the South Lands (K25).
- **Aenor reached the West Lands only by ship (E).** The drowned Low Country never joined the West Lands to the Middle Lands, because the Blue Sea always lay between them.
- **No divine judgment.** No god drowns, splits or freezes any of the six realms.

## Names and who uses them

Each realm has one name in Common Speech and others in the tongues of its keepers.
The keepers use their own names, and a player learns each name only through the gate that the knowledge layers give it.

- **Muzerar** (Orran, the Veiled and the Redbark), **Ozelkak** (the city of the Moot, the cord-mothers), **Humaran** (the giants' name for the low sea), and **the Nimlan banks** (the sailors' name for the shoals) are four names for one drowned land.
- **Tarkia** (Alerhic), **Tarqis** (Noric), **Pontoseno** (Stabilic) and **Qadurum** (the city on the bridge) name the Bridge Kingdom and its city.
- **Rhabtarkia** is the empire's own court name, **the Kalioth Crown** is the name the chroniclers use, and **Barzoth** is what the mine-folk of Erco call the ruin.
- **Gromatrak** and **Tromahrim** name the Stone Road and its great hall, and **the Deep King** is the holds' old title.
- **Margorodai** (Tritonic) and **Halgohavnai** (the Holy Harbour) name the league and its seat.
- **Heulufaenn** (reconstructed proto-Boreal) and its forms, **Heulfaenn** (Coldic), **Faenn-Cheulu** (Gunwyn) and **Heulofaennos** (Tritonic), name the Sunfields. **Tawrhalwe** is its king's hall.

The narrator never gives a fallen realm's name before its gate.
A realm learns the name Muzerar from the Veiled, not from the narrator.

## Realm by realm

### Muzerar, the Low Country

**Age.** The Age of Kingdoms, in about the last thousand years before the War of Men. Its height lay about 1,500 to 900 BA (P).

**Extent at its height.** The plain that now lies under the eastern two-thirds of the Crown Sea, from the Aenor Isle shore west to within a day's sail of Varnondor's island, north to the Sea of Monsters and the Goldoroth arm, and south to the Nesmael.
Aenor Isle and the Tinta Hills were its eastern heights.
On the mainland it held the river valleys of the western and central Middle Lands and the Konorar up to the red-oak forest by the Narrow Strait.
Hirolon's ring remained the giants' own.

**People.** Orran-speaking men, whose descent ran through mothers.
Every family belonged to a beast-clan: Wolf, Bear, Eagle, Horse, Raven and Water.
The Moot of Mothers at Ozelkak governed, with a seat for the eldest cord-mother of every clan.
For a war the Moot chose a war-chief and took the office back at the peace, so the Low Country never had a king or a crown.
Its law was kept on knotted cords, one knot for each field, debt, marriage and death.
The hill giants lived on the low hills as partners. They raised the long sea-dikes from field-stone and kept their cairn-fields on the high ground.

**What made it great.** Three harvests a year on the low fields behind the dikes.
A moot that governed more land than Aenor ever held, without a crown and without a long war between its clans.
The cord-reckoning, the first account-keeping of Aoaz, which counted every field and what a field had given.

**How it fell.** The mothers drew on the magic of the place to give the fields a third harvest and the cord-mothers their long rule.
Over the centuries the ground lost its strength and settled below the level of the sea, so it had to be held by ever higher dikes, and every field the sea took was tied as a black knot on the Moot's cord.
The giants' cairn-keepers warned the Moot, and the mothers divided between those who would stop and those who would draw harder.
The second party won. In the Night of the Sea, 620 BA, one storm-tide broke the last dikes, and the sea took Ozelkak and the low fields in one night.
The overdraw is the Low Country's own cause, and no god judged it.

**After the fall.** The surviving eastern provinces became the Orran kingdoms of the river valleys.
Their wars over a shrinking land ran on into the War of Men, which Aen ended by making one crown over the kingdoms.
Orran houses that would not kneel took their cords into the grey downs and the red-oak forest.
The giants withdrew into their ring and raised the Eldest Cairn, to which its keeper adds one stone in every year since the sea came.

**What survives.** A tongue, Orran, among the Veiled and the Redbark and under the Aenoran names of the Vale's rivers.
The mother-line law and the cord-mothers of the Cursed Lands and the Bloody Forest.
The Isle Houses of Aenor Isle, ruled by their eldest women, who choose their queen at the turn of the tide and give their dead to the tide.
The Low Country's last law is kept by a people who no longer speak its tongue.
Places: the tide-chambers of Orohaf, rock-cut halls that flood at high water; the Nimlan banks; the Mothers' Mound, the top of Ozelkak's greatest mound, which breaks the water as a reef west of Aenor Isle at the lowest spring tides; the Eldest Cairn in Hirolon; and the eldest barrow of the Cursed Lands, where the Last Cord lies.
Relics: the Last Cord, the Mothers' Stone and the dike-stones.

### Tarkia, the Bridge Kingdom

**Age.** Deep antiquity, with its height about 3,000 to 2,300 BA (P).

**Extent at its height.** Before the rift opened, the east coast of the Magical Lands and the East Lands were one landmass, joined by a broad isthmus where the Rift Sea now runs.
Tarkia held the western East Lands, the isthmus with Qadurum astride it, and the Bridge Road west along the southern edge of the elven woods, through the Zilin Hills and over the South Pass into the downs of Herez and Noleksun.
Its eastern provinces ran to the Melty Mountains and through the Melty Pass into lands beyond the edge of the charts.
The rift-coast elves were its neighbours and never its subjects: the road crossed their shore under a treaty of passage.

**People.** The one Eastern people before the roads, whose single tongue later parted into the desert, trade and river speech.
It was a river-temple civilization of stair-temples above the flood-line, scribes writing the reed letters from which the Alerhic script descends, and kings anointed at the river.

**What made it great.** The first great road of Aoaz, the first written law of the East, the stair-temples, the canals of its western march and the horses and cedar that it carried west.

**How it fell.** A great king left two heirs, the House of the Eastern Stair and the House of the Western Stair (working labels), and the realm split along its road.
In their war the isthmus broke, and the sea came in from the north and the south until the two shores stood apart across a long rift.
Qadurum broke in two.
Its western half lies under the fungal belt of the Mushroom Forest beside Luv-zhise, and its eastern half under the grass of the Delbiz coast, where the horse-fair is held among fallen pillars.
The western house faded among the hills of the Middle Lands' south-east, and the eastern half broke into river crowns, and the one tongue began to part.

**What broke the bridge.** **Mystery (kept).** The owner decided that the cause stays a mystery.
The temple chronicles of Alerhia say that the gods divided the land to end the brothers' war (belief).
The rift-coast elves' oldest rolls say that the western heir's sorcerers woke something that sleeps beneath the East, and the salt-boilers say it still breathes under the Hot Lake (disputed).
Neither telling is settled, and no text confirms either.

**What crossed before the Breaking.** The Eastern road-people reached the south-east of the Middle Lands and faded there. The horse came west over the bridge, and the riders of the Middle Lands and the horse-lords of Delbiz share a memory of it. The cedar came west as well. The River Temples faith crossed too, and the Lake Litany of Luv-zhise keeps it beside the twelve. Ogres crossed, which explains one people on both sides of a sea without making them sailors. The elves' treaty of passage is the Archive's proof that the elves were never subjects.

**What survives.** The Eastern family of tongues and its shared roots.
Alerhia's first stair, which the valley people raised above the flood-line so that the river and the gods would each have a place.
The River Temples faith, which holds the river to be the oldest god anyone can see.
The Two Gates, the halves of Qadurum on the two shores of the Narrows.
The old roads that end at Lenkei, where Eldafaen keeps its Archive and the treaty of passage.
Noleksun's ditches under its fields.

### Rhabtarkia, the Kalioth Crown

**Age.** The First Age, from about 1A 500 to its end, with its height about 1A 900 to 1500 (P).

**Extent at its height.** The southern East, from the Kalioth and the Plutondo valley over the slopes of the Smoking Teeth, the Hardtmuth passes and the northern wells of the South Lands, and through the Melty Pass along the edge road to eastern provinces that sent tribute over the glacier.
The empire never held Alerhia's river crown or the towns of the Lake Road.
From about 1A 1500, as Volgan's ally, it held the south-east of the Middle Lands, the downs of Herez and Noleksun and the South Pass, for about a century (adopted, K64).
Volgan formed alliances with rulers of the eastern and southern lands, and Herez is the march that first feels Volgan's march north.

**People.** Alerhic-speaking mountain kings, lords of mines and iron, who ruled mine-folk, Noric desert tribes and the pass-houses of the Hardtmuth.
It was a fortress empire of heavy horse, iron, slave-worked mines and walled upland cities.
Its kings called themselves the restorers of the Bridge Kingdom and swore to rejoin the shores.

**What made it great.** The greatest eastern power between the Bridge Kingdom and the caravan roads, a road over a glacier pass that nobody has kept since, a name for iron and siege-craft, and the only eastern realm that ruled land in the Middle Lands after the Bridge broke.

**How it fell.** To rejoin the shores its kings took Volgan's side and paid the Black Tribute, which Alerhia refused in 1A 1214.
Volgan's victories gave them the south-eastern Middle Lands.
At Dardan their host stood with Volgan's and died there.
After Dardan their western garrisons starved, and in 2A 12 the mine-lords of the southern mountains shut the passes against the beaten host and besieged the last king in his hall, and Erco was founded on the ruin.
The provinces beyond the Melty Pass fell silent. **Open (kept):** what became of them stays a mystery.

**What survives.** The Kalioth halls under Erco's seven forts on the old fortress line.
The last king's iron diadem, walled up in the hall of Barzoth by mine-lords who would neither wear it nor melt it.
The glacier road over the Melty Pass.
The Black Tribute in the Alerhic chronicles.
Miaed's pass-houses, which sold water and guides to Volgan's eastern clients.
In Herez and Noleksun, a word for eastern raiders and the stone forts that the occupiers built on the downs.

**The diadem in play.** Erco may take the diadem out of the wall. The adopted routes are three: walling it up again, which keeps the realm's peace; melting it, which keeps the mine-lords' oath of 2A 12; and wearing it, which makes the realm a rare and risky claimant to the Kalioth crown. The choice is a play matter, and the knowledge layers below keep its consequences for the mechanic spec.

### Gromatrak, the Stone Road

**Age.** Its first roads were cut in the Age of Kingdoms, beneath the low country while it was still dry land.
It outlived the drowning above it, because the roads ran in deep rock, and it was at its height in the First Age until its fall at the end of the age (P).

**Extent at its height.** The western ranges (now the holds of Brundahrim and Gromkerm), the Great Wall range (Kunhrim) and the High Mountains of the North Lands (Hrakbrund), joined by roads under the floor of the Crown Sea and under the Narrow Strait.
Tromahrim, the Great Hall and the seat of the Deep King, lay deep beneath the low country's northern hills, which are now the floor of the Sea of Monsters.

**People.** The dwarves of every great range, as one realm (decision K22: dwarves hold the great ranges of the West and North Lands and the Great Wall range).
The one tongue that every hold still speaks is the realm's legacy.
The realm says nothing about where the dwarves first came from, which stays open (O).

**What made it great.** It is the only realm that ever spanned three landmasses, and a dwarf could walk from the West Lands to the North Lands without seeing the sky.
It sold iron to Aenor through the Western Gate and sent its engineers to Dardan.

**How it fell.** In Volgan's war the Damned Spirits rose from beneath the mountains and came into the Stone Roads.
The Deep King ordered the roads under the sea broken and the sea let in, and Tromahrim drowned with the spirits inside it, while Kunhrim's delvers collapsed the tunnels at Dardan.
The four holds were cut off from one another and have had no Deep King since.
Who gave the order is disputed: Kunhrim says the Deep King ordered it, and the western holds say Kunhrim's lords urged it to save their own gate.

**What survives.** One tongue in four holds, the four Sea-Doors, the Halls Below the Halls, the Deep King's lamp, and the grudge between the western holds and Kunhrim.
Each hold keeps a sealed Sea-Door at the head of its old road.

### Margorodai, the Sea-Roads

**Age.** From its swearing at Halgohavnai about 1A 820, when Aenor's decline left the western sea-roads unguarded, to the gale of 2A 904 that ended it. Its height lay about 1A 1100 to 1675 (P).

**Extent at its height.** The three arcs and the Pupor, with harbours leased on the coasts of the West Lands and the Cold Lands, and the sea-roads from the Cold Lands ports to the Islands of the South.
It leased harbours and never conquered land.

**People.** The Tritonic chart-islanders of the three arcs, a Boreal people. They look like lean, mixed, weathered sailors of their own culture (K64).

**What made it great.** It carried the supply fleets of the Grand Alliance, under Senra of the Pupor Isles as its admiral (adopted, L3). It kept the first lit passages, drew the first great charts and built Halgohavnai's four moles on the reef shelf, a harbour where any ship of any flag paid one due.

**How it fell.** The Grey Breath, a sickness working name, came into Halgohavnai on the ships that served the league, and the league's own fleets carried it to every isle.
The outer isles emptied and corsairs took their harbours.
Halgohavnai was left to its dead and never relit, and the gale season of 2A 904 broke its moles, so that its walls became the Cursed Square, a square of reefs that wrecks ships.
The league dissolved into chart-houses, brokers and corsair captaincies.

**What survives.** Adel's Chart Hall, which descends from the league's chart-office.
The Drowned Names, read at the close of every gale season.
Raerby's banks-court, Pollos's strong-room, the Lamp-House of the Lens, the Ransom Tide law of the corsairs, and Nondondor's claim to rule the banks.

**The Cursed Square was Halgohavnai (adopted, L2).** The islanders hold that the Square is cursed, and Benan's corsairs will not sail into it (belief). Whether the Grey Breath still waits in the Square's wrecks is disputed. That the Square was a harbour at all is learned from Adel's oldest charts (gated by play).

### Heulufaenn, the Sunfields

**Age.** Deep antiquity, with its height about 3,500 to 2,500 BA and a slow decline over about a thousand years after (P).

**Extent at its height.** The southern and western Cold Lands when the ice lay far to the north: the Fandan valley, the interior that is now the Frozen Lands of Coldor, the west coast below the Icy Mountains, the neck and the Neridor Gap, the northern West Lands, and the nearer isles of the arcs.

**People.** The proto-Boreal men of the north-west, ancestors of the Coldic, Gunwyn and Tritonic peoples (adopted, S3).

**What made it great.** The first sea-craft of Aoaz, whose words for reef, strait, cape and tide are older than any chart.
Bronze, great timber halls and horse-herds in the Fandan meadows.
The first kings of the north-west, which is why *tawr*, "king", is a word all three Boreal tongues still share.

**How it fell.** No one worked this fall. The winters lengthened over many lifetimes, the ice came a little farther south in every age, the fields failed and the halls emptied, and the realm broke into clans (P, natural cause adopted).
Its people went three ways: south over the neck, where they became the Gunwyn clans of the West Lands; west by sea to the arcs, where they became the Tritonic islanders; and the clans who stayed and learned to live through the Long Dark, who became the Coldic ice clans.

**What survives.** The shared Boreal words.
The Winter Hearth rite, which relights every house-fire from the clan fire.
The Neck Road over the Neridor Gap, which the clans showed to Aenor's surveyors about 1A 400.
Timber halls and bronze ship-prows that the ice gives back in mild summers in the Frozen Lands.
The snowline fair at Fentene, read as the Sunfields' old border-market.
The people of Mp'E believe that the Watcher in the Hall is a sleeping king of the old north (belief only, adopted, S4). Horos stays what the vault says he is, and who built the castle stays open (O).

## When the lands were joined

The Bridge Kingdom's isthmus joined the East Lands, the Magical Lands and Mirolon until the Breaking.
The rift opened from both ends during the War of the Two Shores, and the Rift Narrows are the last throat of the isthmus.
The Stone Road joined the western ranges, the Great Wall range and the High Mountains of the North Lands under the Crown Sea and the Narrow Strait until the end of the First Age, so three landmasses were joined underground long after they were parted above.
Muzerar reached to within a day's sail of Varnondor's island, and the Blue Sea always lay between the Low Country and the West Lands.

The cairn count follows from the adopted date.
The count takes one stone in the year of the Night of the Sea and one in every year since, so with the Night in 620 BA the Eldest Cairn holds 620 + 6,816 = 7,436 stones on 1 Lumae 4A 1748.
The number is the only figure of the deep past that the game shows.

## How the game shows dates before Aen

- Dates before 1A 1 appear in game text only as **"long before Aen"**, with no year and no BA figure (K64).
- The one number learned in play is the Eldest Cairn's count of **7,436 stones on 1 Lumae 4A 1748**, which the Two Counts (the Cord and the Stone brought together) reveal. It is derived from the Night of the Sea in 620 BA.
- No text states the age of any being. The count measures years since an event and is laid by a succession of keepers, never by one giant.
- The BA dates stay in the lore vault and the lore documents as the authorial chronology.

## What the adopted lore does not add

- No fallen realm returns as a new tag or a full country, and the drowned land does not rise again.
- No discovery is world news. The deep past stays regional, and no super event is adopted for it (G4).
- No free cores and no claims on elven land. The Magical Lands stay unconquered.
- No second sunken realm. The Square is a broken harbour, the Stone Road a flooded road and the Bridge a broken neck of land.
- No exploration of the far east or of the lands south of the South Lands (K25).
- No new language family. The names use the existing dictionaries, and the Boreal reading of three names as one land is a discovery in play, not a data change.
