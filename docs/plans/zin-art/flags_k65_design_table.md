# Realm flags, K65 design table (25 October 2026)

Status: proposal for the lead's review. No image has been generated and no heraldry data has been written. Yeldenne (YEL) is retired and is not in the table, so 155 realms are listed.

Owner decisions that bind this table: K53 (culture, not region; every flag has something special; no real-world or LOTR emblems; each culture its own look), K63 (the Konori horse-head pilot is the style target: one bold stylised emblem, a deep field, a cultural hem), and K65 (culture, not region; handmade, so each banner can be cut and stitched by hand and read from a distance).

## How the devices were chosen

- Each device is the culture's sign: a founding deed, a dynasty or crown, an oath, a custom, a sacred beast, a relic or a people's own vessel. The "What it remembers" column cites the realm's history entry (file keyed by region in brackets).
- A device is a single cut-cloth shape with a stitched outline. Tools, weapons and vessels appear only as stylised signs (a rod, a crook, a halberd), never as scenes or bundles.
- Colours are at most four flat tinctures plus one metal thread. No shading, gradients or scenes.
- A device appears once in its region. Each culture's hand, hem and ground (table B) differ from every other culture of the same region, checked by script with no duplicates.
- Regional signs (the "Regional?" column) stay at 14 realms, each with a culture justification, inside the owner's "about 15" cap.
- Forbidden devices: no stars, crosses, fleur-de-lis, crescents, crenellation, roundels, disc-on-plain-field, fire or burning objects, raised fists, clasped hands, interlaced knots, equal bands carrying the device, literal botanical drawings, real arms, the twelve gods' devices, or any named person. The table's device text was scanned for these words.
- Only one crown appears per flag. The spec's three-crown trio (the Sweden look-alike) is not used.

## Method of the hand, hem and ground columns

Table B gives each culture's hand, hem and ground. The K53 triples were kept except for the items the checklist forbids, which are replaced as follows: interlaced becomes couched (no knotwork); vine becomes cord (no botanical sprig); tasselled becomes knobbed (no fringe at the edge); notched becomes scalloped (no battlement steps); diapered and lozenged grounds become woven (no fleur-like semé or lozengy field); strewn becomes plain (no dots); radiant becomes elongated (no sun rays).

Hand (how a device is cut and stitched): armorial is a bold silhouette with one stitched outline; angular is straight-cut pieces with angled edges; elongated is a tall narrow silhouette; jointed is a device built from separate cut pieces with a visible seam; scrolled has curled outer edges (no spirals); stamped is a block-printed outline in one contrast colour; stippled is an outline of small dots; applique is a cut shape laid on the ground and outlined with one stitch; couched is an outline laid with a cord.

Hem (the border row): chain (linked), cord (twisted), pearled (small round studs), scale (overlapping scales), dagged (jagged), scalloped (small round scallops), knobbed (small knobs at intervals), lozenge-row (a row of lozenges on the edge only).

Ground (the field habit): plain; woven (a faint basket-weave in one tincture); counter (counterchanged: the device split between the two field colours).

## Items for the lead to rule on

1. Tools, weapons and vessels used as signs. About 26 realms carry one: rod (SFT), crook (FRE), pipe (PAZ), sword (KIN, SNT), crossbow (KLU, KRA), sling (HER), halberd (BLD), picks (BRU, ERC), hammers (MLK, NIV, BRT, HEO), double-bitted axe (HRA), mace (DRA), spears (NLR, NAN), feud-blade (WER), hook-blade (GLI), pact-blade (DLN), sledge (HOR), boss ring (MUS), drinking horn (THN), toll-horn (MIE), tusk (DUL), bull's horns (DAK). The checklist lists tools, bows and spears as literal objects that fail; the brief allows a weapon or tool held as a sign. Each is drawn as one bold shape, but the lead must accept the class or ask for a change.
2. Ships. Seven realms use a single hull with the prow raised (LER, TIR, BLK, PLT, SOY, FNG, plus the canoe prow of VER). Ships are the seafaring cultures' own signs and are not counted as regional, although SOY is one of the 14 regional signs for its own reason.
3. Rings. NOL (three linked rings), WEN (ring on a rope), MUS (boss's ring), KER (two crowns joined by a ring), NND (captain's ring). None has a bar, so none is a roundel, but the lead should check the three linked rings of NOL at 41x26.
4. Trees and plants (justified, stylised, no leaves or veins): SEL (Moot Tree), KIL (eldest tree), ANR (three-trunked tree), MAG (Singing Grove), MSH (gilled dome). The checklist fails literal botanical drawings; these are trunks and a cap-shaped dome, not sprigs, but they need the lead's ruling because the regional cap is counted with them.
5. Canon guards. WOR shows one hand offering a loaf, which the spec allows but the owner may read as a hand gesture. TOR shows a plain standing stone with no cord (Ailtal's device is a cord-bound stone). NET shows the single pentagram allowed inside a demon sigil. PUV shows the Gate of Nafeher alone: the canon "ring of names" is left out because it would need lettering, which is forbidden. ZOL shows the broken concentric rings of the Will of Zol, which may read as a roundel at small size.
6. Crown-form devices: about a dozen realms (ORH, BRR, DND, GOL, NAA, OST, CLD, HLD, HEO, DUN, ELD, TLV), each showing one crown; KER is the only double crown. Two bells (AEN, MOS). The class count is for the lead.
7. The "star pattern" option in the brief is not used: stars are forbidden by the checklist and by spec 1.4.
8. The Konori horse head (K63 pilot) is kept as the style reference for steppe and the first regional sign. The pilot failed on naturalism in the previous round, so its prompt needs the handmade and stylised wording before generation.

Checks run on the table: 155 rows and 155 unique tags; every culture resolves to a row of table B; no hand, hem and ground triple repeats within a region; 14 regional signs with a justification each; device text free of the forbidden words listed above (the only match in the table is a history sentence, not a device).

## Rulings applied (lead review, 25 October 2026)

Spot-check fixes (lead, 25 October 2026): crowns kept only as a realm's own charge (AEN, KUN, GOL, DND, DUN, NAA, HLD, NEZ, NND); kingdom crown marks are dropped from all records; crenellated bands removed (KUN, BRR, CUR, HRA, CLD); the disc-on-bands (EEK) and the sling (HER) and oar-crown (ORH) redesigned; the diagonal horse of HNH is on a plain field; the magpie stands without its ring.

1. Weapons may be signs (sword, spear-head, crossbow, halberd, war-axe, war-hammer, sling, mace, club, feud-blade). Utensils, tools, vessels and structures are removed as devices: bells, pipes, crooks, rods, picks, ferry-poles, footbridges, gates, stairs, arches, towers, cairns, hall-blocks, wells and similar rows are replaced by beasts, mythic creatures, crowns or abstract marks.
2. Deep history (docs/specs/02_lore/10_fallen_realms.md): ERC shows its own goat and does not show the Kalioth diadem; KUN carries the Deep King's crown of Gromatrak (the Stone Road); ALR carries a leaping stag in river gold as the river crown's sign (the Bridge Kingdom's stair is no longer drawn).
3. Ships: four (LER, BLK, PLT, SOY), within the limit of five. NOL's three linked rings are replaced by three geese, two above one. ZOL keeps the Will of Zol drawn as broken arcs, large in the field, never a target.
4. Trees and the mycid dome stay as stylised single shapes: SEL, KIL, ANR, MAG, MSH.
5. Canon guards stand: WOR (hand offering a loaf, public arms of the hostels), NET (one pentagram in a demon sigil), PUV (the Gate of Nafeher, no ring of names), TOR (a plain stone).
6. No stars.

Regional signs (lead ruling 2, 25 October 2026): only the landscape, local fauna or goods as such are regional; a culture animal (founder's, oath or sacred beast) is a culture sign. Regional now: SEL, KIL, ANR, MAG, MSH (trees and the mycid dome), AUT (narwhal), SEI (turtle), NRS (camel), KEA (frigatebird), MIR (heron). Each needs its justification in the generator (k65_designs.mjs).
