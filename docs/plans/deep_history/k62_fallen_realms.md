## 1. The promise

The roster's realms already have histories, but almost all of them begin in the First Age or later, so the world feels young under its old names.
The owner asked for realms that were once very great and are now a shadow of what they were: a great kingdom now beneath the sea, empires of the East that reached the far east and into the Middle Lands, and a time when lands that are now apart were joined.

This addendum proposes six fallen realms, spread across the ages and the regions, each with its own people, its own kind of greatness, its own cause of fall and its own survivors, and ties them to about thirty realms of the roster.
In play the deep past is knowledge held in pieces by realms that distrust one another, places that can be reached at a real cost, relics that change what a realm can claim, and a few ambitions that are tempting and dangerous.
Most of it stays quiet, as K61 requires: a dive at a drowned hall is news for the diver and its neighbours, never for the world.

The six cover every kind of fall the lead asked to vary: drowning by a magical catastrophe of the realm's own making, a broken land bridge in a civil war, a lost war, a self-inflicted drowning to stop a spirit's release, a plague, and a slow decline that nobody worked.

## 2. Ground rules this addendum keeps

These keep the proposals from colliding with the vault, and the lead should hold the implementation to them.

- **Aen made the first united kingdom of men (E).** No fallen realm of men before Aen had one crown over many peoples of men. Muzerar was ruled by a moot of mothers and never had a crown, and Tarkia and the Sunfields lie so far back, and so far from the Middle Lands, that they are realms of other peoples of men in other lands. The owner may judge this differently (section 8, question G2).
- **Open questions stay open.** What "Ainu" means (V12), where Dardan was (V1), why Zol's Mistake is called a mistake (V2), which god Volgan served (C11 and C12, sealed), where the dwarves first came from (V11), whether the Gala and the Sunhot are kin (V16), how Eindil came to sleep (E2), who built Castle Yeldenne, who carved Aen's name in the shrine beneath the tide (Islands of the South, owner question 4), and how Aen was born. Nothing below answers them.
- **No exact age of any being (K18, V23).** Dates are given for events, never for the lives of beings. The Eldest Cairn's count (3.1) counts years since an event and is laid by a succession of keepers, never by one giant.
- **The Magical Lands were never conquered (E)** and **the Dark Lands were never one nation (E).** Tarkia's road crossed the elven shore by treaty, and no fallen realm is placed in the Dark Lands, in Zol's Mistake or in the burning lands beyond the South Lands (K25).
- **Aenor reached the West Lands only by ship (E).** The drowned Low Country never joined the West Lands to the Middle Lands, because the Blue Sea always lay between them.

## 3. The six fallen realms

### 3.1 Muzerar, the Low Country (sunk beneath the Crown Sea)

**Names.**

| Name | Language and meaning | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Muzerar** | Orran *muzer* "low" + *-ar* "country", stressed on the last syllable as Orran words are | the Veiled and the Redbark, in their own tongue |
| **Ozelkak** | Orran *ozelk* "hall" + *-ak* "the many": the Many Halls, the city of the Moot | the cord-mothers |
| **Humaran** | Giants' Speech *hum* "low" + *maran* "sea": the low sea, the giants' name for the water over the land | the cairn-keepers of Hirolon |
| **the Nimlan banks** | Aenoran *nim* "low" + *lan* "field": the low fields, the sailors' chart-name for the shoals where nets bring up field-walls and quern-stones | Orohaf's fishers and the Concord's charts |
| **the Low Country** | Common Speech | scholars of Parim and Selen, once they learn of it |

The four names belong to four different keepers and four knowledge layers, and bringing them together is the mechanic of section 6.4.

**People and culture.** Orran-speaking men, the people whose tongue the language data already calls older than Ainic and the speech of the kingdoms Aen defeated.
Descent ran through mothers, as Orran descent still does, and every family belonged to a beast-clan (Wolf, Bear, Eagle, Horse, Raven and Water, the clan stems of the Orran data).
The realm was governed by the **Moot of Mothers** (working label) at Ozelkak, where the eldest cord-mother of every clan held a seat.
For a war the Moot chose a war-chief (Orran *ukhar*) and took the office back when the war ended, so the Low Country had no king and no crown in all its centuries.
Its law was kept on knotted cords, one knot for every field, debt, marriage and death, and the long peace between its clans was later called the **Peace of the Cords** (working label).
The hill giants lived on the low country's hills as partners and not as subjects: they raised the long sea-dikes from field-stone and kept their cairn-fields on the high ground.

**Age.** The Age of Kingdoms, in the last thousand years or so before the War of Men, with its height about 1,500 to 900 years before Aen's victory (P, section 8, question M1).

**Extent at its height.** The plain that now lies under the eastern two-thirds of the Crown Sea, from the Aenor Isle shore west to within a day's sail of Varnondor's island, north to the Sea of Monsters and the Goldoroth arm, and south to the Nesmael.
Aenor Isle and the Tinta Hills were its eastern heights.
On the mainland it held the river valleys of the western and central Middle Lands (the land the Vale of Aenor now covers, the Selenar, the Aenar down to the Great Lake) and the Konorar up to the red-oak forest by the Narrow Strait.
Hirolon's ring stayed the giants' own, an ally that the Moot never ruled.
Measured on the layout, the drowned plain alone is larger than the whole Middle Lands region, so Muzerar was greater than Aenor at Aenor's height in land as well as in fame.

**What made it great.** Three harvests a year on the low fields behind the giants' dikes.
A moot that governed more land than Aenor ever held, without a crown and without a war between its clans for centuries.
Ozelkak's moot-halls standing on piled mounds above the fields.
The **cord-reckoning**, a way of keeping account of every field and of everything a field had given, which is the first account-keeping of Aoaz.

**How it fell: the Long Settling and the Night of the Sea** (working labels).
The mothers drew on the magic of the place itself to give their fields the third harvest and their cord-mothers their long rule, generation after generation.
Over the centuries the ground lost its strength and settled below the level of the sea, so it had to be held by ever higher dikes, and every field the sea took was tied as a black knot on the Moot's own cord.
The giants' cairn-keepers warned the Moot.
The mothers divided between those who would stop drawing on the land and those who would draw harder to raise the dikes, and the second party won.
One winter a storm-tide broke the last dikes, and the sea took Ozelkak and the low fields in a single night.

This is the working model of magic in `docs/lore/references/magic_and_wizards.md` (M1, the magic of the place is one of the three purses, M2, place matters, and M5, overdrawing consumes the one who draws) at the greatest scale the world remembers, and it makes the Low Country the first great overdraw, long before Elzon's.
It is ZIN's own cause: no god judged the realm, it was not proud against heaven, and its ruin took centuries before it took a night.

| Layer | What it holds (proposed) |
| --- | --- |
| Authorial truth, if the owner adopts it | the overdraw of the place, as told above |
| Belief | the Veiled tell it as "the land that was asked for too much" and count the souring of their downs by the Black Roots as the second land to die under them |

| Mystery-kept | the giants know and will say only that the land was asked for too much |

**After the fall.** The surviving eastern provinces became the Orran kingdoms of the river valleys, each house claiming the Moot's inheritance and its own mothers' cords.
Their wars over a shrinking, crowded land ran on for centuries into the War of Men, which Aen ended by making one crown over the kingdoms, the very thing the Low Country never had (P: the drowning as a deep root of the War of Men, section 8, question M4).
The Orran houses that would not kneel took their cords into the grey downs and the red-oak forest (the existing proposal of the Withdrawal in 1A 1).
The giants withdrew into their ring and raised the **Eldest Cairn**, to which the keeper of that office has added one stone in every year since the sea came (P: the existing legend that the Eldest Cairn is older than Aen, read as this count).

**What survives.**

- **A tongue.** Orran lives among the Veiled and the Redbark and under the Aenoran names of the Vale's rivers and towns (existing proposal).
- **A law and a custom.** Mother-line descent, the cord-mothers of the Cursed Lands and the Bloody Forest, and the Old Knots faith. The Isle Houses of Aenor Isle are ruled by their eldest women, choose their queen in the tide-chamber at the turn of the tide and give their dead to the tide (existing proposals). This addendum reads those customs as the Low Country's last law, kept by a people who no longer speak its tongue and do not know why they keep it.
- **Places.** The tide-chambers of Orohaf, rock-cut moot-halls of the last eastern hill that flood at high water. The Nimlan banks. The **Mothers' Mound** (working label), the top of Ozelkak's greatest mound, which breaks the water as a reef west of Aenor Isle at the lowest spring tides. The Eldest Cairn in Hirolon. The eldest barrow of the Cursed Lands, where the Last Cord lies (the Cursed Lands tree already keeps barrows "older than the Concord" and a yearly count on knotted cords in *The Old Tallies*).
- **Relics.** The Last Cord, the Mothers' Stone and the dike-stones (6.5).

**Why this is not Númenor.**
Númenor was an island raised and given to men by the gods, ruled by long-lived kings, drowned by divine judgment for its pride against the gods, and its faithful exiles founded the greatest realms of the next age with seeing-stones and a tree in their keeping.
Muzerar was a low plain joined to the mainland and won from the sea by men and giants together, ruled by mothers who never had a crown, and destroyed by its own long overdraw of the land's magic with no god involved.
Its survivors did not found the next age's great realms: they were the houses Aen beat, and what they carry is a tongue, a cord of knots, a count of years and a custom of the tide.

### 3.2 Tarkia, the Bridge Kingdom (the joined shores)

**Names.**

| Name | Language and meaning | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tarqu** | the reconstructed proto-Eastern word for "bridge" (`families.eastern.proto.bridge` in `languages.json`) | scholars who compare the three Eastern tongues |
| **Tarkia** | Alerhic *tark-* "bridge" + the kingdom ending *-ia*: the Bridge Kingdom | the scribes of Alerhia, and the realm's best-known name |
| **Tarqis** | Noric *tarq* + *-is* "country" | the desert tellers of Noris |
| **Pontoseno** | Stabilic *ponto* "bridge" + *seno* "old": the old bridge, because the trade tongue lost the old word and names it with its own | the ledgers of Stabilo |
| **Qadurum** | proto-Eastern *qada* "holy" + *urum* "city", in Alerhic **Kadurum** | the city that stood on the bridge |
| **sara rabu** | proto-Eastern "great king", in Alerhic *saria rhabis* | the title of its kings |
| **the Bridge Kingdom** | Common Speech | everyone else |

**People and culture.** The one Eastern people before the roads, whose single tongue later parted into desert, trade and river speech (the existing proposal that Noric *biir*, Stabilic *bro* and Alerhic *biria* are one word).
It was a river-temple civilization of stair-temples above the flood-line, scribes writing the reed letters from which the Alerhic script descends, and kings anointed at the river.
Its great work was the **Bridge Road** (working label), which ran from the Melty Pass in the far east over the isthmus and into the west.

**Age.** Deep antiquity, with its height about 3,000 to 2,300 years before Aen (P, section 8, question T1).

**Extent at its height.** Before the rift opened, the east coast of the Magical Lands and the East Lands were one landmass, joined by a broad isthmus where the Rift Sea now runs, broadest in the south.
The Rift Narrows, about 200 pixels of water between the Mushroom Forest coast and the Delbiz coast, are the last throat of that isthmus.
Tarkia held the western East Lands (the river valleys that became Alerhia, the Delbiz grasslands and the shores of the Hot Lake), the isthmus with Qadurum astride it, and the Bridge Road west along the southern edge of the elven woods, through the Zilin Hills and over the South Pass into the south-east of the Middle Lands, the downs of Herez and Noleksun.
In the east its provinces ran to the Melty Mountains and through the Melty Pass into lands beyond the eastern edge of every chart, whose names nobody keeps.
The rift-coast elves were its neighbours and never its subjects: the road crossed their shore under a treaty of passage.

**What made it great.** The first great road of Aoaz, the first written law of the East, the stair-temples, the canals of its western march (Noleksun's fields still drink from its ditches, P), and the horses and cedar it carried west (section 4).
At its height it held more land than Aenor did at Aenor's.

**How it fell: the War of the Two Shores and the Breaking** (working labels).
A great king left two heirs, the House of the Eastern Stair and the House of the Western Stair (working labels), and the realm split along its road.
During their war the isthmus broke: the sea came in from the Gorlab Sea in the north and from the Reeto Sea in the south, and within a lifetime the two shores stood apart across a long rift.
Qadurum broke in two.
Its western half lies under the fungal belt of the Mushroom Forest beside Luv-zhise, and its eastern half under the grass of the Delbiz coast near Furtonlin, where the horse-fair is held among fallen pillars.
The western house, cut off from its river crowns, faded among the hills of the Middle Lands' south-east and left no realm behind it.
The eastern half broke into river crowns, and the one tongue began to part.

| Authorial truth | the owner's choice (section 8, question T2), with mystery-open recommended so that both tellings stay alive in play |

**What survives.** The Eastern family of tongues and its shared roots.
Alerhia's first stair, which the valley people raised above the flood-line "so that the river and the gods would each have a place" (the existing founding story, with the proposal that the flood was the Breaking's).
The River Temples faith, which holds the river to be the oldest god anyone can see.
The **Two Gates** (working label), the halves of Qadurum on the two shores of the Narrows.
The old roads that end at Lenkei, where Eldafaen keeps its Archive "at the end of the old roads" (existing name), and the treaty of passage that the Archive still holds.
Noleksun's ditches, the Lake Litany of Luv-zhise, and the horse-lines of the riders if the owner adopts that link (section 4).

### 3.3 Rhabtarkia, the Kalioth Crown (the eastern empire of the First Age)

**Names.**

| Name | Language and meaning | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rhabtarkia** | Alerhic *rhab-* "great" + *tarkia*: the Greater Bridge Kingdom, the name of what its kings claimed to be | the empire's own court |
| **the Kalioth Crown** | after the Kalioth, the dry upland of Erco where its kings sat (Alerhic *kalia* "dark" + *-oth* "high place", the existing name of the upland) | the chroniclers of every other realm |
| **Barzoth** | Alerhic *barzis* "iron" + *-oth*: the iron height, its seat on the Kalioth | the mine-folk of Erco, who still call the ruin by it |
| **the Black Tribute** | the existing Alerhic chronicle name for the ships, grain and men the eastern clients gave Volgan | Alerhia's scribes and Noris's tellers |

**People and culture.** Alerhic-speaking mountain kings (Erco's existing history already names "the old mountain kings of the Kalioth upland"), lords of mines and iron who ruled mine-folk, Noric desert tribes and the pass-houses of the Hardtmuth.
It was a fortress empire of heavy horse, iron, slave-worked mines and walled upland cities, and its kings called themselves the restorers of the Bridge Kingdom and swore to rejoin the shores.

**Age.** The First Age, from about 1A 500 to its end in 2A 12, with its height about 1A 900 to 1500 while Aenor declined under its bad kings.

**Extent at its height.** The southern East from the Kalioth and the Plutondo valley over the slopes of the Smoking Teeth, the Hardtmuth passes and the northern wells of the South Lands, and through the Melty Pass along the Burning Rim's edge road to eastern provinces beyond the edge of the charts, which sent tribute over the glacier: this is the empire that reached the very east.
It never held Alerhia's river crown, which defied it, or the towns of the Lake Road.
From about 1A 1500 to the end of the age, as Volgan's ally, it held the south-east of the Middle Lands, the downs of Herez and Noleksun and the South Pass, so that its garrisons sat in the old western march of the Bridge Kingdom, and for a century its claim to be Tarkia restored looked like the truth.
This fits the vault: Volgan formed alliances with rulers of the eastern and southern lands and eastern client rulers gave him ships and landing sites (E and P in *The First Age of Aoaz*), and Herez is the march that first feels Volgan's march north (existing proposal).

**What made it great.** It was the greatest eastern power between the Bridge Kingdom and the caravan roads, it kept a road over a glacier pass that nobody has kept since, it was famous for its iron and its siege-craft, and it is the only eastern realm that ever ruled land in the Middle Lands after the Bridge broke.

**How it fell: the Black Tribute and Dardan.** To rejoin the shores its kings took Volgan's side and paid the Black Tribute, which Alerhia refused in 1A 1214 (Alerhia's existing event already says that the mountain kings to the south gave Volgan what Alerhia would not).
Volgan's victories gave them the south-eastern Middle Lands.
At Dardan their host stood with Volgan's and died there.
After Dardan their western garrisons starved, and in 2A 12 the mine-lords of the southern mountains shut the passes against the beaten host and besieged the last king in his hall (the existing founding of Erco).
The provinces beyond the Melty Pass fell silent and were never heard from again, and what became of them is a mystery that stays kept.

**What survives.** The Kalioth halls under Erco's seven forts, which stand on the old fortress line (the existing Seven Forts of the Kalioth, 4A 1120).
The last king's iron diadem, walled up in the hall of Barzoth by the mine-lords who would neither wear it nor melt it (6.5).
The glacier road over the Melty Pass.
The Black Tribute in the Alerhic chronicles.
Miaed's pass-houses, which sold water and guides to Volgan's eastern clients (existing South Lands proposal).
In Herez and Noleksun, a word for eastern raiders and the stone forts the occupiers built on the downs.

### 3.4 Gromatrak, the Stone Road (the dwarves' realm under the seas)

**Names.**

| Name | Language and meaning | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gromatrak** | Dwarvish *grom* "stone" + *trak* "road", with the old *a* coming back where three consonants would meet, as in Brundahrim | every hold |
| **Tromahrim** | Dwarvish *trom* "great" + *hrim* "hall": the Great Hall, seat of the Deep King | every hold |
| **the Deep King** | Dwarvish *drav* "king" | every hold |
| **the Stone Roads** | Common Speech, kept in the epithet of Brunek of the Stone Roads (vault proposal) | men who trade at the gates |

**People.** The dwarves of every great range as one realm (owner decision: dwarves hold the great ranges of the West and North Lands and the Great Wall range).
The one tongue that every hold still speaks, so that the name of a western hold and the name of a northern one are built from the same words (language data), is the realm's legacy.
The realm is a union of holds and says nothing about where the dwarves first came from, which stays open (V11).

**Age.** Its first roads were cut in the Age of Kingdoms beneath the low country while it was still dry land.
It outlived the drowning above it, because the roads ran in deep rock far below the plain, and it was at its height in the First Age until its fall at the age's end.

**Extent at its height.** The western ranges (the holds now Brundahrim and Gromkerm), the Great Wall range (Kunhrim) and the High Mountains of the North Lands (Hrakbrund), joined by roads under the floor of the Crown Sea and under the Narrow Strait.
Tromahrim lay deep beneath the low country's northern hills, which are now the floor of the Sea of Monsters.

**What made it great.** It is the only realm that ever spanned three landmasses, and a dwarf could walk from the West Lands to the North Lands without seeing the sky.
It sold Aenor its iron through the Western Gate (the existing proposal of 1A 300) and sent its engineers to Dardan.

**How it fell: the Letting-In of the Sea** (working label).
In Volgan's war the Damned Spirits rose from deep beneath the mountains and the underground world (E), and they came into the Stone Roads, from wherever the vault leaves them.
The Deep King ordered the roads under the sea broken and the sea let in, and Tromahrim drowned with the spirits inside it, while Kunhrim's delvers collapsed the tunnels at Dardan (existing proposal).
The four holds were cut off from one another and have had no Deep King since.
Each keeps a sealed **Sea-Door** (working label) at the head of its old road.

| Mystery-kept | what remains below the sea |

**What survives.** One tongue in four holds, the four Sea-Doors, the Halls Below the Halls (an existing focus of the holds' shared tree), the Deep King's lamp (6.5), and the grudge between the western holds and Kunhrim.

## 6. How it enters play

### 6.1 Principles

- **Each fallen realm has its own loop.** Muzerar is pieced together from three keepers who distrust each other, Tarkia is a crossing with two halves, the Kalioth Crown is one dangerous object, Gromatrak is four sealed doors, Margorodai is a dead harbour, and the Sunfields come back a find at a time out of the ice. They share a presentation pattern and a namespace, never a generic progress bar.
- **Most things remain unheard (K61).** A discovery is told to the realm that makes it, to the other heirs of the same fallen realm only where a keeping or a moot links them, and to bordering realms by a minor report. None of these moments is world news (section 6.8 lists every audience).
- **No new tags.** Restorations are cosmetic identities on existing heirs (`set_cosmetic_tag`), which keeps the tag total under the S12 budget.
- **No whole-world iteration.** The loops run on decisions, focus completions and targeted events, and they ride on two pulses that already exist: the Cold Lands' yearly winter roll (`zin_cold.10`, global `zin_cold_winter`) and the eastern mechanic's own events. Nothing adds an `on_monthly` or `on_weekly` over all countries.
- **Dynamic values in one place.** Every threshold, cost and weight below is a script constant in one new file (working name `common/script_constants/zin_deep_past.txt`, category `deep_past`), and costs scale with the realm's size, war state and what it already knows.
- **Knowledge layers.** Every text is written for the layer that holds when it fires, with the names of section 3 revealed only behind their gates (a realm learns the name Muzerar from the Veiled, not from the narrator).

Each fallen realm gets one decision category for its heirs (working keys `zin_deep_low_country`, `zin_deep_two_shores`, `zin_deep_kalioth`, `zin_deep_sea_doors`, `zin_deep_sea_roads`, `zin_deep_ice`).
A category becomes visible only after the realm's first discovery, and until its gate it is named with the heir's own word for the thing (Orohaf sees the tide-chambers, not the Low Country).
These are plain decision categories with ordinary decisions, so a static category picture is allowed under the decision skill's eligibility rule, and no scripted GUI is planned.
Every decision states its non-political costs in its tooltip, and every blocked requirement has its own localised tooltip.

#### Muzerar: the Three Keepings

The truth of the Low Country is split between three keepers, and each holds a part the others lack.

| Keeping | Keeper at start | What it holds | Flag (working) |

| **the Tide** | Orohaf | which tides open the tide-chambers and when the Mothers' Mound breaks the water | `zin_deep_keeps_tide` |

**Who seeks.** Any keeper, the Bloody Forest, and Aenor, whose universities of Parim and Selen (founded in 4A 340, existing proposal) take up the question once a scholar of the Lost Cause has come back from the edge of the Cursed Lands (the existing focus *A Scholar at the Edge* in the Cursed Lands tree is the source event).
A realm seeks a keeping with a targeted decision, and the keeper answers by event.

| Decision (working label) | Asker pays | Keeper's price and AI | On yes | On no |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask the Cord-Mothers | infantry equipment, scaled to the asker's size, and salt and iron in trade (a 365-day resource export to the Cursed Lands) | the asker holds no claim on a Cursed Lands state and has not warred on it for a constant number of years, AI yes by opinion, never to a realm whose tax-men it turned back that year | shared flag `zin_deep_shared_cord`, opinion `zin_opinion_keeping_given` both ways | 180-day cooldown, the asker's opinion of the Veiled falls |

| Ask the Isle Houses | navy experience for the divers' training and a guarantee of Orohaf's independence, or for Aenor the Tide-Seats confirmed at a political power cost | the asker is not at war with Orohaf and does not hold it as a puppet, AI yes by opinion and by the guarantee | shared flag `zin_deep_shared_tide` | 180-day cooldown |

**Taking instead of asking.** A realm that controls the keeper's site state in war destroys the keeping rather than gaining it.
If the eldest barrow is taken, the cord-mothers burn the Last Cord (`zin_deep_cord_burned`), the Moot of Mothers can never form, and every Orran realm takes `zin_opinion_keeping_taken` toward the taker.
If the Eldest Cairn is taken, the keepers scatter its upper stones and the count is lost (`zin_deep_count_scattered`).
If Orohaf is made a puppet, the rite passes but the queens' houses refuse their divers, and the dive costs twice as much.
With two keepings destroyed the Low Country can never be pieced together in that game.

**The Two Counts.** A realm that holds or shares the Cord and the Stone receives the event that brings the cords and the stones together.
The two counts agree, the realm learns the year of the Night of the Sea and where the dikes stood (`zin_deep_low_country_charted`), the Nimlan coasts gain `zin_deep_low_fields_charted`, the art Cord-Reckoning becomes researchable for it (6.10), and the Western Crossing hook of 6.3 becomes live for its side.

**The Dive at the Mothers' Mound.** A realm that holds or shares all three keepings and owns the Mound shore state, or has Orohaf's consent by event, can send a dive.
The window opens only in Lumae and Ailtal, the months of the great tides (months 3 and 9 of the twelve).
It needs a naval base in the shore state at a constant level, and it costs navy experience, convoys and manpower for the divers.
A storm roll, read from constants and lowered by the Tide keeping, decides the outcome: the divers bring up the Mothers' Stone (6.5) and the dike-craft (the art Dike-Craft, 6.10), or they find the hall-door and must dive again next window, or they are lost (manpower and navy experience gone, a year's cooldown, and a mourning event at Borrak).

**Aenor's road and its price.** Aenor can piece the Low Country together for its scholars and its fleets, and its reward is the charting of the banks before the Western Crossing sails.
Every keeping it takes by force, and every tax-man it sends to the Cursed Lands while it asks, makes the Orran heirs hold that Aen's crown has robbed them twice.

**A gated hook (optional).** If the Cursed Lands have let in the almoners of the Deliverer (the existing focus *Healers from Outside*), the almoners ask to copy the cords "for safekeeping".
A yes gives the Cursed Lands bread and medicine at once, and only behind `zin_afrit_revealed` does the realm learn that the copy went to the Hidden Council's archive, where names are kept for other uses (A3 holds until then).

Sealing is a mission with a deadline: essence and manpower, the door shut again, and the road lost.
If the deadline passes, the chain becomes a regional crisis for the holds and every realm whose states the modifier has reached, and Kunhrim's neighbours (Kingson, Burrikkrit and the Klungori Guard) are the first to feel it.
This is the only part of the deep past that can hurt realms that never touched it, and it never reaches the whole world's news (K61).

## 7. What not to add

The deep past should enrich the present without crowding it.

- **No fallen realm returns.** None of the six comes back as a new tag or a full country, and the drowned land does not rise again.
- **No world news.** Discoveries stay regional, and nothing here is a world-audience super event.
- **No divine judgment.** No god drowns, splits or freezes any of the six, whatever the believers in the world say.
- **No answers to the open questions** of section 2, and in particular nothing about Aen's birth, the Ainu, Volgan's god, Zol's Mistake, Dardan's place, the dwarves' origin or Eindil's sleep.
- **No restoration for every heir.** One ambition per fallen realm (two routes for the Sea-Roads), and the other heirs gain knowledge, relics or consent roles rather than crowns.
- **No free cores and no claims on elven land, ever.** The Magical Lands stay unconquered in their own history and in the play this adds.
- **No scripted GUI.** Plain decision categories carry every loop, which keeps the interface work for the systems that need it.
- **No second sunken realm.** The Low Country is the one realm that sank. The Square is a broken harbour, the Stone Road a flooded road and the Bridge a broken neck of land.
- **No exploration of the far east.** The Kalioth Crown's provinces beyond the Melty Pass stay silent, and the lands south of the South Lands stay off the map (K25).
- **No new language family.** The names use the existing dictionaries, and the one etymological reading added (that the three Boreal names are one) is a discovery, not a data change.
