# 02. Total overhaul specification

Requirements carry ids (R-xx). Each names its acceptance check in [04-EXECUTION-AND-ACCEPTANCE.md](04-EXECUTION-AND-ACCEPTANCE.md) (A-xx). They set **minimum** coverage: the implementation's own inspection must find further affected areas and add them, recording each in `PROGRAM.md`.

## 0. The standard

- **R-00 One complete delivery.** ZIN is delivered as a complete total overhaul in one assignment. Internal stages, workers, tests and questions are allowed; an MVP, a demo, a first import, a new main menu or a partly converted game is not completion. No requirement is quietly dropped, left generic, replaced by a temporary substitute or moved to unspecified later work. If something cannot be done, it is reported as a blocker with evidence (04, section 6), never as done.
- **R-0a Nothing vanilla or real-world shows.** Every surface the player sees belongs to ZIN: names, text, pictures, icons, sounds, rules. Real-world references (countries, ideologies, people, religions, brands, dates such as 1936, words such as factory, rifle, tank, oil), placeholders and unfinished localisation are removed. The existing inventories (`docs/specs/inventory/*.csv`, 734 rows; the interface inventory `tools/content/data/ui/vanilla_inventory.json`, 31,561 entries) are the starting list.
- **R-0b One coherent world.** Geography, peoples, history, characters, creatures, gameplay, art and interface support one another. Every capital, faction, creature, character, mechanic and asset has enough context to explain its role and look (R-47).
- **R-0c Engineering rules hold.** All game files come from the deterministic builder and the politics step through the shared atomic rewrite engine; the repositories' `AGENTS.md` rules apply in full (02 does not relax them).

## 1. Canonical corrections and migrations

- **R-01 Exchange Erdae and Aoaz** (01, 2.1). Rename the main setting to Aoaz and the neighbouring small continent to Erdae everywhere: lore and the vault, localisation, map data and labels, events, interface text, working assets and their file names, data files (`erdae.json` and the like), code identifiers that carry the name, documentation and specs. Method: (1) inventory every occurrence of Erdae, Arda and Aoaz with its meaning (main setting or neighbour); (2) replace main-setting occurrences with a unique temporary token, then neighbour occurrences with "Erdae", then the token with "Aoaz", in one scripted, idempotent migration with a dry run and a report; (3) add a build check that fails on "Erdae" in a main-setting context and on "Arda"; (4) keep traceability: a table `docs/lore/names_traceability.md` lists every renamed file, folder, identifier and term with its old name and where the old name still legitimately appears (private originals keep their file names; source quotes keep their wording with a note). Acceptance A-01.
- **R-02 The Great Wall of Aenor is a natural range** (01, 2.2). Rewrite the vault and specs that make it artificial, void master plan P4 and P13, and model it as an enormous mountain range in the geography: passes, valleys, snowlines and the routes they allow, which shape terrain, settlement, trade, cultural contact and military movement (supply, attrition, impassable sections, named passes with history). Acceptance A-02.
- **R-03 Zol's servant replaces Zol in the War of the Last Crowns** (01, 2.4). After the owner names the servant (Q-03), propagate the correction through the vault, the shared contract (`zin_zol_manifested` and dependents), mechanics, events, endings, focus trees, characters, art and their consequences; keep everything else in the war intact. Acceptance A-03.
- **R-04 Contradiction register.** The name changes of 01, section 2.7 (Vez'nan is Volgan; Edvoid renamed, including the "Ocean of Edvoid" label) are migrated like R-01, with traceability. Every correction that contradicts a source gets a row in the vault's `09 - Development/Names and Retcons.md` (or the vault's equivalent) with the old statement, the new canon and the date.

## 2. World and map

- **R-10 Import the actual supplied map through the MCP workflow.** The owner's map (the file chosen under Q-04) passes through the HOI4 Agent Tools pipeline and produces a map that loads and behaves correctly in the game. Requirements: correct dimensions and orientation (5,120 x 2,560 game layout unless the owner decides otherwise), consistent coordinate handling between drawing, model and game, land and sea, terrain, rivers, lakes, province boundaries, unique ids and colours, connectivity (no orphan provinces, valid adjacencies, straits as named crossings), states, strategic regions, countries, capitals, victory points, buildings, supply and ports, positions, names; all layers aligned (`provinces.bmp`, `heightmap.bmp`, `terrain.bmp`, `rivers.bmp`, `trees.bmp`, normal and colour maps, `definition.csv`, `adjacencies.csv`, state and region files, localisation). Recurring defects are fixed in the importer and server, not patched in output (03). Authored adjustments live in a source file that survives regeneration; repeated imports never duplicate content, corrupt ids, erase established work or move geography unexpectedly; failures give accurate diagnostics and incomplete work is never reported as success. The affected existing workflows (reference-image and procedural `create_world`, the legacy partition, `map_inspect`, `map_rewrite` edits, renders) keep passing their own checks alongside the ZIN import. Acceptance A-10.
- **R-11 Terrain, relief, rivers and coasts read as real fantasy geography.** Mountains as continuous ranges with crests and passes (the Great Wall, the ring around Kingson, the East Lands' huge ranges and volcanoes), fertile plains where the lore says (Aenor is fertile, never desert), rivers from high ground to sea or lake with deltas and watersheds, real lakes that render as water, coasts and straits that make sense, climate bands, rain shadows, forests, marshes, deserts and ice placed for reasons. The geography model's known defects (01, 4.2) are fixed. Acceptance A-11.
- **R-12 Provinces, states and borders follow the land.** Borders follow rivers, ranges, forests and coasts (border-quality metric at vanilla parity or better: enrichment at least 1.3 for provinces, states and countries; natural share at least 0.70 for states and 0.73 for countries); province sizes vary with terrain and density; no "District N", "Region N", "STATE_123" or placeholder names anywhere. Acceptance A-12.
- **R-13 Seas and water are named coherently.** Each sea, ocean, strait, gulf and lake has one name that fits its place, climate and peoples; no two names contradict (no Sea of Death that is also a Sea of Light) or differ only by a plural; changed owner labels are kept as `formerLabel` and reported. Acceptance A-13.
- **R-14 The Nether exists below the Dead Lands**, as the vault describes it (`05 - Geography/Nether.md`, `Abyss.md`, `Volgans Dead Lands.md`), with the King of Demons' realm, its peoples and its access routes. Acceptance A-14.
- **R-15 Clear regions.** Each region is a coherent area with natural borders and a name; strategic regions, map modes and the region names in text agree. Acceptance A-12.
- **R-16 Settlements and cities.** Cities, towns and lesser places are placed where water, food, trade, defence and history put them; capitals are chosen by each realm's own story (decision L2: a place the lore names is not automatically central; Castle Yeldenne is an ordinary far-northern place). Every victory point and state has a meaningful name from its people's language. Acceptance A-16.
- **R-17 Resources and buildings follow geography** (iron in ranges, timber in forests, grain on fertile plains, essence where magic gathers), and so do infrastructure, ports and supply. Acceptance A-16.
- **R-18 The world edge.** The engine always wraps the map horizontally; the edge is open sea with frame borders so it never reads as a globe; no land touches the seam. Acceptance A-11.

- **R-19 Regional dossiers.** Every region (and the Nether, Hirolon and each distinct sub-region) has a dossier in `docs/lore/regions/<region>.md` deep enough to establish: physical character (terrain, climate, water, hazards), inhabitants (peoples, cultures, population and where they live), livelihoods and resources, institutions and political forms, beliefs and faiths, history (with the regional history data of R-45), neighbours and relations, wildlife and creatures, and the region's role in gameplay (mechanics, trees, events, AI). Map, roster, content and art for a region are built from its dossier and checked against it. Acceptance A-19.

## 3. Peoples, politics and the roster

- **R-20 Race, culture and state are distinct.** A people (race) is not a culture and not a state; mixed societies and internal cultural differences exist where the lore supports them. Data: peoples, cultures, faiths and languages (`tools/content/data/world/`) and their in-game expression (culture ideas, faith spirits, state cultures). Acceptance A-20.
- **R-21 The full range of inhabitants.** Humans and elves alongside dwarves, hobbits (name subject to Q-06), ents, goblins (including hobgoblins and dwarf goblins of the cards), giants, orcs, trolls, ogres, gnomes, yeti, the dead, demons and devils and other established or newly developed peoples. Their presence changes the map, societies, politics and gameplay: their own realms, enclaves, clans and wild lands, units, laws, faiths, flags and names. Acceptance A-20.
- **R-22 Hirolon, the kingdom of giants** (spelling and status under Q-05), restored with its geography and the mountains that enclose it; settlements, population, history, relations and the effects of the enclosure (defence, trade, isolation). Acceptance A-22.
- **R-23 Spirits, demons and devils.** Devils are corrupted Lesser Spirits who served an Old God and escaped to Aoaz, not necessarily Zol's (01, 2.6); demons, angels, Great Spirits and the damned keep the vault's distinctions. Their places (the Nether, the Death Islands, the Goldoroth Mountains and others the sources name) and their effects on play follow from that. Acceptance A-40.
- **R-24 Fewer, smaller elven realms.** The eastern elven kingdoms are too many and too large (on 19 October the roster had 24 elf and 41 sun-elf countries). Elven realms become smaller, scattered and less populous, each placed by its history and surroundings, with variation between elven communities; the east is not uniformly elven and not a field of interchangeable states. Acceptance A-24.
- **R-25 The Middle Lands are varied.** Not an unbroken run of human kingdoms: a goblin kingdom in the mountains, other peoples' holds and enclaves, contested and wild lands. Acceptance A-24.
- **R-26 Political forms fit the inhabitants:** kingdoms, city-states, clans, confederations, enclaves, forest domains, contested territories, isolated communities, wild lands; expressed through government, laws, succession and AI. Acceptance A-20.
- **R-27 The roster and tags.** The roster is redesigned to satisfy R-21 to R-26; every country has a real three-letter tag from its name (NOR, AEN ...), never a debug-like tag, none equal to a vanilla tag; ZIN tags plus dynamic tags stay below 439 (the flag-sheet crash); a migration rewrites every tag reference; dynamic tags are sensible and documented. Acceptance A-27.
- **R-28 Country and faction names.** No country is named after its leader (the Zol country is "Zol's Mistake"; Volgan's country gets its own name); no bureaucratic names for factions, institutions, laws or mechanics; adjective and short forms consistent. Acceptance A-28.
- **R-29 Population follows the land:** food, water, livelihoods, safety, history and the people's nature; the demography model (about 86.6 million people) is revised for the new roster and map and applied to states and manpower. Acceptance A-29.

## 4. Gods, religion, magic and the wider world

- **R-30 The twelve New Gods** (01, 2.3; purpose under Q-01). Twelve distinct gods with identities, domains, symbols, relationships, histories, temples, clergy, festivals and reasons people worship them; together they cover the practical, personal, social and spiritual needs that make people turn to religion; overlaps are deliberate, identities stay clear. Glernaen, god of power and wisdom, is the most powerful. Some New Gods serve Zol (vault). No twelve interchangeable fantasy labels. Acceptance A-30.
- **R-31 The calendar honours the twelve.** Month names, abbreviations and the start date (currently "1 Thaw 4A 1748") are rebuilt on the gods in every localisation language the calendar module writes, reconciled with the chronology (Q-02). Acceptance A-30.
- **R-32 Faith in play.** Faiths, cults, sects, festivals and holy sites affect stability, culture, events and decisions; false gods and Afrit's public cult keep the vault's distinction between belief and truth. Acceptance A-30.
- **R-33 Knowledge layers.** Authorial truth, local belief, disputed information and deliberate mystery are separate in data and text (01, section 3); Erdu, Dunda and Zol's creator never become common knowledge; Lao is belief; travellers from Erdae and Haven are rare and partial. A text check prevents leaks. Acceptance A-33.
- **R-34 Wizards and magic.** Varner is one practitioner in a world of magical traditions, institutions, rivalries and reputations. **Bastian** is a well-known wizard of the realm of men (details under Q-09). Further notable wizards have distinct backgrounds, disciplines, limits, relationships and influence grounded in history. Acceptance A-34.
- **R-35 Magic has causes, limits and consequences** (the Fading, Zol's slowly growing power, Aoaz's resistance to channelling); where magic changes geography, settlement or war, the cause and limits are stated. Acceptance A-34.

## 5. Creatures and ecology

- **R-40 Bestiary.** Every card (01, 4.3) is transcribed, translated and checked, applying the name changes of 01, section 2.7 (Vez'nan is Volgan; Edvoid gets a new name); consistent concepts are kept, contradictions (especially years and metaphysics) are resolved against the vault or asked about. Then the bestiary grows: wildlife, useful animals, dangerous species, rare beings and creatures of particular magical environments, without the same creatures renamed region by region. Acceptance A-40.
- **R-41 Ecology matters.** Habitats, behaviour and rarity affect travel, farming, settlement, trade, war and local tradition (monster events, mounts, beasts of burden, hunting economies, unit types). Acceptance A-40.

## 6. History, alternate history and continuing lore

- **R-45 Rich history beyond the vault** (decision L1): ages before the Fourth Age, founding stories, dynasties, wars, treaties, migrations, plagues, schisms, trade routes, cities with local legends, notable figures, for every region and country; labelled proposed; checked by `tools/content/lib/history_schema.mjs` (started on 19 October; no regional files exist yet). Acceptance A-45.
- **R-46 Alternate history and replayability** (decision L3). The vault's storyline is the main historical direction; at every beat the player and the AI can diverge, and every system stays coherent: a beat-by-beat divergence table, a soft-lock audit with fixes, successor and fallback events, a divergence measure, game-start variety that can be switched off for a historical game, dynamic succession, crises and diplomacy for all countries. Acceptance A-46.
- **R-47 Continuing lore development is part of implementation.** After each substantial task: (1) list the facts and relationships introduced; (2) check them against geography, chronology, characters and rules; (3) update the canonical references; (4) propagate consequences into related content; (5) record any decision that needs the owner. Undeveloped areas are expanded when canon gives direction; consequential contradictions and missing foundations are asked; intentional mysteries stay. Confirmed reusable findings improve the project instructions and skills. Acceptance A-47.
- **R-48 Synchronised references:** a timeline, an atlas, the pantheon, the bestiary, character references, cultural notes and naming guides, kept current with the implementation (in the vault, labelled per the Canon Policy, or in `docs/lore/` with the vault linking them). Acceptance A-47.

## 7. Cultures, languages and names

- **R-50 Naming conventions** for each major culture and language (the 24 languages and sound laws in `tools/content/data/world/languages.json`, `tools/content/lib/sound_laws.mjs`, the generator `modules/names.mjs`): cities, people, rivers, mountains, institutions and other names follow them; unrelated peoples do not share syllables and endings. Acceptance A-50.
- **R-51 Shared names and roots are explained** by ancestry, migration, conquest, trade or contact. Acceptance A-50.
- **R-52 Clean names and text:** no bureaucratic names, no names with `/` or other separators, no debug or placeholder final text, no raw keys, no modern words (banned list `tools/content/data/banned_words.json`, glossary `glossary.json`), no quotes that compare gods and men as trivia; quotes carry worldbuilding. Acceptance A-52.

- **R-60 Ruleset.** The stage 2 slices (technologies and doctrines, units and equipment, ships and the winged, economy, energy as Provisions and Stored Essence, buildings, laws, diplomacy, factions, peace, intelligence, institutions, starting forces, AI templates) are verified, completed where their notes list gaps (treasury decisions and economic events, ship and dragon entities, designer windows, counters, equipment pictures) and revised for the new roster, peoples, creatures and calendar. Acceptance A-60.
- **R-61 Focus trees for every country** (95 trees written on 19 October under `docs/design/focus/`) are reviewed, revised for the new roster and corrections, and compiled with zero problems; every country has a tree of its tier. Acceptance A-61.
- **R-62 Mechanics** of `docs/specs/04_mechanics/` (14 specs) are finished, including the interrupted Aenor, Afrit, Zol, Cold, Dark, East and Sun slices, with their scripted GUIs, and revised for the corrections. Acceptance A-62.
- **R-63 Story and events.** The Golden King and succession, Eindil's journey, the War of the Last Crowns, flavour and world events, the three endings and other outcomes, the Chronicle codex; all wired, paced by the story rule, and divergence-safe (R-46). Acceptance A-63.
- **R-64 Decisions and missions** for mechanics, regions and peoples (including those the trees and notes require). Acceptance A-63.
- **R-65 Characters:** rulers, heirs, advisors, commanders, wizards and named figures with portraits (image-generated) and biographies from the histories. Acceptance A-65.
- **R-66 AI:** every country behaves sensibly (peaceful start, the main historical direction by default, divergence handled, focus and research weights, war planning by reasons). Acceptance A-66.
- **R-67 Game rules and difficulty** fit ZIN (story rule, start variety, dark powers). Acceptance A-60.
- **R-68 Inherited systems review.** Each inherited system (espionage, MIO, special projects, raids, the market, operations, decisions categories, scripted GUIs) is either reshaped for ZIN, hidden with a reason, or kept with ZIN presentation; none is left vanilla by accident. Acceptance A-60.

## 9. Interface

- **R-80 Complete interface coverage.** Every reachable surface receives an intentional ZIN treatment, with an inventory that covers at least: main menu, loading screens, campaign setup and country selection; in-game interface, date display, top bar, alerts and notifications; country, political, government and diplomacy screens; research, technology, economy, production and construction; units, equipment, military organisation, combat and logistics; focus trees, decisions, events, missions and character presentation; state and province panels, map modes, borders, markers and labels; tooltips, icons, buttons, tabs, scrollbars, dialogs and progress indicators; pause, settings, save, load and other reachable menus. The reskin infrastructure (`docs/design/stage2/ui_infrastructure.md`) and its nine checks are the base; the 204 pending textures and every unreachable-marked surface the scripts actually reach are resolved. Acceptance A-80.
- **R-81 Visual system:** typography, colour, materials, symbols, spacing and hierarchy defined once and applied everywhere; legible over all backgrounds. Acceptance A-80.
- **R-82 States and stress cases:** hover, pressed, selected, disabled and empty states; long names and differing content amounts; supported resolutions and interface scaling (at least 1920 x 1080 and 2560 x 1440 at 100%, and the smallest and largest UI scale the game offers). Acceptance A-82.
- **R-83 Text tells the truth:** tooltips and descriptions describe implemented behaviour exactly. Acceptance A-82.

## 11. Technical quality

- **R-98 Zero errors:** the game's `error.log` has no ZIN-caused lines in a Linux run (the error budget reaches zero), the builder's checks and HOI4 Agent Tools validators pass, every country starts and runs, an observer game runs to 4A 1760 and to 4A 1800, saving and loading work. Acceptance A-98.
- **R-99 Performance:** monthly and daily scripts stay within budget (no every-country daily loops); a year of observer play runs at a speed comparable to vanilla on the Linux harness (measured). Acceptance A-98.
